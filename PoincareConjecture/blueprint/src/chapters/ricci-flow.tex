\chapter{Ricci flow and curvature estimates}
\label{ch:ricci-flow-v4}

Ricci flow changes a metric according to its Ricci curvature.  Its local
existence theory allows us to start the evolution; curvature estimates
determine how long it continues and what can appear after rescaling near a
singularity.  The distinction between local bounds, bounds on a whole time
slab, and bounds on individual slices is essential throughout this chapter.

All manifolds below have no boundary and are smooth, Hausdorff, and second
countable.  A closed manifold is compact.  We use its Borel measurable
structure and its Riemannian volume.  An interval may include an endpoint;
smoothness and time derivatives there are understood from within the interval.
A Ricci flow on an interval $J$ is a smooth family of positive definite
metrics $g(t)$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-equation}
                         \partial_t g=-2\operatorname{Ric}.
\end{equation}
Its connection is always the Levi--Civita connection of the same metric.
Completeness means metric completeness on every component, for every
specified time.  On a disconnected manifold the length distance between
different components is infinite.

Our curvature convention is
$\operatorname{Rm}(u,v,w,z)=\langle\mathcal R(u,v)z,w\rangle$,
with $\operatorname{Rm}(u,v,u,v)>0$ on an orthonormal pair on a positively
curved sphere.  We write $R$ for scalar curvature and use the Hilbert tensor
norm for $\operatorname{Rm}$ and its covariant derivatives.  The rough
Laplacian has scalar principal part $g^{ij}\partial_i\partial_j$.
The curvature operator is the self-adjoint operator on $\Lambda^2 TM$
whose quadratic form on $u\wedge v$ is
$\operatorname{Rm}(u,v,u,v)$.  Nonnegative curvature operator means
nonnegativity on every two-form, a stronger condition than nonnegative
sectional curvature in general dimensions.

\section{Scaling and normalized initial data}
\label{sec:rf-normalization}

For a constant $c>0$, the metrics $g$ and $cg$ have the same connection.
Their distance, volume and curvature norms satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-scaling}
 d_{cg}=\sqrt c\,d_g,\qquad d\mu_{cg}=c^{n/2}d\mu_g,\qquad
 |\operatorname{Rm}|_{cg}=c^{-1}|\operatorname{Rm}|_g.
\end{equation}
Indeed the connection formula contains one inverse metric and one
derivative of the metric, so its factors of $c$ cancel.  The covariant
curvature tensor acquires one factor of $c$ and its squared norm four
inverse factors, giving the last identity.  Consequently the parabolic
rescaling
\[
                    \widetilde g(s)=Q\,g(t_*+s/Q),\qquad Q>0,
\]
is again a Ricci flow on precisely those $s$ for which $t_*+s/Q\in J$.

\begin{definition}[Normalized initial data]\label{def:rf-normalized}
A normalized metric on a three-manifold is complete, has finite total
volume, and satisfies, for every $x$ and every $0<r\leq1$,
\[
 |\operatorname{Rm}_{g_0}(x)|\leq1,\qquad
 \mu_{g_0}(B_{g_0}(x,r))\geq\tfrac12\omega_3r^3.
\]
Here $\omega_3$ is the Lebesgue volume of the Euclidean unit ball.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:rf-normalize}
Every closed smooth three-manifold admits a normalized metric.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose a smooth metric $g$.  Compactness gives $B<\infty$ with
$|\operatorname{Rm}_g|\leq B$.  In coordinates, the metric and volume
density converge to their Euclidean values at the center.  A finite
coordinate cover makes the resulting radius uniform: for some $r_0>0$,
$\mu_g(B_g(x,r))\geq\omega_3r^3/2$ for every $x$ and $0<r\leq r_0$.
Choose $c>\max\{B,r_0^{-2}\}$.  Formula~\eqref{eq:rf-scaling} gives the
curvature bound, and for $0<r\leq1$ gives
\[
 \mu_{cg}(B_{cg}(x,r))
 =c^{3/2}\mu_g(B_g(x,r/\sqrt c))\geq\tfrac12\omega_3r^3.
\]
Compactness supplies finite volume and completeness.  When the initial
bound is instead on unit-input curvature evaluations, the elementary
three-dimensional estimate $|\operatorname{Rm}|\leq9B$ permits the
explicit choice $c=\max\{9B,r_0^{-2}\}+1$.
\end{proof}
This is the normalization of \cite[Definition 4.10, p.~67]{MT2007}.
Its compatibility with the logarithmic pinching condition is proved in
Section~\ref{sec:rf-pinching}.

\section{Distance comparison and cut-locus supports}
\label{sec:rf-distance-comparison}

We record the geometric comparison used for escaping limits and
Busemann levels.  All distances and covariant derivatives in this
section belong to one fixed metric.

\begin{lemma}[Squared-distance and triangle comparison]
\label{lem:rf-squared-distance-comparison}
Let $(M,g)$ be a complete connected Riemannian manifold with
nonnegative sectional curvature.  For every $p\in M$ and every
constant-speed geodesic $\eta$ of speed $v$, the function
\[
                  t\longmapsto d(p,\eta(t))^2-v^2t^2
\]
is concave on its parameter interval.
If $\alpha:[0,a]\to M$ and $\beta:[0,b]\to M$ are unit-speed
minimizing segments starting at $p$, with $a,b>0$, put
$c=d(\alpha(a),\beta(b))$.  Then
\[
 c^2\leq a^2+b^2-2ab
              \langle\dot\alpha(0),\dot\beta(0)\rangle,
\]
and, for $0\leq s\leq a$ and $0\leq t\leq b$,
\[
 d(\alpha(s),\beta(t))^2
 \geq s^2+t^2-2st\,\frac{a^2+b^2-c^2}{2ab}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Index forms and regular shortened segments.}
For a geodesic $\gamma:[0,L]\to M$ write
\[
 I(V,W)=\int_0^L
 \bigl(\langle D_tV,D_tW\rangle
       -\langle\mathcal R(V,\dot\gamma)\dot\gamma,W\rangle\bigr)\,dt.
\]
Here $D_t$ denotes covariant differentiation along $\gamma$.
If $\gamma$ minimizes and $V$ is continuous, piecewise smooth
and zero at the endpoints, then $I(V,V)\geq0$.  Indeed, realize
$V$ by $\exp_{\gamma(t)}(sV(t))$ for sufficiently small $s$,
using the same value at any junction.  This gives a variation
with fixed endpoints and smooth energy on each piece.
Cauchy--Schwarz bounds its energy below by its length squared
divided by twice the parameter length, and the minimizing
constant-speed geodesic attains this lower bound.  The second
variation is therefore nonnegative.  Differentiating the energy
twice, commuting the covariant derivatives and integrating by
parts identifies it with $I(V,V)$.

This also rules out an interior conjugate point.  Suppose a
nonzero Jacobi field $J$ vanishes at zero and at $c\in(0,L)$.
Extend its restriction to $[0,c]$ by zero to obtain $V$.
The Jacobi equation and integration by parts give $I(V,V)=0$.
Uniqueness for that linear second-order equation gives
$D_tJ(c)\ne0$.  Let $P_t$ be parallel transport from $c$ and
set $W(t)=t(L-t)P_tD_tJ(c)$.  Then
\[
 I(V,W)=c(L-c)|D_tJ(c)|^2>0.
\]
Consequently $I(V-\epsilon W,V-\epsilon W)<0$ for sufficiently
small $\epsilon>0$, a contradiction.

Take a minimizing unit-speed segment $\gamma:[0,d]\to M$
from $p$ to $x\ne p$.  For $0<\theta\leq1/2$, set
$q=\gamma(\theta d)$ and $r_0=(1-\theta)d$.
The exponential map at $q$ is nonsingular at the tangent vector
of length $r_0$ pointing along the remaining segment.  Otherwise
a kernel vector would produce a nonzero radial Jacobi field
vanishing at $q$ and $x$.  Reverse the full original segment:
the same field would vanish at its initial endpoint $x$ and at
the interior point $q$.  Its initial derivative cannot vanish
by uniqueness, contradicting the preceding index argument.
Thus a smooth inverse exponential branch exists near $x$.
Let $\rho$ be the norm of this branch.  It is smooth and
positive near $x$, $\rho(x)=r_0$, and the radial paths imply
\[
          d(p,y)\leq\theta d+\rho(y)
          \quad\hbox{near }x,\qquad
          d(p,x)=\theta d+\rho(x).
\]
Only an inverse branch is needed; no global smoothness of
distance is asserted.

\emph{The sharp limiting Hessian estimate.}
Parametrize the radial segment from $q$ to $x$ on $[0,1]$.
For $w\in T_xM$, endpoint variation through the inverse branch
gives a Jacobi field with $J(0)=0$ and $J(1)=w$.
If $Z(t)=tP_tw$, where now $P_t$ transports $w$ from the terminal
point, integration by parts and the Jacobi equation give
\[
 I(J,J)=I(J,Z)=\langle D_tJ(1),w\rangle .
\]
Since $Z-J$ vanishes at both endpoints, index nonnegativity
implies $I(J,J)\leq I(Z,Z)$.  Nonnegative sectional curvature
then gives $I(Z,Z)\leq |w|^2$.
The radial variation formula, or differentiation of its energy,
identifies $I(J,J)$ with
$\operatorname{Hess}(\rho^2/2)(w,w)$ at $x$.
Thus $\operatorname{Hess}\rho^2\leq2g$ at this endpoint.
For $l=\theta d$, differentiating the shifted square gives
\[
 \operatorname{Hess}(l+\rho)^2
 =\left(1+\frac l\rho\right)\operatorname{Hess}\rho^2
       -\frac{2l}{\rho}\,d\rho\otimes d\rho
 \leq\frac{2}{1-\theta}\,g
 \quad\hbox{at }x.
\]
Given any positive error, choose $\theta$ sufficiently small.
Composition with $\eta$ gives a touching smooth upper support
for $d(p,\eta(t))^2$ whose second derivative is at most
$2v^2$ plus that error.  At $\eta(t_0)=p$ the support
$v^2(t-t_0)^2$ follows directly from the length of $\eta$.
This handles both the cut locus and the basepoint.

\emph{From supports to concavity.}
Here is the scalar step, including the nonsmooth case.
Suppose a continuous function $f$ has touching twice smooth
upper supports with second derivative at most every prescribed
positive $\epsilon$.  If $f$ lay below its chord $A$ on
$[u,w]$, choose $\epsilon>0$ small enough that
\[
                   f(t)-A(t)+\epsilon(t-u)(w-t)
\]
is negative somewhere.  It is zero at the endpoints and has
a negative minimum at an interior point $t_0$.
Replace $f$ there by a touching upper support with second
derivative at most $\epsilon$.  The resulting smooth function
still has a local minimum at $t_0$, but its second derivative
is at most $-\epsilon$, impossible.  Hence $f$ is concave.
Apply this to $f(t)=d(p,\eta(t))^2-v^2t^2$ on every compact
subinterval.  No global minimizing hypothesis on $\eta$ was
needed for this local support passage.

\emph{The hinge and corresponding sides.}
For the hinge, take the reversed first segment from $\alpha(a)$
to $p$.  Its midpoint supplies the inverse branch just
constructed.  The shifted squared radius, composed with
$\beta$, touches
$F(t)=d(\alpha(a),\beta(t))^2$ from above at zero.
Gauss's lemma identifies its derivative there as
$-2a\langle\dot\alpha(0),\dot\beta(0)\rangle$.
The concave function $F(t)-t^2$ lies below this supporting
tangent: its secant slopes from zero decrease, and their
upper limit is at most the derivative of the touching
support.  Evaluating at $b$ proves the hinge inequality.

For corresponding sides put
\[
 D(s,t)=d(\alpha(s),\beta(t))^2-s^2-t^2 .
\]
It is concave in each variable separately, and minimality of
the two sides gives $D(0,t)=D(s,0)=0$.  Taking chords first
in $s$ and then in $t$ gives
\[
 D(s,t)\geq\frac{s}{a}D(a,t)
          \geq\frac{st}{ab}D(a,b).
\]
Since $D(a,b)=c^2-a^2-b^2$, this is precisely the second
comparison inequality, including the endpoints.
\end{proof}

The triangle statements are the two forms of
\cite[Theorem 2.4, p. 23]{MT2007}.  The shortened-segment
support and the reversal argument are the Calabi construction
in \cite[Lemma 7.1.9, pp. 284--285]{Petersen2016}.
The proof above keeps the selected minimizing segment and
its inverse branch, as needed when later estimates retain
a specified direction or a fixed convergence map.

\section{Local existence, uniqueness, and continuation}
\label{sec:rf-local}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:rf-local}
Let $M$ be a closed smooth $n$-manifold and $g_0$ a smooth metric.
There is a $T>0$ and a Ricci flow on $[0,T)$ with initial metric $g_0$.
Two Ricci flows on intervals with least element zero and the same initial
metric agree at every time in the intersection of their intervals.
If $0<T<\infty$ and a flow on $[0,T)$ satisfies
\[
 \exists C\geq0\quad\forall t\in[0,T)\quad\forall x\in M,\qquad
                       |\operatorname{Rm}|(x,t)\leq C,
\]
then it extends, agreeing on $[0,T)$, to $[0,T')$ for some $T'>T$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The gauge equation}

Fix $g_0$ and put
$W^k(\bar g)=\bar g^{ij}(\Gamma(\bar g)^k_{ij}-\Gamma(g_0)^k_{ij})$.
The difference of connections is a tensor, so this defines a vector field
independently of the chart.  The equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-deturck}
 \partial_t\bar g=-2\operatorname{Ric}_{\bar g}
                       +\mathcal L_{W(\bar g)}\bar g,\qquad \bar g(0)=g_0
\end{equation}
has principal part $\bar g^{ab}\partial_a\partial_b\bar g_{ij}$.
The mixed second derivatives in Ricci are cancelled by the Lie derivative.
For $\bar g$ in a sufficiently small neighborhood of $g_0$, this is
uniformly parabolic.

Here is the analytic construction used to solve this equation.  Choose
a finite family of smooth vector fields $F_i$ which is Parseval for $g_0$:
$\sum_i g_0(F_i,v)F_i=v$.  Such fields result from local orthonormal
frames and smooth cutoffs whose squares sum to one.  Fix also a smooth
positive measure $\mu$, obtained from a finite weighted coordinate cover.
For a smooth tensor $h$, its probes $h_{ij}=h(F_i,F_j)$ have norm
\[
 \|h\|_H^2=\int_M\sum_{i,j}|h_{ij}|^2\,d\mu.
\]
This is faithful: zero norm makes every continuous probe zero on the
support of $\mu$, which is all of $M$; the spanning property then makes
$h$ zero.  Let $H$ be the closure of these probes in the finite-vector-valued
$L^2$ space, restricting throughout to symmetric tensors.

The first-order space $V$ is the closure of the graph
\[
 h\longmapsto \bigl((h_{ij})_{ij},(F_kh_{ij})_{kij}\bigr),\qquad
 \|h\|_V^2=\|h\|_H^2+
                   \int_M\sum_{k,i,j}|F_kh_{ij}|^2\,d\mu.
\]
The projection $j:V\to H$ is injective.  Indeed, if the values of a
sequence tend to zero and its derivative probes tend to $w_{kij}$,
integration by parts against any compactly supported smooth test function
$\varphi$ gives
\[
 \int w_{kij}\varphi\,d\mu
 =-\lim_m\int (h_m)_{ij}
       \bigl(F_k\varphi+\varphi\operatorname{div}_{\mu}F_k\bigr)\,d\mu=0.
\]
Thus $w=0$.  The same finite charts and spanning fields identify bounded
$V$ sets locally with bounded first-order Sobolev sets.  Cutoff localization
and Rellich compactness make $j$ compact; smooth approximation makes its
range dense, and the graph norm gives $\|j\|\leq1$.

Consequently $B=jj^*:H\to H$ is compact, positive, self-adjoint and
injective.  Its orthonormal eigenvectors have eigenvalues $0<\beta_\alpha
\leq1$.  Set $\lambda_\alpha=\beta_\alpha^{-1}-1\geq0$ and let
$\mathcal A$ be the operator with these spectral parameters.  Its weak
definition is
\[
 \langle z,w\rangle_V=\langle jz,v+a\rangle_H\quad(z\in V),
 \qquad jw=v,\qquad \mathcal Av=a.
\]
These are derivatives of scalar probes; connection and cutoff terms
must be retained when comparing $\mathcal A$ with the geometric equation.
In local coordinates its principal part is the negative background
metric contraction, since $\sum_k F_k^aF_k^b=g_0^{ab}$.
We need to show that these spectral coordinates control actual spatial
derivatives.  For this purpose put
\[
 S_k=(I+\mathcal A)^{-k/2},\qquad k=0,1,2,\ldots.
\]
In the eigenbasis this multiplies the $\alpha$th coordinate by
$(1+\lambda_\alpha)^{-k/2}$.  We shall prove that every localized scalar
probe of $S_kx$ has weak derivatives through order $k$, bounded in $L^2$
by a constant times $\|x\|_H$.

Here is the local elliptic argument, including the cutoff terms.
Write $u=\eta h_{ab}$ in a chart, with $\eta$ compactly supported there.
The generator equation $\mathcal Ah=b$ gives a weak equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-local-divergence}
 -\sum_{i,j}\partial_j(a^{ij}\partial_i u)=G.
\end{equation}
The matrix $a$ is smooth and uniformly positive on a neighborhood of
$\operatorname{supp}\eta$.  It can be extended smoothly with compact
support in the chart; ellipticity is needed only on a fixed buffer
around that support.  The source $G$ consists of the localized probe of
$b$, fixed smooth coefficients times the probes of $h$ and their first
derivatives, and the divergence correction
$-\sum_{i,j}(\partial_j a^{ij})\partial_i u$.
The coefficients include the derivatives of the cutoff, the spanning
fields and the projection onto tensor probes.  Thus the other probes
can couple in the source, but their order is at most one.  Choose a
larger cutoff equal to one near $\operatorname{supp}\eta$ when forming
these terms.  Derivatives of the larger cutoff then vanish wherever
the smaller equation is tested.

For completeness, the gain of two derivatives in
\eqref{eq:rf-local-divergence} follows directly from difference quotients.
Let $\delta_h f=(f(\,\cdot+he_k)-f)/h$, and suppose
$a^{ij}\xi_i\xi_j\geq\ell|\xi|^2$ on the buffer and
$|Da^{ij}|\leq B$.  Test the weak equation with
$-\delta_{-h}\delta_h u$, first on smooth approximations to its
first-order graph.  Discrete integration by parts and
\[
 \delta_h(a^{ij}\partial_i u)
 =a^{ij}(\,\cdot+he_k)\delta_h\partial_i u
      +(\delta_h a^{ij})\partial_i u
\]
give, with $X^2=\sum_i\|\delta_h\partial_i u\|_2^2$,
\[
 \ell X^2
 \leq \left(\|G\|_2+nB\sum_i\|\partial_i u\|_2\right)X.
\]
Here $\|\delta_{-h}\delta_h u\|_2\leq X$, and the coefficient
error follows from $|\delta_h a^{ij}|\leq B$.  Translations remain in
the elliptic buffer when $|h|$ is small.  Density passes the identity
to the first-order graph, giving the uniform bound
\[
 \|\delta_h\partial_i u\|_2
 \leq \ell^{-1}\left(\|G\|_2+nB\sum_j\|\partial_j u\|_2\right).
\]
Pairing with a smooth compactly supported test function and moving
$\delta_h$ to that function produces a bounded functional whose limit
is $-\int \partial_i u\,\partial_k\varphi$.  The Riesz representation
theorem supplies $\partial_k\partial_i u\in L^2$ with the same bound.

To continue the argument, differentiating the weak equation once yields
the same principal operator on $\partial_k u$, with source
\[
 \partial_kG+\sum_{i,j}\left[
  (\partial_j\partial_k a^{ij})\partial_i u
       +(\partial_k a^{ij})\partial_j\partial_i u\right].
\]
This identity is obtained by applying the original equation to
$\partial_k\varphi$ and integrating each coefficient derivative by
parts.  Weak mixed derivatives commute because the corresponding
derivatives of the test function commute.  Repeating this calculation
shows that derivatives of order $s$ of $G$, together with derivatives
through order $s+1$ of $u$, give derivatives through order $s+2$ of $u$.
Every commutator has a derivative on a smooth coefficient, so its
derivative of $u$ has order at most $s+1$.  For the generator equation,
the nested cutoff ensures that its lower-order source has $s$
derivatives whenever all probes of $h$ have $s+1$ derivatives on the
larger localization and the probes of $b$ have $s$ on the smaller one.

Now $S_0x=x$ has localized $L^2$ values, and $S_1x$ belongs to the
first-order space $V$ by the eigenbasis of its form norm.  The identities
\[
 S_{k+2}=S_{k+1}S_1,\qquad
 \mathcal A S_{k+2}x=S_kx-S_{k+2}x
\]
complete a two-step induction.  The first identity gives $k+1$
derivatives of every probe of $S_{k+2}x$ on every localization; the
right side of the second has $k$ derivatives.  The local argument
therefore supplies the missing order $k+2$.  No unproved smoothness of
the spectral eigenvectors is needed for this induction.

These weak derivatives depend boundedly on $x$.  Indeed, uniqueness
against compactly supported test functions makes each derivative map
linear.  If $x_m\to x$ and its derivative of a fixed order converges
in $L^2$ to $w$, passing to the limit in the weak derivative identity
identifies $w$ with the derivative of $S_kx$.  The graph is closed,
so the closed graph theorem makes the map bounded.  There are finitely
many charts, probes and derivative words at each fixed order; summing
their operator bounds gives one constant chosen before $x$.

The precise evaluation budget is as follows.  If $u$ has $k$ weak
derivatives, its derivative $D^wu$ has a bounded continuous representative
provided
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-evaluation-budget}
 2p+|w|\leq k,\qquad n<4p.
\end{equation}
To see this, apply $(1-c\Delta)^p$ to $D^wu$, where $c>0$ normalizes
the Fourier convention.  This is a finite sum of the available weak
derivatives and has Fourier transform
$(1+|\xi|^2)^p\widehat{D^wu}(\xi)$.  Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \|\widehat{D^wu}\|_1
 \leq\left(\int_{\mathbb R^n}(1+|\xi|^2)^{-2p}\,d\xi\right)^{1/2}
       \|(1+|\xi|^2)^p\widehat{D^wu}\|_2.
\]
The integral is finite exactly when $n<4p$; Fourier inversion then
gives the continuous representative and its supremum bound.
Localized directional derivatives along the fields $F_i$ are finite
sums of coordinate derivatives, with smooth coefficients, so the same
budget applies to them.  For continuous values one can take $p=n+1$
and $k=2(n+1)$; continuous derivatives through order $s$ require
$k\geq2(n+1)+s$.  We retain this extra derivative budget whenever
continuous jets are used below.

The positive self-adjoint background operator also has a spectral heat
response.  The
state space is a square-summable sequence Hilbert space $\mathcal H$
for the localized tensor coordinates; forcing lies in
$L^2([0,T];\mathcal H)$.  In a
spectral coordinate with parameter $\lambda_\alpha\geq0$, zero initial
data and forcing $f_\alpha$ give the response
\[
 u_\alpha(t)=\int_0^t e^{-\lambda_\alpha(t-s)}f_\alpha(s)\,ds .
\]
The weighted response and its derivative coordinates give a bounded
linear map $\mathcal S_T$ on the forcing space.  The underlying energy
estimate follows by multiplying
$u_\alpha'+\lambda_\alpha u_\alpha=f_\alpha$ by
$\lambda_\alpha u_\alpha$, integrating and summing:
\[
 \frac12\sum_\alpha\lambda_\alpha |u_\alpha(T)|^2
 +\frac12\int_0^T\sum_\alpha\lambda_\alpha^2|u_\alpha|^2
 \leq\frac12\int_0^T\sum_\alpha|f_\alpha|^2.
\]
The same argument after spectral weighting gives the higher-order
estimate.  Time integration and Cauchy--Schwarz give the additional
$\sqrt T$ in the lower-order estimate.  Applying the nonlinear
residual of \eqref{eq:rf-deturck} to this response gives a map
$\mathcal N_T$ on that space.

The residual has a mixed estimate which is crucial for this construction.
Write a metric's lower jet as $p=(G,\partial G)$ and its second jet as
$Q=(\partial_a\partial_bG_{ij})$.  On a fixed chart the source is
$G^{-1}:Q+L(p)$, with $L$ smooth on positive metric jets.  Subtracting
the fixed principal part gives
\[
 E(p,Q)=(G^{-1}-g_0^{-1}):Q+L(p).
\]
For $q=(H,\partial H)$ and second jet $S$, subtraction splits exactly as
\[
\begin{split}
 E(p,Q)-E(q,S)
 ={}&(G^{-1}-g_0^{-1}):(Q-S)\\
    &+(G^{-1}-H^{-1}):S+L(p)-L(q).
\end{split}
\]
On a compact convex positive lower-jet neighborhood, inversion and $L$
have Lipschitz constants $L_I,L_0$.  In component supremum norms this gives
\[
\begin{split}
 |E(p,Q)-E(q,S)|\leq{}&n^2|G^{-1}-g_0^{-1}|\,|Q-S|\\
 &+n^2L_I|p-q|\,|S|+L_0|p-q|.
\end{split}
\]
The first coefficient is small when the metric value is close to $g_0$;
no pointwise bound on the second jet is required.  In spacetime this
estimate uses continuous lower jets and $L^2$ second jets, by
$\|av\|_2\leq\|a\|_\infty\|v\|_2$.
For differentiated probes, Leibniz's rule gives the same highest-derivative
term and finitely many lower-jet products.  Their estimates keep track
of the order of each factor.  If $D_w$ is an ordered word of at most
$q$ derivatives along the fields $F_i$, then $D_w(fg)$ is a sum over
the $2^{|w|}$ assignments of its derivatives to the two factors.  Each
assignment preserves the order within each factor and the two word
lengths sum to $|w|$.  At least one length is at most $\lfloor q/2\rfloor$.
Consequently, if $a,b$ bound the supremum norms of these lower derivatives
of $f,g$ and $A,B$ bound their $L^2$ derivatives through order $q$, then
\[
 \|D_w(fg)\|_2\leq 2^{|w|}(aB+bA).
\]
This follows term by term from $L^\infty\times L^2\to L^2$; it does
not require the fields to commute.  The continuous lower derivatives
are recovered using \eqref{eq:rf-evaluation-budget}, so a scale used for
this purpose must have order at least $2p+\lfloor q/2\rfloor$ as well
as the required $q$ square-integrable derivatives.

For the actual source there is a stronger finite-order observation.
Assign weight $j$ to a metric derivative of order $j$, and weight zero
to an undifferentiated inverse metric entry.  The coordinate Ricci and
Lie derivative formulas have total derivative weight at most two in
each monomial.  Differentiating an inverse uses
$D(G^{-1})=-G^{-1}(DG)G^{-1}$, and hence raises this weight by one,
just as differentiation of a metric jet does.  After a word of length
at most $2r$, the total weight is at most $2r+2$.  Two factors of
weight greater than $r+1$ cannot occur in the same monomial.  The
differentiated source is therefore exactly
\[
 c(\text{lower jets})+
       L(\text{lower jets})(\text{higher jets}),
\]
where $L$ is linear in its last argument, and $c,L$ depend smoothly on
the jets of order at most $r+1$ and on the inverse metric values.
This identity can also be checked recursively: on multiplying two
expressions of total weight at most $2r+2$, the product of their two
higher-jet parts is absent.  Finite sums and ordered differentiation
preserve the asserted form.  Thus these products never require
multiplication of two unrestricted $L^2$ functions.

Choose $2p\leq r$ and $n<4p$.  The trace scale of order $2r+1$
then recovers continuous jets through order $r+1$, since
$2p+(r+1)\leq2r+1$.  It also retains square-integrable derivatives
through order $2r+1$.  The higher response has square-integrable
derivatives through order $2r+2$, while the differentiated forcing has
order $2r$.  The concrete choice $p=n+1$, $r=2(n+1)$ satisfies all
these inequalities.  The coefficients $c,L$ act on continuous paths
of lower jets, and $L$ multiplies a spacetime $L^2$ higher jet.  The
constant term is included using the constant-one $L^2$ function on
the compact manifold and finite time interval.  Its time norm brings
the factor $\sqrt T$.  Smooth dependence follows from this affine
form and smooth composition on a neighborhood of the compact range
of lower jets.  Matrix inversion is smooth there because positivity
keeps the determinants away from zero.

Chart cutoffs and probe coefficients are fixed smooth
functions, so the finite sum transporting these estimates to $H$ is
bounded.  Terms from the fixed background jet and from the probe
generator enter the lower-order constant.  Smooth dependence follows
from smooth composition on a compact positive lower-jet neighborhood
and the continuous bilinear map $C^0\times L^2\to L^2$.

The positivity neighborhood is fixed before the forcing radius.
Let $B_0$ be the norm of the bounded map from trace coordinates
to the continuous vector of empty-word probes, and put
$\eta_+=1/[4(B_0+1)]$.  If a trace state has norm at most
$\eta_+$, its probe vector has norm at most $1/4$ at every point.
The Parseval identity reconstructs the tensor from these probes and
gives $|h(v,v)|\leq g_0(v,v)/4$.  Choose a radius
$\eta\leq\eta_+$ also inside the neighborhoods where the residual
and background-correction formulas are exact.  Cut off outside this
trace neighborhood.  Enlarge the resulting principal constant, if
necessary, to at least $1/\eta$; the forcing radius chosen below
then satisfies $2\rho_F<\eta$.
Let $C_1,C_2\geq0$ be the resulting principal and lower-order
mixed-estimate constants, and let $A$ be the norm of its spatial
value at zero.  The spectral energy estimate bounds the high response
by $2\|F\|$ and the intermediate response by
$2\sqrt T\|F\|$.  To compare two nonlinear residuals, first vary the
highest derivative with its coefficient fixed, then vary the
coefficient with that derivative fixed.  On a forcing ball of radius
$\rho$, these two principal terms each contribute at most
$4C_1\rho\|F-G\|$.  The lower-order term contributes
$2C_2\sqrt T\|F-G\|$.  Thus
\[
 \|\mathcal N_T(F)-\mathcal N_T(G)\|
 \leq(8C_1\rho+2C_2\sqrt T)\|F-G\|,\qquad
 \|\mathcal N_T(0)\|\leq\sqrt T A.
\]
Choose
\[
 \rho_F=\frac1{64(C_1+1)},\qquad
 s=\min\left\{1,\frac1{16(C_2+1)},\frac{\rho_F}{4(A+1)}\right\},
 \qquad T=s^2.
\]
Then $8C_1\rho_F\leq1/8$, $2C_2\sqrt T\leq1/8$ and
$\sqrt T A\leq\rho_F/4$.  We have therefore obtained
\[
 \|\mathcal N_T(F)-\mathcal N_T(G)\|\leq\tfrac14\|F-G\|,
 \qquad \|\mathcal N_T(0)\|\leq\tfrac14\rho_F
 \quad(\|F\|,\|G\|\leq\rho_F).
\]
Thus the closed ball is invariant, and the contraction iteration converges
to a unique $F$ in it.  At the fixed point,
$\|F\|\leq\rho_F/4+\|F\|/4$, hence $\|F\|\leq\rho_F/3$.
The recovery estimate bounds the tensor trace coordinates by $2\|F\|$.
Thus $2\|F\|\leq2\rho_F/3<\eta$, strictly inside both the
exactness and positivity neighborhoods.  In particular the recovered
tensor $h$ satisfies
$g_0+h\geq3g_0/4$.  The cutoff is therefore inactive along the solution.
The response equation gives the actual tensor integral equation
\[
 h(t)=\int_0^t
   \bigl(-2\operatorname{Ric}_{g_0+h(s)}
       +\mathcal L_{W(g_0+h(s))}(g_0+h(s))\bigr)\,ds.
\]

Spatial smoothness is recovered by introducing smooth localized
diffeomorphism parameters, with the original $T$ kept fixed.
We explain why the smooth fixed-point branch is the actual pullback
orbit.  For any background metric $Q$, denote the right side of
\eqref{eq:rf-deturck} with that background by $\mathscr D_Q(g)$,
and write
\[
 N_Q(h)=\mathcal Ah+\mathscr D_Q(g_0+h).
\]
This expression is interpreted between the high spatial level and
the forcing level already constructed.  Let $u_F$ be the zero-initial
linear response, so $(\partial_t+\mathcal A)u_F=F$.
For a smooth seed $\kappa$, the parameter equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-parameter-forcing}
             F=N_Q(\kappa+u_F)-\mathcal A\kappa.
\end{equation}
The seed is inserted at both the continuous trace and the
square-integrable high level.  The subtraction makes this precisely
$\partial_tu_F=\mathscr D_Q(g_0+\kappa+u_F)$.

The right side is smooth in the background, seed and forcing near
$(g_0,0,F)$.  To check the only new term, changing background from
$g_0$ to $Q$ changes the gauge vector by
$g^{ab}(\Gamma(g_0)-\Gamma(Q))^i_{ab}$.
Its Lie derivative contains at most first derivatives of the current
metric; the current metric's second-derivative terms cancel.
Thus this correction depends on lower current jets and smooth fixed
background jets.  It is covered by the preceding composition and
mixed estimates.  At the original solution the derivative in the
forcing variable has norm at most $1/4$, since the solution lies
strictly inside the contraction ball.  Therefore
$I-D_F\mathcal N_T$ is invertible, with inverse
$\sum_{j\geq0}(D_F\mathcal N_T)^j$, and the Banach implicit-function
theorem gives a locally unique smooth branch for
\eqref{eq:rf-parameter-forcing}.

Now take a smooth family $\Phi_p$ of diffeomorphisms with
$\Phi_0=\mathrm{id}$, let $P_p=\Phi_p^*$, and put
$Q_p=P_pg_0$, $\kappa_p=Q_p-g_0$.  If $u=u_F$, its
transported zero-initial forcing must be
\[
 F_p=P_pF+[\mathcal A,P_p]u,
 \qquad [\mathcal A,P_p]=\mathcal AP_p-P_p\mathcal A.
\]
Indeed $(\partial_t+\mathcal A)P_pu=F_p$, so linear-response
uniqueness gives $u_{F_p}=P_pu$.  The commutator maps the high
spatial level to the forcing level.  Pullbacks are uniformly bounded
on these levels for small $p$, and are strongly continuous there:
first verify continuity for smooth tensors, then use density and
the uniform bounds.  Dominated convergence gives continuity of
$F_p$ in the time-$L^2$ space.  Smooth parameter dependence of this
rough pullback action is not assumed.

By continuity, decrease the parameter neighborhood so that
$\|\kappa_p\|_{\mathrm{trace}}+2\|F_p\|<\eta$, where the
seed norm is at the trace level $2r+1$.  For a smooth perturbation
$v$ within these margins, covariance of the Ricci--DeTurck equation gives
\[
 N_{Q_p}(\kappa_p+P_pv)-\mathcal A\kappa_p
   =P_pN_{g_0}(v)+[\mathcal A,P_p]v.
\]
To pass to the current Sobolev state at almost every time, approximate
by smooth states at the high level.  The strict trace margins keep both the original
and pulled-back metrics positive and inside the exact residual
neighborhood; the mixed estimates pass the identity to the limit.
Now substitute $N_{g_0}(u)=F$.  The right side is $F_p$, so
$F_p$ solves \eqref{eq:rf-parameter-forcing} in the forcing space.
Its continuity places it in the local uniqueness neighborhood for
small $p$, so it equals the smooth implicit-function branch.
Only the parameter neighborhood shrinks; the time interval is the
one chosen before any parameter family.

Finally choose localized smooth vector fields spanning $T_xM$.
Composing their small flows makes $p\mapsto\Phi_p(x)$ a local
coordinate map after selecting $n$ independent fields.
Evaluation of the smooth trace-state orbit at $x$, followed by
undoing the two tensor-slot differentials of $\Phi_p$, gives the
tensor at $\Phi_p(x)$.  This proves spatial smoothness with values
in $C([0,T])$, hence continuity in time of every spatial derivative.
The integral equation then supplies all time and mixed derivatives,
including their one-sided values at zero.  This constructs a smooth
solution of \eqref{eq:rf-deturck} on one common time interval.

Solve $\partial_t\Phi_t=-W(\bar g(t))\circ\Phi_t$ with $\Phi_0=\mathrm{id}$.
On a possibly shorter interval compactness gives smooth diffeomorphisms.
For $g(t)=\Phi_t^*\bar g(t)$ the derivative-of-pullback identity gives
\[
 \partial_tg
 =\Phi_t^*(\partial_t\bar g-\mathcal L_W\bar g)
 =-2\Phi_t^*\operatorname{Ric}_{\bar g}=-2\operatorname{Ric}_g.
\]
This proves existence.
The gauge mechanism is discussed in \cite[Theorem 3.11, pp.~39--40]{MT2007}.

\subsection{Comparison energy and the terminal metric}

Uniqueness uses an energy comparison of the original flows.  On a compact
common slab write
\[
 H=g-\widehat g,\qquad A=\nabla-\widehat\nabla,\qquad
 S=\mathcal R-\widehat{\mathcal R},
\]
where $\mathcal R$ is the curvature tensor with one raised index.
The metrics are uniformly equivalent there, and all coefficients and
derivatives needed below are bounded.  Choose a finite coordinate cover
and subordinate squared cutoffs.  The sum of the integrals of
$|H|^2+|A|^2+|S|^2$, against a fixed smooth positive density, is a
nonnegative energy $E$ which vanishes exactly when these tensors vanish.
Let $D$ be the analogous sum of squared coordinate first derivatives
of $S$.  On the fixed compact chart regions, the inverse metric has
one ellipticity lower bound $\lambda>0$.

Subtracting the metric equations bounds the metric contribution to $E'$
by $C_HE$.  Subtracting the connection variation formula introduces
first derivatives of $S$, while $A$ occurs only undifferentiated
with bounded coefficients.  Young's inequality bounds
this contribution by $C_\epsilon E+\epsilon D$ for any
$\epsilon>0$.

The curvature equation needs a divergence formulation: direct
subtraction of its Laplacians would leave derivatives of $A$ that
are not controlled by $E$.  In one coordinate chart let
$\Gamma_j*T$ denote the connection action on the tensor slots of
$T$.  Its flux difference splits as
\[
 g^{ij}\nabla_j\mathcal R-
       \widehat g^{ij}\widehat\nabla_j\widehat{\mathcal R}
       =g^{ij}\partial_j S+U^i,
\]
where
\[
 U^i=g^{ij}(\Gamma_j*S)
    +(g^{ij}-\widehat g^{ij})\widehat\nabla_j\widehat{\mathcal R}
    +g^{ij}(A_j*\widehat{\mathcal R}).
\]
The inverse identity
$g^{-1}-\widehat g^{-1}=-g^{-1}H\widehat g^{-1}$ shows
$|U|^2\leq C(|H|^2+|A|^2+|S|^2)$.
Expanding the remaining connection actions in the divergence and
the curvature reaction gives
\[
 \partial_t S=\partial_i(g^{ij}\partial_jS)+\partial_iU^i+W,
 \qquad
 |W|\leq C(|\partial S|+|H|+|A|+|S|).
\]
All coefficients here are uniformly bounded on the chosen slab.
In particular no derivative of $A$ remains in $W$.

Pair this equation with $2\chi^2S$ and integrate against the fixed
coordinate density.  The principal term contributes at most
$-2\lambda\int\chi^2|\partial S|^2$ before the lower terms
are absorbed.  Integrating $\partial_iU^i$ by parts transfers its
derivative to $\chi^2S$ and the density.  Its pairing with
$\partial S$, and the corresponding term from $W$, are bounded
by arbitrarily small multiples of $D$ plus a constant times $E$.
Derivatives of the fixed cutoffs and density have bounded coefficients;
the finite cover makes their zero-order integrals controlled by $E$.
After choosing these small multiples, the curvature contribution is
at most $C_SE-cD$ for some $c>0$ depending on ellipticity.
Choose $\epsilon=c/2$ in the connection estimate.  Consequently
\[
                         E'\leq CE-\tfrac c2D\leq CE.
\]
Equal initial metrics have equal Levi--Civita connections and curvatures,
so $E(0)=0$.  Gronwall gives $E=0$.  For any common interior target time,
apply this on a compact slab reaching that time; continuity deals with an
included terminal endpoint.  No uniform coefficient bound over an entire
maximal interval is required.

For continuation, the assumed curvature bound first gives uniform
equivalence to $g(0)$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:rf-metric-comparison}.
Derivative estimates on slabs bounded away from zero give uniform bounds
for $\nabla^j\operatorname{Rm}$ of every order near $T$.  The connection
variation formula controls the time derivative of the connection by
$\nabla\operatorname{Ric}$.  The coordinate induction has a useful
triangular form.  Let $L_r$ be the fully covariant coordinate components
of $\nabla^r\operatorname{Rm}$, and let $*$ denote finite sums
of the indicated contractions, with their fixed signs.
Metric compatibility and the variation formula give
\[
 \partial g^{-1}=-\Gamma*g^{-1},\qquad
 \partial L_r=L_{r+1}+\Gamma*L_r,
 \qquad \partial_t\Gamma=g^{-1}*g^{-1}*L_1.
\]
Uniform metric equivalence and the covariant curvature bounds control
the undifferentiated $g^{-1}$ and every $L_r$.  If the connection jets
strictly below order $m$ are bounded, the first two identities bound
the order-$m$ coordinate jets of these tensors.  Differentiating the
last identity therefore bounds $\partial_t\partial^m\Gamma$
without requiring a bound on $\partial^m\Gamma$ on its right side.
Integrate from one fixed $a\in(0,T)$ to obtain that missing bound.
This proves every connection-jet bound by induction, starting at
$m=0$.  Finally $\partial_tg=-2g^{-1}*L_0$ bounds the time rate
of every metric jet; integration from $a$ bounds the jet itself.
Their time derivatives are
bounded near $T$, so each jet is uniformly Cauchy as $t\uparrow T$.
Their limits are compatible under differentiation.  They define a smooth
metric $g_T$, and uniform metric equivalence makes it positive definite.
Start the local construction at $g_T$ and translate its time origin to
$T$.  The equation determines the limiting time derivatives from the
spatial jets on both sides.  Induction gives matching mixed derivatives,
so the old and new metrics glue smoothly.  This proves continuation.
See \cite[Proposition 3.12, p.~40; Proposition 4.12, p.~68]{MT2007}.

\section{Evolution, derivative estimates, and scalar comparison}
\label{sec:rf-curvature}

For fixed tangent inputs, put
$B(u,v,w,z)=\sum_{a,b}\operatorname{Rm}(u,e_a,v,e_b)
                         \operatorname{Rm}(w,e_a,z,e_b)$
in an orthonormal basis.  Define
\[
\begin{split}
 Q(u,v,w,z)={}&2\{B(u,v,w,z)-B(u,v,z,w)\\
              &\hspace{13mm}-B(u,z,v,w)+B(u,w,v,z)\}\\
 &-\operatorname{Rm}(\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}u,v,w,z)
  -\operatorname{Rm}(u,\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}v,w,z)\\
 &-\operatorname{Rm}(u,v,\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}w,z)
  -\operatorname{Rm}(u,v,w,\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}z).
\end{split}
\]
These are contractions of tensors, hence basis independent.

\begin{proposition}[Evolution identities]\label{prop:rf-evolution}
On every smooth Ricci flow, scalar curvature is smooth in space and time,
and
\[
\begin{split}
 (\partial_t-\Delta)\operatorname{Rm}&=Q,\\
 (\partial_t-\Delta)\operatorname{Ric}(u,v)
 &=2\sum_{a,b}\operatorname{Rm}(u,e_a,v,e_b)\operatorname{Ric}(e_a,e_b)
       -2\sum_a\operatorname{Ric}(u,e_a)\operatorname{Ric}(e_a,v),\\
 (\partial_t-\Delta)R&=2|\operatorname{Ric}|^2.
\end{split}
\]
At an included endpoint these are derivatives within the time interval.
Also $|\nabla^0\operatorname{Rm}|=|\operatorname{Rm}|$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Differentiating metric compatibility and torsion freeness gives
\[
 \partial_t\Gamma^k_{ij}
 =-g^{k\ell}(\nabla_i\operatorname{Ric}_{j\ell}
             +\nabla_j\operatorname{Ric}_{i\ell}
             -\nabla_\ell\operatorname{Ric}_{ij}).
\]
Insert this in the antisymmetrized derivative defining curvature.
Commuting the two covariant derivatives contributes the curvature
action on each tensor slot.  The differential Bianchi identity turns
the remaining second derivatives into the rough Laplacian; the
quadratic contractions group into the four displayed $B$ terms.
Differentiating the lowering metric supplies the fixed-input corrections
in $Q$.  In each trace one must also differentiate the inverse metric:
$\partial_tg^{ij}=2\operatorname{Ric}^{ij}$.  The first trace gives the
Ricci formula and the second cancels its negative Ricci-square term,
leaving $2|\operatorname{Ric}|^2$.  These calculations and the curvature
symmetries also prove the claimed regularity.
\end{proof}
The fixed-input convention matters in these contractions; compare
\cite[equations (3.5)--(3.6), p.~41; Lemma 3.15, p.~42]{MT2007}.

\begin{theorem}[Local derivative estimates]\label{thm:rf-shi}
For every $n,k\in\mathbb N$ and $K,\alpha,r>0$ there is a constant
$C=C(n,k,K,\alpha,r)>0$ with the following property.  Let $0<T\leq\alpha/K$
and let $g(t)$ be a Ricci flow on $[0,T]$.  If
$\overline{B_{g(0)}(p,r)}$ is compact and $|\operatorname{Rm}|\leq K$ on
$B_{g(0)}(p,r)\times[0,T]$, then
\[
 |\nabla^k\operatorname{Rm}|(x,t)\leq C t^{-k/2}
 \quad (x\in B_{g(0)}(p,r/2),\ 0<t\leq T).
\]
There is also, for every $n,k,l,K,\alpha,r$, a constant with the same
independence from the manifold, center and flow such that
\[
 |\nabla^k\operatorname{Rm}|(x,t)
       \leq C t^{-\max\{k-l,0\}/2}.
\]
For this second assertion assume the same compact-ball condition,
$|\operatorname{Rm}|\leq K$ on the whole manifold throughout $[0,T]$,
and $|\nabla^j\operatorname{Rm}|(x,0)\leq K$ on the whole manifold for
$0\leq j\leq l$.  The conclusion holds on the half ball for
$t\in[0,T]$ with $t>0$ or $k\leq l$; when $k\leq l$ its right side is $C$,
including at zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $N_j=|\nabla^j\operatorname{Rm}|$.  The differentiated curvature equation
gives
\[
 (\partial_t-\Delta)|\nabla^k\operatorname{Rm}|^2
 \leq-2|\nabla^{k+1}\operatorname{Rm}|^2+
 C(n,k)\sum_{i+j=k}|\nabla^i\operatorname{Rm}|
                      |\nabla^j\operatorname{Rm}|
                      |\nabla^k\operatorname{Rm}|.
\]
Indeed, commute a covariant derivative past the curvature heat equation.
The commutator with the Laplacian consists of contractions of curvature
with a tensor derivative and of $\nabla\operatorname{Rm}$ with the tensor.
The time commutator is the connection variation, a contraction of
$\nabla\operatorname{Ric}$ with that tensor.  Induction therefore
expresses the order-$k$ reaction as a finite sum of contractions of
$\nabla^i\operatorname{Rm}$ and $\nabla^j\operatorname{Rm}$ with $i+j=k$.
On differentiating its squared norm, the Bochner identity contributes
$-2N_{k+1}^2$; differentiating the inverse metrics in the norm contributes
only another multiple of $N_0N_k^2$.  Cauchy--Schwarz for the finite
contractions gives the displayed dimension- and order-dependent constant.
We describe the localization and induction explicitly.

\emph{Distance supports and cutoffs.}
For a fixed metric with compact $\overline{B(p,R)}$ and $|\operatorname{Rm}|
\leq K$ there, the distance from $p$ has the following upper supports.
At $q\in B(p,R)$ with $d=d(p,q)>0$ and for every $\varepsilon>0$, there
is a smooth function $u$ near $q$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-shi-distance-support}
 u(q)=d,\quad d(p,y)\leq u(y),\quad |\nabla u|(q)\leq1,
 \qquad \Delta u(q)\leq n/d+nKd+\varepsilon.
\end{equation}
One can construct these without choosing a minimizing geodesic.  Choose
continuously differentiable paths $\gamma_j:[0,1]\to B(p,R)$ from $p$ to $q$ with energies
$c_j^2\to d^2$, where $d\leq c_j\leq R$, and with uniformly bounded
speeds.  Such paths come from the definition of distance, followed by
near-constant-speed reparametrization using a strictly positive
regularization of their continuous speed.  A finite coordinate cover
of the compact closure, with smaller compact chart regions, allows a
common finite subdivision of all paths.  Parallel transport an
orthonormal frame along each path, using the linear connection ODE
and the coordinate changes at subdivision points.  More explicitly,
if chart metric comparison has constant $L_0$ and the Christoffel
coefficients are bounded by $B_0$, the speed bound gives a connection
ODE coefficient bound $B_0L_0R$.  Choose the mesh shorter than
$1/(2B_0L_0R+1)$ and with $R$ times its length smaller than the chart
buffer radius.  Solve $P'=-\Gamma(\dot\gamma)P$ backwards from the
terminal frame on successive intervals.  Metric compatibility makes
$g(Pv,Pw)$ constant, so the frames remain isometric and their coordinate
norms are at most $L_0$.  These choices are uniform over the path sequence.

We need coordinates $z$ at $q$ whose differential is an isometry and
whose coordinate Hessians vanish at $q$.  Start with an ordinary chart
$h$ normalized to have this differential and $h(q)=0$.  If $C^i_{ab}$
are its coordinate Hessians in the chosen orthonormal frame, compose
with $w^i\mapsto w^i-\tfrac12\sum_{a,b}C^i_{ab}w^aw^b$.
Its derivative at zero is the identity, so the inverse function theorem
makes this a local chart; the Hessian chain rule cancels $C^i_{ab}$.
These coordinates suffice to identify the gradient norm and Laplacian
of a support polynomial with its Euclidean gradient and Hessian trace
at zero.  Vary the path endpoint to $z$ in this chart.
Use the variation field $sP_j(s)z$, where $P_j$ is the parallel frame,
and choose its second coordinate jet to be
$-\Gamma(sP_jz,sP_jz)$.  Thus its covariant second variation at zero
is zero.  Local coordinate polynomials realize these jets; corrections
at the subdivision endpoints make neighboring segments meet, without
changing these first two jets.  This gives piecewise continuously
differentiable paths, depending smoothly on $z$, with
the prescribed endpoints, of total energy $E_j(z)$, and hence
$d(p,z)\leq\sqrt{E_j(z)}$.  The first and second variations of the
energy density at $z=0$ are
\[
 2\langle P_jv,\dot\gamma_j\rangle,\qquad
 2\bigl(|v|^2-
   \operatorname{Rm}(\dot\gamma_j,sP_jv,\dot\gamma_j,sP_jv)\bigr).
\]
They hold on each closed segment; interval additivity sums them to the
actual path energy.  Consequently the first two energy jets $L_j,Q_j$
satisfy
\[
 E_j(0)=c_j^2,\quad \|L_j\|\leq2c_j,\qquad
 \operatorname{tr}Q_j\leq2n+2nKc_j^2.
\]
The first inequality is integral Cauchy--Schwarz, and the trace bound
uses $0\leq s\leq1$ and $|\operatorname{Rm}|\leq K$ in the second
variation.  The finite chart cover and bounded path speeds and frame
matrices put the coordinate data in a compact set.  Taylor's theorem
for these coordinate variations gives one parameter radius, a uniform
bound on $Q_j$, and a uniform cubic remainder for $E_j$.  These auxiliary
bounds may depend on this metric and these charts; only the displayed
gradient and trace bounds must be uniform in the final cutoff estimate.

Since $c_j\geq d>0$, taking the square root preserves a uniform cubic
remainder on a smaller common radius.  The resulting linear and Hessian
jets are
\[
 A_j=L_j/(2c_j),\qquad
 H_j=Q_j/(2c_j)-(L_j\otimes L_j)/(4c_j^3).
\]
They have bounded norm, $\|A_j\|\leq1$, and
$\operatorname{tr}H_j\leq n/c_j+nKc_j$.  Choose a convergent subsequence
of the pair $(A_j,H_j)$ in its finite-dimensional space, before choosing
any spatial point.  Its limits $A,H$ satisfy
\[
 d(p,z)\leq d+Az+\tfrac12H(z,z)+C|z|^3,\quad
 \|A\|\leq1,\quad \operatorname{tr}H\leq n/d+nKd.
\]
For $\mu=\varepsilon/(2(n+1))$, restrict to $C|z|\leq\mu$ and replace
the cubic term by $\mu|z|^2$.  The resulting quadratic polynomial
touches the distance exactly, increases its Hessian trace by
$2n\mu\leq\varepsilon$, and leaves its gradient unchanged.  Pulling
back through the normal chart proves \eqref{eq:rf-shi-distance-support}.

Now fix a spatial margin $\delta>0$ inside the initial ball and a
center $x$ at that margin from its boundary.  Local metric comparison
gives $|v|_{g(0)}\leq e^{n\alpha}|v|_{g(t)}$.  Apply this to each
initial part of a path until its first exit from the initial ball.
A path of $g(t)$-length less than $e^{-n\alpha}\delta/4$ cannot reach
that exit, since its initial length would be less than $\delta/4$.
By choosing an initial closed ball with radius strictly between
$\delta/4$ and $3\delta/4$, we obtain a compact set $C$ in the preceding
initial ball containing
\[
 \overline{B_{g(t)}(x,R_*)},\qquad
 R_*=e^{-n\alpha}\delta/4,\qquad 0\leq t\leq T.
\]
The radius of $C$ is distinct from $R_*$.  Compactness follows by taking
a closed subset of the given compact initial closure.

Let $\phi$ be the square of a smooth decreasing cutoff, equal to one
on $(-\infty,1/2]$ and zero on $[1,\infty)$.  For fixed positive
constants $C_1,C_2,G_0$ it satisfies
\[
 -C_1\leq\phi'\leq0,\quad |\phi''|\leq C_2,\quad
 (\phi')^2\leq G_0\phi.
\]
The last inequality follows by differentiating the square of the cutoff.
Set
\[
 b(t)=e^{nKt}/R_*,\qquad
 \eta(t,y)=\phi\bigl(b(t)\min\{d_{g(t)}(x,y),R_*\}\bigr).
\]
Distances to other components are interpreted as infinite before taking
the minimum.  This capped distance is continuous.  The cutoff is one
at $x$, vanishes on $C\setminus\operatorname{int}C$, and takes values
in $[0,1]$.  Local length comparison on the retained balls gives, for
$s\leq t$,
\[
 \min\{d_{g(s)}(x,y),R_*\}
 \leq e^{nK(t-s)}\min\{d_{g(t)}(x,y),R_*\}.
\]
Thus $\eta(t,y)\leq\eta(s,y)$.

At a point where $\eta>0$, compose the upper distance support with
the decreasing function $\phi(b(t)\,\cdot)$.  It gives a spatial lower
support $e$ for $\eta(t,\cdot)$.  On the strict plateau use $e=1$.
On the transition, $1/2\leq b(t)d<1$, so the chain rule and
\eqref{eq:rf-shi-distance-support} give
\[
 |\nabla e|^2\leq G\eta,\qquad -\Delta e\leq L+\varepsilon,
\]
with constants, chosen before the flow and center,
\[
 B=e^{n\alpha}/R_*,\quad G=G_0B^2,\qquad
 L=(2nC_1+C_2)B^2+C_1nK.
\]
The distance-support error can be made arbitrarily small.  Keep $e$
constant in time.  Monotonicity of $\eta$ implies $e(y)\leq\eta(s,y)$
near the contact point for all earlier $s$, and $\partial_te=0$.
We therefore have the full past-time lower-support inequalities
needed in the maximum argument, even at the terminal time.

\emph{A cutoff maximum estimate.}
Suppose $Q\geq0$ is smooth on a compact carrier and
$(\partial_s-\kappa\Delta)Q\leq-cQ^2+d$ with $c>0$, $d\geq0$.
Suppose its cutoff has lower supports as above, with
$\kappa|\nabla e|^2\leq G\eta$ and
$(\partial_s-\kappa\Delta)e\leq L+\varepsilon$.
For $\tau=a+s\leq\Theta_0$, consider $W=\tau\eta Q$.
At an interior positive spatial maximum, replace $\eta$ by its
touching lower support.  Then $eQ$ still has a maximum there, so
$e\nabla Q+Q\nabla e=0$ and $\Delta(eQ)\leq0$.
Multiplying the time derivative bound by $\tau e$ and using these
two identities gives, up to the arbitrarily small support error,
\[
 \tau e\,\partial_s(\tau eQ)
 \leq eW-cW^2+d\Theta_0^2e^2+\tau(L+2G)W.
\]
Complete the square in the first two terms.  Since $0<e\leq1$, the
right side is negative when
\[
 W>\mathcal U(c,d,L,G,\Theta_0):=
 \max\left\{\frac1c,1,
   \frac{4\bigl(d\Theta_0^2+1/(4c)+\Theta_0(L+2G)\bigr)}c\right\}.
\]
Indeed, $W>1/c$ makes $W-e/(2c)>W/2$, while $W>1$ allows each
constant remainder to be bounded by that constant times $W$.
The past-time support comparison rules out crossing this threshold:
apply the compact minimum principle to
$\max\{I,\mathcal U\}-\tau\eta Q$, choosing the support error
arbitrarily small at each negative minimum.  Its initial value is
nonnegative if $a\eta(0)Q(0)\leq I$, and its boundary value is
nonnegative because $\eta$ vanishes there.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-shi-cutoff-bound}
 (a+s)\eta Q\leq\max\{I,\mathcal U(c,d,L,G,\Theta_0)\}.
\end{equation}

\emph{The product of successive energies.}
Fix a target time $\theta>0$, put $\rho=\sqrt\theta$, and keep $\rho$
constant while differentiating on $[\theta/2,\theta]$.  Write
$e_j=\max\{j-l,0\}$, $W_j=\rho^{2e_j}N_j^2$ and $\Theta=\alpha/K$.
Suppose inductively $\rho^{e_j}N_j\leq H$ for $j\leq m$, with
$m\geq l$.  The derivative inequality and Young's inequality give
\[
 \rho^2(\partial_t-\Delta)W_m\leq-2W_{m+1}+C_0,
 \qquad
 \rho^2(\partial_t-\Delta)W_{m+1}
      \leq-2W_{m+2}+\beta W_{m+1}+\gamma,
\]
where, for constants $a_j(n)\geq0$ depending only on dimension and order,
\[
 \Lambda=\Theta\max\{1,\Theta\}^{l},\quad
 C_0=\Lambda a_mH^3,\quad
 \beta=\Lambda a_{m+1}H+1,\quad
 \gamma=(\Lambda a_{m+1}H^2)^2/4.
\]
The scale factor is uniform: for $i+j=m$, the difference
$e_m-e_i-e_j$ lies between zero and $l$, so the extra factor
$\rho^{2+e_m-e_i-e_j}$ is at most $\Lambda$.
For the next-order reaction, the endpoint terms contain
$HN_{m+1}^2$ in scaled variables, and the remaining terms contain
$H^2N_{m+1}$; completing a square gives $\beta$ and $\gamma$.

Differentiating a tensor norm gives
$|\nabla N_j^2|^2\leq4N_j^2N_{j+1}^2$.  Cauchy--Schwarz and Young
therefore imply
\[
 -2\rho^2\langle\nabla W_m,\nabla W_{m+1}\rangle
 \leq W_{m+1}^2+16W_mW_{m+2}.
\]
Set $Q=(8H^2+1+W_m)W_{m+1}$ and $D=9H^2+1$.
In its heat inequality the coefficient of $W_{m+2}$ is at most
$-2(8H^2+1)+14W_m\leq0$.  The remaining terms are bounded by
\[
 -W_{m+1}^2+(C_0+\beta D)W_{m+1}+\gamma D
 \leq-\tfrac12 W_{m+1}^2+\tfrac12(C_0+\beta D)^2+\gamma D.
\]
Since $W_m\leq H^2$, we have $W_{m+1}\leq Q\leq DW_{m+1}$.
Thus
\[
 \theta(\partial_t-\Delta)Q\leq-cQ^2+d,
 \qquad c=1/(2D^2),\quad d=(C_0+\beta D)^2/2+\gamma D.
\]
Change time by $t=\theta/2+\theta s$, $0\leq s\leq1/2$.
The cutoff constants become at most $\Theta L,\Theta G$ and the
diffusion coefficient is $\kappa=\theta$.  Apply
\eqref{eq:rf-shi-cutoff-bound} with $a=I=0$, $\Theta_0=1/2$.
At the center and final time,
\[
 \theta^{e_{m+1}}N_{m+1}^2\leq Q
 \leq2\mathcal U(c,d,\Theta L,\Theta G,1/2).
\]
This produces the next derivative bound without initial control of
order $m+1$.

For $m+1\leq l$, use $\rho=1$ and unscaled energies on $[0,T]$.
The same product argument applies with reaction scale
$\max\{1,\Theta\}$.  Initially
$Q\leq(9H^2+1)K^2$.  Apply \eqref{eq:rf-shi-cutoff-bound} with
$a=1$, $I=(9H^2+1)K^2$ and $\Theta_0=1+\Theta$.
It gives a uniform bound for $Q$, including time zero.

Finally, for the desired order $k>0$, choose
$\delta=r/(2k)$ and initial radii $r_j=r-j\delta$, $0\leq j\leq k$.
At every point of $B_{g(0)}(p,r_{m+1})$, the compact carrier above lies
in all preceding balls $B_{g(0)}(p,r_j)$, $j\leq m$.
If their bounds are $N_j(s)\leq A_js^{-e_j/2}$, then on the half-time
interval $\rho^{e_j}N_j(s)\leq2^{e_j/2}A_j$.
Choose $H=1+\sum_{j\leq m}2^{e_j/2}A_j$ and apply the appropriate
step.  This constructs $A_{m+1}$ using only the previous constants and
$n,k,l,K,\alpha,r$.  The induction ends at $r_k=r/2$.
Order zero follows directly from $N_0=|\operatorname{Rm}|$;
in dimension zero every curvature derivative is zero.  Taking $l=0$
proves the first assertion.  The second follows from the stated initial
bounds, retaining its global hypotheses even though the induction
itself uses them only on the initial ball.
\end{proof}

Compare \cite[Theorems 3.28--3.29 and their proof, pp.~51--57]{MT2007}.
The product-energy argument follows that proof.  Here the scale is
fixed on each half-time interval, and the cutoff is built from evolving
distance supports on compact carriers.  This supplies the localization
without requiring the exponential map to be injective on the original
ball.

\begin{proposition}[Metric comparison]\label{prop:rf-metric-comparison}
If $s\leq t$ belong to the flow interval and
$|\operatorname{Rm}|(x,\tau)\leq K$ for all $x$ and $\tau\in[s,t]$, where
$K\geq0$, then
\[
 e^{-2nK(t-s)}g(s)\leq g(t)\leq e^{2nK(t-s)}g(s).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Contraction gives $|\operatorname{Ric}(v,v)|\leq nK g(v,v)$.
For a fixed nonzero tangent vector,
$|(\log g(\tau)(v,v))'|\leq2nK$.  Integrate from $s$ to $t$;
the zero vector case follows directly.  The same argument is local in
space if a curvature bound is known only along the fixed point.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Scalar lower barrier]\label{prop:rf-scalar}
Let $n>0$ and let $g(t)$ be a Ricci flow on a closed $n$-manifold on
$[a,b)$, where $a<b$.  If $r_0<0$ and $R(x,a)\geq r_0$ for all $x$, then
\[
 R(x,t)\geq \frac{r_0}{1-2r_0(t-a)/n}\quad(a\leq t<b).
\]
In dimension three, if $a\geq0$ and $R(x,a)\geq-6/(1+4a)$, then
$R(x,t)\geq-6/(1+4t)$ on $[a,b)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Cauchy--Schwarz for the Ricci eigenvalues gives
$|\operatorname{Ric}|^2\geq R^2/n$.  The displayed comparison function
$u$ solves $u'=2u^2/n$ and $u(a)=r_0$; its denominator stays positive.
On each compact subinterval,
\[
 (\partial_t-\Delta)(R-u)\geq \tfrac2n(R+u)(R-u).
\]
The coefficient is bounded there.  An exponential weight and a small
strict barrier rule out a first negative minimum, since the Laplacian
is nonnegative at that minimum.  Let the strict perturbation tend to
zero.  Every $t<b$ lies in such a subinterval.  Substitution of
$n=3$ and $r_0=-6/(1+4a)$ gives the last formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Positivity and rigidity]\label{prop:rf-positivity}
On a closed three-manifold, a Ricci flow on $[0,T]$, $T>0$, preserves
nonnegative sectional curvature and preserves nonnegative Ricci curvature.
On a connected three-manifold, a flow on $[0,T]$ with nonnegative
sectional curvature at every time and $R(p,T)=0$ has
$|\operatorname{Rm}|(x,t)=0$ for every $x$ and $0\leq t\leq T$.
The rigidity assertion requires no compactness or completeness hypothesis.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In an evolving orthonormal frame the curvature operator eigenvalues
$\lambda,\mu,\nu$ in dimension three have reaction
$2(\lambda^2+\mu\nu)$ and its cyclic permutations.
At a zero least eigenvalue with the other two nonnegative, its reaction
is nonnegative.  The compact tensor maximum principle therefore
preserves the cone of nonnegative curvature operators, which in
dimension three is the sectional-curvature cone.

For Ricci, suppose $\mu+\nu=0$ is the smallest Ricci eigenvalue.
Then $\lambda+\mu,\lambda+\nu\geq0$, and the reaction of $\mu+\nu$ is
$2(\mu^2+\nu^2+\lambda(\mu+\nu))=4\mu^2\geq0$.
The same cone argument applies.  For rigidity, the scalar evolution
makes $R\geq0$ a supersolution of the heat equation.  The local strong
maximum principle, propagated through connected coordinate
neighborhoods, makes it zero at every earlier positive time if it
vanishes at $(p,T)$.  Continuity includes both endpoints.  A
nonnegative curvature operator in dimension three has nonnegative
eigenvalues whose sum is $R/2$; they therefore all vanish.
\end{proof}
See \cite[Proposition 4.1, pp.~64--65; Corollaries 4.14--4.15,
pp.~69--70; Theorem 4.18, pp.~71--72]{MT2007}.

\section{The Hamilton--Ivey invariant region}
\label{sec:rf-pinching}

In dimension three put
\[
 k_{\min}(x,t)=\inf\{\operatorname{Rm}(u,v,u,v):
                   u,v\text{ orthonormal at }(x,t)\},\qquad
 X(x,t)=\max\{-k_{\min}(x,t),0\}.
\]
The three eigenvalues $k_1\geq k_2\geq k_3$ of the curvature operator obey
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-spectrum}
 k_{\min}=k_3,\qquad R=2(k_1+k_2+k_3),\qquad
 |\operatorname{Rm}|^2=4(k_1^2+k_2^2+k_3^2).
\end{equation}
One obtains this operator without choosing an orientation by using
$\tfrac12R\,g-\operatorname{Ric}$ on the tangent bundle; in an oriented
orthonormal frame the Hodge identification with $\Lambda^2$ gives the
same spectrum.  Changing orientation does not change these identities.

\begin{definition}\label{def:rf-pinched}
A slice is Hamilton--Ivey pinched at absolute time $t$ if $t\geq0$,
$R\geq-6/(1+4t)$, and, at every point with $X>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-log-pinching}
                  R\geq2X\{\log X+\log(1+t)-3\}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:rf-pinching}
Let $0\leq a<b$ and let $g(t)$ be a Ricci flow on a closed
three-manifold on $[a,b)$.  If its initial slice is pinched at time $a$,
then every slice is pinched at its own time $t$.
At each point where $R\leq R_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-full-bound}
             |\operatorname{Rm}|\leq13\max\{R_0,e^4\}.
\end{equation}
In particular normalized initial data satisfy the initial pinching
condition at time zero.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Convexity and the reaction calculation}

Write $S=R/2$ and $f_t(X)=X(\log X+\log(1+t)-3)$, continuously extended
by $f_t(0)=0$.  There is a unique $b_*>e^2$ with
$b_*(\log b_*-3)=-3$: strict convexity, the values at $1,e^2,e^3$,
and monotonicity beyond $e^2$ prove this.  Set $b(t)=b_* /(1+t)$.
The convex region used in the tensor argument is
\[
 \mathcal C_t=\{B=B^*:\
       \operatorname{tr}B\geq f_t(\max\{b(t),X(B)\})\},\qquad
 X(B)=\max\{-\lambda_{\min}(B),0\}.
\]
The clipped function is constant $-3/(1+t)$ below $b(t)$ and convex
increasing above it.  Since $\lambda_{\min}$ is the infimum of the
linear Rayleigh functionals, $X(B)$ is convex.  Composition with the
increasing convex clipped function proves convexity of $\mathcal C_t$.
It is closed and orthogonally invariant.

For an ordered spectrum, membership implies $S\geq f_t(X)$ even in the
clipped range.  If $0<X\leq1/(1+t)$, then $f_t(X)\leq-3X\leq S$.
Between $1/(1+t)$ and $b(t)$, convexity puts $f_t(X)$ below the
horizontal level $-3/(1+t)$.  Beyond $b(t)$ the inequality is part of
the definition.  Conversely the stronger scalar bound
$S\geq-3/(1+4t)$ together with \eqref{eq:rf-log-pinching} implies
membership for $t\geq0$.

The key algebra can be checked without differentiating eigenvectors.
On a reaction trajectory diagonalized initially, the fixed eigenbasis
remains diagonal.  Write its ordered entries as
$\lambda\geq\mu\geq\nu$, put $X=-\nu>0$, $Y=-\mu$, and first use the
reaction with rate one.  Then
\[
 S'=X^2+Y^2+\lambda^2+XY-\lambda(X+Y),\qquad
 X'=-X^2+\lambda Y.
\]
Direct expansion gives
\[
 XS'-(S+X)X'=X^3+
             XY^2+\lambda Y(Y-X)+\lambda^2(X-Y).
\]
The last three terms are nonnegative.  If $Y\leq0$, each term can be
written as a product of nonnegative factors, since $\lambda\geq-Y$.
If $Y\geq0$, use the identity
\[
 XY^2+\lambda Y(Y-X)+\lambda^2(X-Y)
 =Y^3+(X-Y)\{(\lambda-Y/2)^2+3Y^2/4\}.
\]
It follows that $(S/X-\log X)'\geq X$ for rate one, and at least $2X$
for the geometric reaction of rate two.  On the increasing logarithmic
boundary $X\geq b(t)>1/(1+t)$, this exceeds the derivative of
$\log(1+t)-3$.  On the horizontal boundary,
$S'=2(\lambda^2+\mu^2+\nu^2+\lambda\mu+\mu\nu+\nu\lambda)
\geq4S^2/3$, so the vector field also points inward.
Repeated eigenvalues are handled by the symmetric polynomial equation
or supporting functionals, without requiring a smooth ordered
eigenbasis.  This proves reaction invariance.

\subsection{From the reaction to the geometric equation}

Transport $\tfrac12R\,g-\operatorname{Ric}$ to a fixed initial Euclidean
bundle using the time-dependent isometries induced by the metric
equation.  Explicitly, at each point solve
$\partial_tP_t=\operatorname{Ric}_{g(t)}^{\sharp}P_t$, $P_a=I$.
Differentiating $g(t)(P_tv,P_tw)$ gives zero: the two input
derivatives cancel $\partial_tg=-2\operatorname{Ric}$.  Thus $P_t$
is an isometry from the initial fiber to the time-$t$ fiber.
Denote the pulled-back endomorphism by $B$.  The moving-frame
evolution has reaction $2(B^2+\operatorname{adj}B)$, with the transported
rough Laplacian as spatial part.  Scaling $U=(1+t)B$ replaces
$\mathcal C_t$ by the fixed set $\mathcal C_0$ and gives
\[
 (\partial_t-\Delta)U
       =(1+t)^{-1}\{U+2(U^2+\operatorname{adj}U)\}.
\]
For every outward supporting functional $\ell$ at $A\in\mathcal C_0$,
reaction invariance implies that $\ell$ of this right side is
nonpositive at $A$.  Indeed start the finite-dimensional reaction
curve at $A$, rescale it to stay in $\mathcal C_0$, apply $\ell$,
and differentiate the resulting one-sided maximum at zero.

Here $\Delta U$ denotes the pullback of the geometric rough
Laplacian, with the same spatially constant factor $1+t$.
Its contact sign requires a spatial argument.  Fix a time and a
point $p$ at which the distance of a smooth tensor $T(x)$ to the
fiberwise carrier $K_x$ has a local maximum.  Choose an active unit
support $(A,N)$ at $p$, meaning
\[
 A\in K_p,\qquad |N|=1,\qquad
 \langle N,Z-A\rangle\leq0\quad(Z\in K_p),\qquad
 \langle N,T(p)-A\rangle=\operatorname{dist}(T(p),K_p).
\]
Extend an orthonormal basis at $p$ by parallel transport along
coordinate rays in a small centered chart.  In coordinates each
extended vector $Y$ solves
\[
 \frac{d}{ds}Y(sz)=-\Gamma(sz)(z,Y(sz)),\qquad Y(0)=v.
\]
Smooth dependence for this linear ODE gives smooth local fields.
Uniqueness identifies the endpoint field on every ray with the
parallel solution along that ray.  At the center this proves
$\nabla_wY=0$ for every $w$; differentiating the same ray equation
once more gives $\nabla_w\nabla_wY=0$ there, where the direction
is extended with constant coordinate components.  The correction
$\nabla_{\nabla_ww}Y$ also vanishes because all first derivatives
vanish.  Only these diagonal second derivatives enter the rough
Laplacian.

Metric compatibility makes the resulting maps $Q_x:T_pM\to T_xM$
isometries.  They preserve $K$, because its definition is invariant
under orthogonal changes of frame.  Write $Q_x$ also for the induced
tensor isometry and set $f(x)=\langle Q_xN,T(x)\rangle$.
The support inequality and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 f(x)-\langle N,A\rangle
   =\langle Q_xN,T(x)-Q_xA\rangle
   \leq\operatorname{dist}(T(x),K_x).
\]
There is equality at $p$, so $f$ has a local maximum at $p$.
Expand $f$ as a finite sum of evaluations of $T$ on the transported
basis fields.  The first and diagonal second derivative identities
remove every input-field term in its Laplacian.  Consequently
\[
 \langle N,\Delta T(p)\rangle=\Delta f(p)\leq0.
\]
Apply this to $(1+t)(\tfrac12Rg-\operatorname{Ric})$ on the
time-$t$ slice.  Isometric pullback by $P_t$ preserves distances,
active supports and this pairing, yielding the required sign for
the diffusion term of $U$.  This transfer uses the slice calculation;
it does not differentiate $P_t$ in space.

To finish comparison on a closed time slab, choose finitely many
compact coordinate neighborhoods with local isometries preserving
the carrier.  Continuity bounds $|U|$ by some $H$ on their union.
Transporting one carrier point from each coordinate fiber also
gives carrier points of uniformly bounded norm, say at most $J$.
The nearest carrier point to $U$ then has norm at most $2H+J$:
its distance from $U$ is at most $H+J$.  Therefore all positive
distances are realized by supports $(A,N)$ with
$|A|\leq2H+J$ and $|N|=1$.  In each coordinate fiber these supports
form a closed bounded set.  The finite union over the compact
neighborhoods is a compact parameter space for the functions
\[
 F_{x,A,N}(t)=\langle N,U(x,t)-A\rangle.
\]
A positive maximum among these functions equals the maximum
distance to the carrier.  At such a maximum the diffusion pairing
is nonpositive by the preceding argument.  If $\psi_t$ denotes
the scaled reaction and $D$ is its uniform Lipschitz constant on
a ball containing both $U$ and its nearest carrier point, inwardness
gives the following estimate.  Put $A_*=\operatorname{proj}_{K_x}U$.
Activity and the support inequality imply
$\langle N,A_*-A\rangle=0$: indeed
$\langle N,U-A_*\rangle\leq|U-A_*|$
and $\langle N,A_*-A\rangle\leq0$, while their sum is exactly
$|U-A_*|$.  Thus $N$ also supports the carrier at $A_*$, and
\[
 \frac{d}{dt}F_{x,A,N}(t)
 \leq\langle N,\psi_t(U)-\psi_t(A_*)\rangle
 \leq D F_{x,A,N}(t).
\]
The last inequality uses $|U-A_*|=F_{x,A,N}(t)$.
Were any $F$ positive, the continuous compact family
$e^{-(D+1)(t-a)}F(t)$ would attain a positive spacetime maximum
at a time greater than $a$.  Its time derivative would be
nonnegative there, including the left derivative at the slab's
right endpoint, whereas the displayed inequality makes it strictly
negative.  This contradiction proves $U\in\mathcal C_0$ without
differentiating the maximum-distance function.
This is the tensor maximum principle of
\cite[Theorem 4.8, p.~66]{MT2007}, applied to the specific carrier just
constructed; the ODE alone would not establish the spatial assertion.
The scalar barrier in Proposition~\ref{prop:rf-scalar} supplies the
stronger scalar component of Definition~\ref{def:rf-pinched}.
Apply the argument on each $[a,t]$ with $t<b$.  The algebra and carrier
are those of \cite[Claims 4.28--4.31, pp.~77--79; Theorem 4.32,
pp.~79--80]{MT2007}.

To prove \eqref{eq:rf-full-bound}, let $K=\max\{R_0,e^4\}$.
If $X>e^4$, the logarithmic inequality and $t\geq0$ imply
$R\geq2X$, hence $X\leq K$; if $X\leq e^4$ this is immediate.
Thus $k_i\geq-K$, while $k_1+k_2+k_3=R/2\leq K/2$ gives
$k_i\leq5K/2\leq3K$.  Formula~\eqref{eq:rf-spectrum} gives
$|\operatorname{Rm}|^2\leq108K^2\leq169K^2$, proving the stated
continuous bound.  Finally, $|\operatorname{Rm}|\leq1$ implies
$X\leq1$ and $R\geq-6$.  For $X>0$, $R/2\geq-3X$ and
$\log X\leq0$, giving the time-zero logarithmic inequality.

\section{Differential Harnack inequalities}
\label{sec:rf-harnack}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:rf-harnack}
Let $g(t)$ be a Ricci flow on an $n$-manifold for $T_0<t<T_1$.
Assume every slice is complete, its curvature operator is nonnegative,
and it is bounded on that slice: for every $t$ there is $K_t\geq0$
bounding its operator norm at every point.  Then for every $(x,t)$
and $v\in T_xM$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-harnack}
 \partial_tR+\frac{R}{t-T_0}+2\,dR(v)+2\operatorname{Ric}(v,v)\geq0.
\end{equation}
If $T_0<t_1<t_2<T_1$ and $\gamma:[t_1,t_2]\to M$ is $C^1$, put
$E(\gamma)=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}|\gamma'(s)|_{g(s)}^2\,ds$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-harnack-path}
 R(\gamma(t_2),t_2)(t_2-T_0)\geq
 R(\gamma(t_1),t_1)(t_1-T_0)e^{-E(\gamma)/2}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The Hamilton block and its scalar trace}

For elapsed time $\tau>0$, define tensors
\[
\begin{split}
 P_{ijk}&=\nabla_i\operatorname{Ric}_{jk}
                         -\nabla_j\operatorname{Ric}_{ik},\\
 M_{ij}&=\Delta\operatorname{Ric}_{ij}
       -\tfrac12\nabla_i\nabla_jR
       +2\sum_{a,b}\operatorname{Rm}_{aibj}\operatorname{Ric}_{ab}
       -\sum_a\operatorname{Ric}_{ia}\operatorname{Ric}_{aj}
       +\frac{\operatorname{Ric}_{ij}}{2\tau}.
\end{split}
\]
They form a quadratic form on $\Lambda^2 TM\oplus TM$ with curvature
block $\operatorname{Rm}$, off-diagonal block $P$, and vector block
$M$.  With the two-form convention fixed by decomposable forms, its
value at $(v\wedge w,w)$ is
$\operatorname{Rm}(v,w,v,w)+2P(v,w,w)+M(w,w)$.
Hamilton block positivity says this full quadratic form is nonnegative.

The contracted Bianchi identity and the scalar evolution give
\[
 \sum_iP(v,e_i,e_i)=\tfrac12dR(v),\qquad
 \operatorname{tr}M=\tfrac12\Delta R+|\operatorname{Ric}|^2
                                      +R/(2\tau).
\]
Sum twice the block inequality at $(v\wedge e_i,e_i)$ over $i$.
Since $\sum_i\operatorname{Rm}(v,e_i,v,e_i)=\operatorname{Ric}(v,v)$,
the result is exactly \eqref{eq:rf-harnack}.

\subsection{Reaction, contact, and the noncompact barrier}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:rf-bounded-harnack}
Let $g(t)$, $a<t<b$, be a Ricci flow with complete slices and
nonnegative curvature operator.  Suppose there is one $K\geq0$ such
that $|\operatorname{Rm}|(x,t)\leq K$ for all $x$ and $a<t<b$.
Then its Hamilton block with elapsed time $t-a$ is positive semidefinite.
In particular \eqref{eq:rf-harnack} holds with $T_0=a$.
For a complete ancient flow with nonnegative curvature operator and
one full-curvature bound on its whole time range, sending $a$ to
$-\infty$ gives
$\partial_tR+2dR(v)+2\operatorname{Ric}(v,v)\geq0$.
Neither conclusion uses an asymptotic-volume-ratio theorem.
\end{proposition}

We prove block positivity first on a complete connected slab with a
uniform bound on full curvature.  Buffering the left endpoint and
applying the derivative estimates bounds curvature and its first two
derivatives by some $K$.  Shift the buffered initial time to zero, and
write $0<\tau\leq T$ for elapsed time.  All the constants in this
argument may depend on $n,K,T$.

The finite-dimensional positivity mechanism can be displayed explicitly.
In an orthonormal frame use ordered skew coefficients $U_{ab}=-U_{ba}$
and vector coefficients $W_a$, and form
$\mathsf A=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\operatorname{Rm}&P\\P^t&M\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
on the index set $(I\times I)\sqcup I$.
If $\mathsf A$ is positive semidefinite, its tensor square is too:
writing $A_{ij}=\langle a_i,a_j\rangle$ shows directly that
\[
 \sum_{i,k,j,l}K_{ik}A_{ij}A_{kl}K_{jl}
            =\left|\sum_{i,k}K_{ik}a_i\otimes a_k\right|^2\geq0.
\]
Take the four blocks of $K$ to be
\[
 K_{(a,c),(b,d)}=-2U_{ab}\delta_{cd},\quad
 K_{(a,c),d}=W_a\delta_{cd},\quad
 K_{c,(b,d)}=-W_b\delta_{cd},\quad K_{c,d}=0.
\]
Expanding the contraction and using the pair symmetry of curvature and
the skew symmetries of $P,U$ gives the nonnegative sharp reaction
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-harnack-sharp}
\begin{split}
 &2\sum_{a,c,b,d}R_{acbd}M_{cd}W_aW_b
 -2\sum_{a,c,d,b}P_{acd}P_{bdc}W_aW_b\\
 &\quad+8\sum_{a,c,b,d,e}R_{acde}P_{cbe}U_{ab}W_d
 +4\sum_{a,c,b,d,e,f}R_{acde}R_{bcfe}U_{ab}U_{df}\geq0.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Here $R_{acbd}$ denotes curvature components, not scalar curvature.
This identifies the sign of the mixed reaction; separate signs for
its four displayed summands are neither needed nor generally available.

The geometric contact calculation also involves derivatives of the
test fields.  We first make its spatial minimum test explicit.
Use the radial metric-preserving frame constructed in the pinching
argument; its first and diagonal second covariant jets vanish at the
contact point.  Let $\mathsf A(x)$ be the tensor block in this frame,
and let $z=(U,W)$ be null.  Positivity implies $\mathsf A z=0$:
the polynomial $\langle\mathsf A(z+sw),z+sw\rangle$ is nonnegative
for both signs of $s$ and has zero constant term, so its linear
coefficient vanishes for every $w$.

Choose smooth coefficient functions with value $z$ and prescribed
gradients $Z_e$.  They can be chosen affine in a coordinate chart;
their second derivatives need not vanish.  The local scalar minimum of
$\langle\mathsf A(x)z(x),z(x)\rangle$ gives
\[
 0\leq\langle(\Delta\mathsf A)z,z\rangle
       +4\sum_e\langle(\nabla_e\mathsf A)z,Z_e\rangle
       +2\sum_e\langle\mathsf A Z_e,Z_e\rangle.
\]
The terms containing $\Delta z$ vanish because $\mathsf A z=0$.
The frame jets identify these derivatives of matrix entries with
the covariant derivatives of the original tensors.
Set $Z_e=(V_e,0)$ and prescribe the skew first jet
\[
 V_{eac}=\nabla_eU_{ac}=\frac12\left[
 \left(\operatorname{Ric}_{ea}+\frac{\delta_{ea}}{2\tau}\right)W_c
 -W_a\left(\operatorname{Ric}_{ec}+\frac{\delta_{ec}}{2\tau}\right)\right].
\]
Antisymmetrizing the affine coefficient functions realizes this jet
while retaining $U_{ac}=-U_{ca}$ in the neighborhood.

Here is the cancellation with the evolution equations.  In the next
formulas repeated indices are summed in the orthonormal frame.  Put
$k=(2\tau)^{-1}$ and use the covariant heat operator
\[
 \mathcal HT=\partial_\tau T-\Delta T+
       \sum_{\text{covariant slots}}T(\ldots,
                         \operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}(\cdot),\ldots).
\]
Contracted differential Bianchi and the Ricci commutator give
\[
 \nabla_eR_{ebcd}=P_{cdb},\qquad
 \nabla_eP_{ebd}=M_{bd}-R_{abcd}\operatorname{Ric}_{ac}
                             -k\operatorname{Ric}_{bd}.
\]
For the second identity, expand the divergence of
$P_{ebd}=\nabla_e\operatorname{Ric}_{bd}-\nabla_b\operatorname{Ric}_{ed}$.
Commuting the two derivatives in the second term gives
$\tfrac12\nabla_b\nabla_d R+\operatorname{Ric}_{ba}\operatorname{Ric}_{ad}
-R_{abcd}\operatorname{Ric}_{ac}$; comparison with the definition of
$M$ gives the displayed result.

For reference, the evolution equations used in the contraction are
\[
\begin{split}
 (\mathcal HR)_{abcd}
 &=2(B_{abcd}-B_{abdc}-B_{adbc}+B_{acbd}),\\
 B_{abcd}&=\sum_{e,f}R_{aebf}R_{cedf},\\
 (\mathcal HP)_{abc}
 &=2(R_{adbe}P_{dec}+R_{adce}P_{dbe}+R_{bdce}P_{ade})
       -2\operatorname{Ric}_{de}\nabla_dR_{abce},\\
 (\mathcal HM)_{ab}
 &=2R_{acbd}M_{cd}
   +2\operatorname{Ric}_{cd}(\nabla_cP_{dab}+\nabla_cP_{dba})\\
 &\quad+P_{cda}P_{cdb}-2P_{acd}P_{bdc}
   +2\operatorname{Ric}_{ec}\operatorname{Ric}_{ed}R_{adbc}
   -\operatorname{Ric}_{ab}/(2\tau^2).
\end{split}
\]
They follow by differentiating $P=\nabla\operatorname{Ric}
-(\nabla\operatorname{Ric})^{(12)}$ and the divergence formula
for $M$.  In that differentiation the connection varies by
$\partial_\tau\Gamma^k_{ij}
=-g^{k\ell}(\nabla_i\operatorname{Ric}_{j\ell}
+\nabla_j\operatorname{Ric}_{i\ell}-\nabla_\ell\operatorname{Ric}_{ij})$.
Retaining this term in every covariant input and commuting the rough
Laplacian with one derivative produces the displayed curvature and
derivative contractions; differentiating $1/(2\tau)$ gives the last
term.  These are the tensor identities in
\cite[Lemmas 15.18--15.19, equations (15.45)--(15.47),
pp.~273--277]{ChowEtAl2008}.

Skew symmetry removes the half in each contraction with $V_e$.
For example,
\[
\begin{split}
 (\nabla_eP_{abc})V_{eab}W_c
 &= (\nabla_eP_{abc})\operatorname{Ric}_{ea}W_bW_c
      +k(\nabla_eP_{ebc})W_bW_c,\\
 R_{abcd}V_{eab}V_{ecd}
 &=R_{abcd}\operatorname{Ric}_{ea}W_b\operatorname{Ric}_{ec}W_d
   +2kR_{abcd}\operatorname{Ric}_{ac}W_bW_d
   +k^2\operatorname{Ric}_{bd}W_bW_d.
\end{split}
\]
The analogous contraction of $\nabla_eR_{abcd}$ has a
Ricci-weighted derivative term and $kP_{cdb}W_bU_{cd}$.
Pair interchange and skew symmetry cancel its Ricci-weighted part
against the curvature derivative in $\mathcal HP$.
The two divergence identities then cancel the remaining derivatives,
the Ricci-square contractions and the reciprocal-time terms in
$\mathcal HM$.  Explicitly, define the heat contraction after
subtracting the spatial first-jet terms by
\[
\begin{split}
 \mathcal J={}&(\mathcal HM)_{ab}W_aW_b
       +2(\mathcal HP)_{abc}U_{ab}W_c
       +(\mathcal HR)_{abcd}U_{ab}U_{cd}\\
 &-4(\nabla_eP_{abc})V_{eab}W_c
  -4(\nabla_eR_{abcd})V_{eab}U_{cd}
  -2R_{abcd}V_{eab}V_{ecd}.
\end{split}
\]
Substitution in the curvature, Ricci and connection evolution equations
gives the identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-harnack-jet-reaction}
 \mathcal J+\frac2\tau(M_{ab}W_aW_b+P_{abc}U_{ab}W_c)
 =\mathcal S+\sum_{a,b}
       \left(\sum_cP_{abc}W_c+\sum_{c,d}R_{abcd}U_{cd}\right)^2,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal S$ is the sharp reaction in
\eqref{eq:rf-harnack-sharp}.  To check the algebraic collection,
the vector-block evolution supplies the square of $PW$, and first
Bianchi gives
$2\sum_{d,e}R_{adbe}U_{de}=\sum_{d,e}R_{abde}U_{de}$.
Thus the curvature reaction supplies the square of $RU$ and the
mixed reaction supplies $2\langle PW,RU\rangle$; the remaining
terms are precisely $\mathcal S$.

The vector row of $\mathsf A z=0$ implies
$M_{ab}W_aW_b+P_{abc}U_{ab}W_c=0$.  Hence $\mathcal J\geq0$.
The spatial minimum inequality above and the definition of $\mathcal H$
give $\partial_\tau Q(U,W)\geq\mathcal J$ at contact.
Indeed the Ricci action is an input variation of the full quadratic,
so its contraction is zero when $\mathsf A z=0$.
The same observation removes derivatives of time-dependent test
directions: $2\langle\mathsf A z,z'\rangle=0$.
This relates the covariant calculation to an ordinary time derivative
on fixed tangent inputs.

To make contact strict, replace the curvature block by
$\operatorname{Rm}+\psi I_{\Lambda^2}$ and the vector block by
$M+\alpha g$, keeping $P$ fixed.  Ordered skew coefficients are normalized
so these additions contribute $\psi\sum U_{ab}^2+\alpha\sum W_a^2$.
At a null direction of this perturbed block, assumed nonnegative in a
spatial neighborhood, its vector row instead gives
$M_{ab}W_aW_b+P_{abc}U_{ab}W_c=-\alpha|W|^2$.
Consequently \eqref{eq:rf-harnack-jet-reaction} supplies the favorable
term $2\alpha|W|^2/\tau$.  Metric compatibility gives
$\Delta(\alpha g)=(\Delta\alpha)g$, while the metric identity on
two-forms is covariantly parallel.  The ordinary time derivatives of
these metric tensors cancel their Ricci input actions.  Estimating
the difference between the original reaction and that of the positive
perturbed block, the spatial minimum test therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-harnack-contact}
\begin{split}
 \partial_\tau Q_{\alpha,\psi}(U,W)\geq{}&
 \left(\partial_\tau\alpha-\Delta\alpha+
 \frac{2\alpha}{\tau}-C\alpha-\frac{C\psi}{\tau^2}\right)\sum_aW_a^2\\
 &+(\psi'-C\psi)\sum_{a,b}U_{ab}^2.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
It suffices that $\alpha\geq0$ and $0\leq\psi\leq1$.
To see the form of the errors, replacing $R$ by $R+\psi I$
and $M$ by $M+\alpha g$ changes each reaction monomial by
a term containing $\alpha$ or $\psi$.  Use $\psi^2\leq\psi$
and $\alpha\psi\leq\alpha$.  The mixed terms are bounded by
Young's inequality and
$\sum|U_{ab}|\leq n|U|$, $\sum|W_a|\leq\sqrt n|W|$.
The vector-block bound has the form
$|M_{ab}|\leq C_n(K+K^2+K/\tau)$.
The extra spatial identity term is bounded by
$C_n\psi\sum_e|V_e|^2
\leq C_n\psi(K+1/\tau)^2|W|^2$.
Since $\tau\leq T$, all these errors are absorbed by
$C\alpha|W|^2+C\psi|W|^2/\tau^2+C\psi|U|^2$.
For the numerical bound, put $k=(2\tau)^{-1}$ and
\[
 K_P=2nK,\qquad
 K_M=2n^2K+3n^3K^2+\frac{nK}{2\tau}.
\]
These bound the individual entries of $P$ and $M$ by contraction
of the common curvature-derivative bound.  Expanding the reaction
replacement and applying the preceding finite-sum estimates bounds
its loss by
\[
\begin{split}
 &2n^3(K+1)\alpha|W|^2
 +(2n^3K_M+6n^4K_P^2)\psi|W|^2\\
 &\hspace{15mm}+[6n^3+8n^4(2K+1)]\psi|U|^2.
\end{split}
\]
The prescribed spatial jet contributes at most
$(4n^4K^2+4nk^2)\psi|W|^2$.  Thus the total coefficient of
$\psi|W|^2$ is
\[
 D(\tau)=4n^5K+30n^6K^2+4n^4K^2+
                   \frac{n^4K}{\tau}+\frac n{\tau^2}.
\]
Multiplying by $\tau^2$ and using $\tau\leq T$ bounds it by
the corresponding expression with $T^2,T,1$.  Consequently one
admissible error constant is
\[
\begin{split}
 C={}&1+2n^3(K+1)+6n^3+8n^4(2K+1)\\
 &+(4n^5K+30n^6K^2+4n^4K^2)T^2+n^4KT+n.
\end{split}
\]
The reciprocal first power comes from the bound on $M$; the
prescribed jet contributes the reciprocal square.  No upper bound
on $\alpha$ is required: the replacement contains no $\alpha^2$
term, and $\alpha\psi\leq\alpha$ uses only $\psi\leq1$.
The spatial minimum contributes
$-\Delta\alpha$, and differentiating the vector perturbation and its
time correction leaves $\alpha_\tau+2\alpha/\tau$.

A proper smooth function $\rho$ can be chosen, on this buffered slab,
with
\[
 1+d_{g(\tau)}(O,x)\leq\rho(x)\leq B(1+d_{g(\tau)}(O,x)),\quad
 |d\rho|\leq B,\quad \nabla^2\rho\leq Bg(\tau).
\]
Here the initial smoothing is constructed on one complete slice with
bounded curvature.  We give the construction since the uniformity of
its constants is needed on the noncompact manifold.  In the next
paragraphs the metric is fixed, $|\sec|\leq K_0$, $K_0>0$, and
$n\geq2$.  Smooth the distance function to obtain $u$ with
$|u-d(O,\cdot)|\leq1$ and $|du|\leq2$.  Such first-order smoothing
uses convolution on locally finite coordinate balls: choose the
convolution errors so that the sum of their products with the
partition derivatives is less than the available gradient margin.
The Lipschitz constant of distance is one, so this leaves the stated
bound two (\cite[Proposition 26.49, equation (26.131), p.~378]{ChowEtAl2010}).

Let $H(x,y,t)$ be the increasing limit of the Dirichlet heat kernels
on a nested exhaustion by connected relatively compact open domains
with smooth boundary.  The uniformity in this construction can be
seen directly from its spectral response.  On a domain $\Omega$,
the coercive form
$\int_\Omega(uv+\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle)$ on
$H^1_0(\Omega)$ gives the inverse of $I-\Delta_D$ by Riesz
representation.  Rellich compactness makes this inverse compact
on $L^2(\Omega)$; it is positive, injective and self-adjoint.
For the compact inclusion, see
\cite[Section 5.7, Theorem 1 and the zero-boundary remark,
pp.~286, 289]{Evans2010}; a finite partition of unity transfers
the Euclidean result to the fixed smooth metric.
Its spectral basis defines the nonnegative Dirichlet operator
$A_\Omega=-\Delta_D$ and $P_t^\Omega=e^{-tA_\Omega}$.
The elementary inequality $s^je^{-s}\leq j!$ gives
\[
 \|A_\Omega^jP_t^\Omega f\|_2\leq j!t^{-j}\|f\|_2.
\]
Interior elliptic regularity and Sobolev evaluation turn these
spectral bounds into actual spatial derivatives.  More precisely,
fix a compact set $K$ inside a relatively compact open coordinate
region $V$, with positive interior buffer, and fix derivative order
$m$.  There are an integer $q$ and
$C$ depending only on these regions and the fixed smooth metric such
that, whenever $\Omega$ contains $V$,
\[
 \sup_K|D^mP_t^\Omega f|
 \leq C\sum_{j=0}^q\|A_\Omega^jP_t^\Omega f\|_2
 \leq C\sum_{j=0}^q j!t^{-j}\|f\|_2.
\]
Successive local elliptic estimates on nested regions give the first
inequality; their constants are chosen before the containing domain
(\cite[Section 6.3.1, Theorems 1--2, pp.~327, 332--333]{Evans2010}).
Boundary regularity on each smooth domain supplies the continuous
zero-boundary realizations needed for comparison.

For $a>0$, evaluation $f\mapsto P_a^\Omega f(x)$ is a bounded
linear functional on $L^2(\Omega)$.  Let $r_a^\Omega(x)$ be its
Riesz vector.  The same estimate for derivatives, tested against
arbitrary $f$, bounds all spatial derivatives of this vector in
$L^2$.  Self-adjointness and the semigroup identity give the kernel
factorization
\[
 H_\Omega(x,y,t)=
 \bigl\langle r_a^\Omega(x),
      P_{t-2a}^\Omega r_a^\Omega(y)\bigr\rangle_{L^2(\Omega)}
                     \qquad(t>2a).
\]
For $t\geq3a$, the preceding operator bounds control every time
derivative of the middle factor, while the row bounds control all
spatial derivatives.  The product rule thus bounds all joint kernel
jets on fixed compact positive-time cylinders, independently of
the containing domain.  Comparison gives positivity, domain
monotonicity and mass at most one.  Smooth compactness now applies
locally to the exhaustion kernels; monotonicity identifies every
subsequential limit with their supremum $H$, so that supremum is
smooth and satisfies the heat equation.  This construction uses no
uniform eigenvalue-counting estimate.

No mass is lost at infinity: a radial cutoff equal to one
at $x$, with values in $[0,1]$, has lower support Laplacians bounded
below by
\[
 -E_R,\qquad E_R=\frac{B_1}{R^2}
       +\frac{C_1}{R}\left(\frac{2(n-1)}R
                                      +(n-1)\sqrt{K_0}\right).
\]
This follows from Laplacian comparison for distance; its upper
supports become lower supports after the decreasing cutoff is
applied.  Comparison with the heat evolution on a domain containing
the cutoff support gives $1-E_Rt\leq\int H(x,y,t)\,d\mu(y)$.
Letting $R\to\infty$ proves $\int H(x,y,t)\,d\mu(y)=1$.

The needed first moment is uniform over $x$.  The static heat-kernel
Harnack inequality and a weighted energy estimate give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-heat-gaussian}
 H(x,y,t)\leq\frac{C}{\operatorname{Vol}B(x,\sqrt t)}
                    \exp\left(-\frac{d(x,y)^2}{192t}\right),
 \qquad 0<t\leq1,
\end{equation}
with $C=C(n,K_0)$.  To see the mechanism, for a smooth weight
$|\nabla\psi|\leq L$, integration by parts gives
\[
 \frac{d}{dt}\|e^\psi P_t f\|_2^2
 \leq2L^2\|e^\psi P_t f\|_2^2.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz applied to indicator functions gives the two-set
bound $\sqrt{\operatorname{Vol}A\operatorname{Vol}B}\,
e^{L^2t+a-b}$ when $\psi\leq a$ on $A$ and $\psi\geq b$ on $B$
(use the weight $-\psi$ in the operator estimate).
Smooth distance weights and optimization in $L$ give, for balls of
radius $\sqrt t$, an integral bound at time $3t$ with factor
$\exp[-(d(x,y)-2\sqrt t)_+^2/(48t)]$.
Apply the static Harnack inequality successively in the two kernel
variables to compare the kernel at time $t$ with this average.
Relative volume comparison absorbs the second ball-volume factor
and the remaining linear distance exponential into a weaker
Gaussian.  This gives~\eqref{eq:rf-heat-gaussian}.
The weighted estimate passes from the domain kernels to their
monotone limit.  The Harnack step uses cutoffs inside the domains
before taking that limit, as follows.

For a positive heat solution $h$, put $f=\log h$,
$w=|\nabla f|^2$ and $q=w-2f_t=-2\Delta f-w$.
The identities $f_t=\Delta f+w$ and Bochner's formula give, when
$\operatorname{Ric}\geq-kg$,
\[
 (\partial_t-\Delta)q
 \leq2\langle\nabla f,\nabla q\rangle
                          -\frac{2}{n}(\Delta f)^2+2kw.
\]
Choose a compact cutoff $0\leq\eta\leq1$ with
$|\nabla\eta|^2\leq A\eta$ and $\Delta\eta\geq-B$.
At a positive spatial maximum of $\eta q$, the identities
$\eta\nabla q=-q\nabla\eta$ and $\Delta(\eta q)\leq0$ imply
\[
 \eta^2q_t\leq(B+(n+2)A)(\eta q)
                  -\frac{(\eta q)^2}{2n}+2nk^2.
\]
Indeed weighted Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$-2\langle\nabla\eta,\nabla f\rangle\leq\eta w/n+nA$.
Its mixed term $(\eta q)(\eta w)/n$ cancels the mixed term in
$-(\eta q+\eta w)^2/(2n)$, and completing the remaining square
$-(\eta w-2nk)^2/(2n)$ leaves $2nk^2$.
Compact maximum comparison for $(t-a)\eta q$ on $[a,t]$ yields
\[
 (t-a)\eta q\leq4n\{1+(B+(n+2)A+k)(t-a)\}.
\]
At $a>0$ the time factor is zero, so no initial logarithmic derivative
bound is needed; then let $a\downarrow0$.
For the radial cutoffs of radius $R$, Laplacian comparison gives
$A=O(R^{-2})$ and $B=O(R^{-2}+\sqrt{K_0}/R)$.
At a cut point use the smooth lower support of the decreasing
distance cutoff in the maximum calculation.  Consequently the
radius error tends to zero.

Applied to a kernel row, this estimate holds along a fixed minimizing
geodesic once its surrounding cutoff ball and the other kernel
variable lie inside the exhaustion domain.  Pass the resulting
finite-domain inequalities to the monotone limit and then let
$R\to\infty$.  The differential estimate becomes
$q\leq4n/t+4nk$.  Along a path from $x$ at time $a$ to $z$ at
time $b$, completing the square gives
\[
 \frac d{dt}f(\gamma(t),t)
 \geq-\frac{|\gamma'(t)|^2}{2}-\frac{2n}{t}-2nk.
\]
Using a constant-speed minimizing geodesic therefore gives
\[
 H(x,y,a)\leq H(z,y,b)
 \exp\left(2n\log\frac ba+2nk(b-a)
                       +\frac{d(x,z)^2}{2(b-a)}\right).
\]
This is the static comparison used in the Gaussian estimate; it is
independent of the Ricci-flow matrix Harnack inequality being proved.

Write $r=\sqrt t$.  On the annulus $jr\leq d(x,y)<(j+1)r$,
relative volume comparison gives
\[
 \operatorname{Vol}B(x,(j+1)r)
 \leq(j+1)^n e^{(n-1)\sqrt{K_0}(j+1)r}
                                      \operatorname{Vol}B(x,r).
\]
Multiplying the Gaussian bound by distance and summing therefore gives
\[
 \int d(x,y)H(x,y,t)\,d\mu(y)
 \leq Cr\sum_{j=0}^\infty (j+1)^{n+1}
  e^{-j^2/192+(n-1)\sqrt{K_0}(j+1)}\leq C'\sqrt t.
\]
The series converges independently of $x$ and $0<t\leq1$.
It also supplies a finite first moment at every later finite time,
which will be needed for derivative domination.  Write
$M_t(x)=\int d(x,y)H(x,y,t)\,d\mu(y)$.
The semigroup identity, conservation, the triangle inequality and
Tonelli give
\[
 M_{s+t}(x)\leq M_s(x)+\sup_z M_t(z).
\]
For any fixed $T_*>0$, choose a positive integer $N$ with $T_*/N\leq1$
and iterate to get $\sup_xM_{T_*}(x)\leq
NC'\sqrt{T_*/N}<\infty$.  No large-time Gaussian bound is assumed.
Consequently the actual integral
\[
 v(x,t)=\int u(y)H(x,y,t)\,d\mu(y)
\]
exists, and conservation and the Lipschitz bound of $u$ imply
\[
 |v(x,t)-u(x)|\leq2\int d(x,y)H(x,y,t)\,d\mu(y)
                  \leq2C'\sqrt t\qquad(0<t\leq1).
\]
This proves both the uniform displacement bound and the uniform
initial trace, although $u$ itself is unbounded.

For the gradient estimate first replace $u$ by $u_R=\eta_Ru$,
where $R\geq1$, $\eta_R=1$ on $B(O,R)$, its support lies in
$\overline B(O,5R)$ and $|\nabla\eta_R|\leq c_0/R$.
Then $|u_R|\leq|u|$ and
$|\nabla u_R|\leq2+6c_0=:B_2$, independently of $R$.
The compact-data evolution $v_R$ has its initial gradient trace.
Here is why that assertion is uniform over the exhaustion domains.
If $P_t^\Omega$ is the Dirichlet heat operator and a compact smooth
function $f$ is supported in $\Omega$, the eigenfunction multipliers
satisfy $|e^{-\lambda t}-1|\leq t\lambda$ and commute with every
Laplacian power.  Thus
\[
 \|\Delta^j(P_t^\Omega f-f)\|_{L^2(\Omega)}
                          \leq t\|\Delta^{j+1}f\|_{L^2(M)}.
\]
On two fixed nested coordinate neighborhoods, local elliptic
estimates and Sobolev evaluation bound the first derivative of the
error by a finite sum of these norms.  Its bound is $C_f t$, with
$C_f$ independent of $\Omega$ once the neighborhoods and support of
$f$ lie inside it.  The resulting error functions are $C_f t$-Lipschitz
on a smaller convex coordinate ball.  Their pointwise limit is the
exhaustion-kernel integral, by dominated convergence.  The same
Lipschitz bound passes to that limit and bounds its first derivative.
Together with continuity of $Df$, this proves the joint initial
gradient trace as $(x,t)\to(p,0)$, not merely a trace at fixed $p$.
Conservation bounds $|v_R|$ by $\|u_R\|_\infty$.
For $q=|\nabla v_R|^2$, the Bochner formula gives
\[
 (\partial_t-\Delta)q
 =-2|\nabla^2v_R|^2-2\operatorname{Ric}(\nabla v_R,\nabla v_R)
 \leq-2|\nabla^2v_R|^2+2kq,\qquad k=(n-1)K_0.
\]
The inequality $|\nabla q|^2\leq4q|\nabla^2v_R|^2$ shows that
\[
 z=e^{-kt}\sqrt{q+1}-\sqrt{B_2^2+1}
 \quad\hbox{satisfies}\quad(\partial_t-\Delta)z\leq0,
 \qquad z(\cdot,0)\leq0.
\]
The square root is useful because $z_+^2\leq q$.
The identity $(\partial_t-\Delta)v_R^2=-2q$, multiplied by a
compact spatial cutoff and integrated by parts, bounds
$\int_0^1\int_{B(O,L)}q$ by a polynomial in $L$ times
$\operatorname{Vol}B(O,5L)$.  Relative volume comparison bounds
this volume exponentially in $L$.  Summing over unit annuli proves
\[
 \int_0^1\int e^{-d(O,x)^2/32}q(x,t)\,d\mu(x)\,dt<\infty.
\]
This finiteness may depend on $R$; the resulting bound for $z$ does
not.  Indeed choose a smooth distance majorant $\rho_1\geq d(O,\cdot)$
with $|\nabla\rho_1|\leq2$ and put
$\xi=-\rho_1^2/[16(2-t)]$.  Then
$\xi_t+|\nabla\xi|^2\leq0$ and
$e^\xi\leq e^{-d(O,\cdot)^2/32}$ on $0\leq t\leq1$.
For a smooth nonnegative convex approximation $w$ of the positive
part of $z$, compactly supported cutoff integration gives
\[
 \frac d{dt}\int\eta^2e^\xi w^2
       \leq4\int e^\xi w^2|\nabla\eta|^2.
\]
One can take a convex detector vanishing exactly on $(-\infty,0]$
and bounded above by the positive part.  Let the cutoff radius tend
to infinity.  The weighted space-time integrability makes the right
side vanish, while the initial integral is zero.  Positivity of
volume on open sets and continuity imply $z\leq0$ everywhere.
Thus
\[
 |\nabla v_R|\leq\sqrt{B_2^2+1}\,e^k\quad(0\leq t\leq1).
\]
Dominated convergence, with dominating function $|u(y)|H(x,y,t)$,
passes the compact-data integrals to $v$.  First pass their distance
Lipschitz inequalities to the limit; after establishing smoothness,
differentiate along short geodesics to obtain the same gradient bound
for $v$.  No convergence of $\nabla v_R$ is required.

The smoothness and equation of this integral also follow from the
same construction.  On a compact positive-time cylinder in a
harmonic lift, interior heat estimates bound each kernel derivative
by a constant times the supremum of that kernel row on a larger
cylinder.  The static Harnack inequality bounds this supremum by a
fixed later row $H(O,y,T_*)$.  That row has a finite first moment, so
it is integrable against $|u(y)|$ and permits differentiation under
the integral to every order.  Local inverses of the lift descend
smoothness to $M$.  Alternatively, for the equation itself, the
compact-data evolutions converge locally uniformly; testing against
compact smooth functions passes
$\frac d{dt}\int\phi v_R=\int v_R\Delta\phi$ to the limit.
Smoothness then identifies the weak equation with $v_t=\Delta v$.

It remains to bound the Hessian at time one.  The uniform harmonic
lift constructed in Section~\ref{sec:rf-harmonic-coordinates} gives,
near each point $p$, a local isometry $e:B(0,2r_0)\to M$ with
$e(0)=p$, $aI\leq h=e^*g\leq bI$, $|Dh|\leq G$ and harmonic
coordinate functions.  The constants depend only on $n,K_0$;
global injectivity of $e$ is not required.  If $A_0$ bounds both
displacement and gradient, the centered solution
$\widehat v(y,t)=v(e(y),t)-u(p)$ satisfies
\[
 |\widehat v|\leq (A_0+1)\sqrt b\,(2r_0)+A_0=:B_3,
 \qquad \widehat v_t=h^{ij}\partial_i\partial_j\widehat v.
\]
Interior parabolic estimates give $|D^2\widehat v(0,1)|\leq C B_3$.
The inverse metric is uniformly elliptic and Lipschitz, hence has
a uniform one-half-H\"older bound on this fixed ball, as required
by the estimate.  Since $|D\widehat v|\leq A_0\sqrt b$ and
$|\Gamma(h)|\leq3G/(2a)$, the identity
$\nabla^2\widehat v=D^2\widehat v-\Gamma D\widehat v$ gives
\[
 |\nabla^2v(p,1)|\leq
 \frac{CB_3+A_0\sqrt b\,3G/(2a)}a=:H_0.
\]
Metric pullback identifies these Hessians.  In dimension one the
complete connected noncompact manifold has a global arclength
coordinate; ordinary Gaussian convolution supplies displacement and
gradient bounds, and a radius-two arclength lift supplies the same
interior Hessian argument.  Dimension zero has no connected
noncompact case.

For clarity, the interior parabolic estimate here has a uniform
constant even though no Hessian bound is supplied as data.  After
rescaling a coordinate cylinder, write its equation as
$f_t=a^{ij}(x)f_{ij}$, with ellipticity constants $\lambda,\Lambda$
and one-half-H\"older coefficient constant $H$.
Freeze $a$ at a point $x$ and multiply $f$ by a spatial cutoff of
radius $r$ and a time cutoff which vanishes initially and is one
on the last half of a time interval of length $T$.
If $B,G,M$ temporarily bound $|f|,|Df|,|D^2f|$ on this cylinder,
the residual for the frozen operator is bounded by
\[
 C\left(HM+
       \frac{\Lambda(G/r+B/r^2)}{(r/2)^{1/2}}\right)|y-x|^{1/2}
                   +\frac{C B}{T}.
\]
The spatial commutator is supported where $|y-x|\geq r/2$;
the last term comes from the time cutoff and vanishes on the last
half interval.  The frozen Gaussian kernel satisfies
$\int|D^2G_s(y)|\,|y|^{1/2}\,dy\leq C s^{-3/4}$.
The zero-initial-data heat representation therefore bounds the
Hessian at the terminal center by
\[
 C\left(HM+
       \frac{\Lambda(G/r+B/r^2)}{(r/2)^{1/2}}\right)T^{1/4}
                   +\frac{CB}{T}.
\]
In the time-cutoff term the kernel time is at least $T/2$, so
its otherwise nonintegrable second-derivative singularity is absent.
Taylor's formula on a ball of radius $2r$ gives
$G\leq2B/r+Mr$.  Choose $T=qr^2$ with $q>0$ small, depending
only on the coefficient and cutoff constants.  For $r\leq1$ the
terms containing $M$ are then at most $M/8$, leaving
\[
 |D^2f(x,t)|\leq A B/r^2+M/8.
\]
Take nested cylinders whose successive available radii are
$r_j=2^{-j}/16$, all inside a fixed compact interior cylinder, and
write $S_j$ for the Hessian supremum on the $j$th one.  Then
\[
 S_j\leq256AB\,4^j+S_{j+1}/8,
 \qquad
 S_0\leq256AB\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}2^{-j}+8^{-N}S_N.
\]
Smoothness bounds all $S_N$ on the fixed compact cylinder, so the
last term tends to zero and $S_0\leq512AB$.  A compact smooth
extension of the original local solution agrees on these cylinders
and permits the frozen-kernel representation; no bound for the
extension enters the final estimate.

With the displacement, gradient and Hessian bounds denoted by
$A_0,A_0,H_0$,
then $s=v(\cdot,1)+A_0+2$ satisfies
\[
 1+d(O,x)\leq s(x)\leq B_0(1+d(O,x)),\qquad
 |ds|\leq B_0,\qquad \nabla^2s\leq B_0g,
\]
where $B_0=\max\{2(A_0+1)+1,A_0,H_0\}$.
The constant shift preserves both derivatives.  The heat estimates,
including their dependence only on dimension and the curvature bound,
are the distance-smoothing input
(\cite[Proposition 26.49, equations (26.128)--(26.152),
pp.~378--385]{ChowEtAl2010}).

The transfer to the whole buffered slab is explicit.  Suppose
$|\operatorname{Ric}(v,v)|\leq K_1|v|^2$,
$|\nabla\operatorname{Ric}|\leq L_1$ and $|t-t_0|\leq T$;
put $E=e^{K_1T}$.  Metric comparison gives
$E^{-1}|v|_{t_0}\leq|v|_t\leq E|v|_{t_0}$.
For the fixed function $s$, connection variation gives
\[
 \partial_t\nabla^2s(v,v)=-ds((\partial_t\nabla)_vv),\qquad
 |\partial_t\nabla^2s(v,v)|
 \leq3B_0L_1E^3|v|_{t_0}^2.
\]
Integrating in time and changing the norm back to $g(t)$ yields
$\nabla_t^2s\leq B_0(1+3L_1E^3T)E^2g(t)$.
Thus $\rho=Es$ has all three required bounds with, for example,
\[
 B=1+B_0E^2+B_0(1+3L_1E^3T)E^3.
\]
Completeness and the distance lower bound make its closed sublevels
compact.  Tracing the Hessian gives $\Delta\rho\leq nB$.

Choose $A>C+nB$, $\varepsilon,\delta>0$ with $C\delta\leq\varepsilon$
and $\delta e^{AT}\leq1$, and put
\[
 \alpha(x,\tau)=\frac{\varepsilon e^{A\tau}\rho(x)}{\tau},
 \qquad \psi(\tau)=\delta e^{A\tau}.
\]
Writing $\phi=\varepsilon e^{A\tau}\rho$, direct differentiation gives
\[
 \alpha_\tau-\Delta\alpha+2\alpha/\tau-C\alpha-C\psi/\tau^2
 =\frac{\phi_\tau-\Delta\phi-C\phi}{\tau}
                         +\frac{\phi-C\psi}{\tau^2}>0.
\]
The first numerator is positive since $\rho\geq1$ and
$A>C+nB$; the second is nonnegative since $C\delta\leq\varepsilon$.
Also $\psi'-C\psi>0$.

There is no initial positivity assumption on $M$.  Nonnegative
curvature and the derivative bounds instead give
$Q(U,W)\geq-C_0w^2-C_0uw$, where
$u^2=\sum U_{ab}^2$, $w^2=\sum W_a^2$ and, for example,
$C_0=6n^4K+3n^5K^2$.  Young's inequality shows
\[
 Q+\alpha w^2+\delta u^2>0\quad\hbox{for }(u,w)\ne(0,0)
 \quad\hbox{if }\alpha>C_0+C_0^2/(2\delta).
\]
Put $D=C_0+C_0^2/(2\delta)+1$.  On
$0<\tau\leq\varepsilon/D$ the lower bound
$\alpha\geq\varepsilon/\tau\geq D$ gives positivity uniformly in
space.  At every time $0<\tau\leq T$, positivity also holds outside
the single compact set
\[
 K_{\varepsilon,\delta}=\{x:\rho(x)\leq TD/\varepsilon\},
\]
since there $\alpha>TD/\tau\geq D$.  The two-form coefficient is
$\psi\geq\delta$, so the same quadratic estimate applies in both
cases.  Normalize test directions to unit length in a fixed
background orthonormal frame bundle.  Over $K_{\varepsilon,\delta}$
their parameter space is compact: its fibers are the compact
orthogonal group and the closed unit sphere of skew two-forms and
vectors.  Homogeneity makes testing these directions sufficient.
For a proposed nonpositive value at time $t$, begin instead at
$a=\tfrac12\min\{t,\varepsilon/D\}>0$.  On $[a,t]$ the quadratic
is continuous, positive initially and positive outside that compact
parameter space.  Its first zero is therefore attained, with ordinary
time derivative nonpositive.
Inequality~\eqref{eq:rf-harnack-contact} gives a strictly positive
derivative there, a contradiction.  Choose
$\delta=\varepsilon/(C+1)$ and
$0<\varepsilon<(C+1)e^{-AT}$.  These choices satisfy all the
perturbation inequalities, and letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$
recovers unperturbed positivity at every fixed point and time.
Finally let the buffered time origin
approach the original left endpoint; the block entries converge and
positive semidefiniteness is closed.  This proves the bounded-slab
version used below, with its original elapsed-time correction
(\cite[Theorem 4.37 and Corollary 4.39, p.~81]{MT2007};
\cite[Theorem 15.16, pp.~272--279; Lemma 15.23, p.~283;
Section 15.4.5, p.~287]{ChowEtAl2008}).

\subsection{Why bounds on separate slices suffice}

The hypothesis $\forall t\,\exists K_t$ cannot be exchanged with
$\exists K\,\forall t$.  The following is the route that bridges them.
Work on one connected component of dimension at least two.  A complete
metric with bounded nonnegative sectional curvature has a uniform
positive lower bound on its unit-ball volumes.  The constant may depend
on the entire component.  Here is the injectivity argument giving it.
Nonnegative sectional curvature and completeness give a nonnegative
proper continuous convex exhaustion $b$, with nonempty zero set.
Here are the construction and the compactness argument.  Fix $p$ and,
for each unit-speed minimizing ray $\sigma$ from $p$, put
\[
 b_\sigma(x)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\bigl(d(x,\sigma(T))-T\bigr),
 \qquad
 b(x)=\sup\bigl(\{0\}\cup\{-b_\sigma(x):\sigma(0)=p\}\bigr).
\]
The quantities defining the limit decrease with $T$ by the triangle
inequality and are bounded below by $-d(p,x)$.  Thus $b_\sigma$ is
well defined, is $1$-Lipschitz, and satisfies
$b_\sigma(\sigma(s))=-s$.  The same Lipschitz bound passes to the
supremum, which lies between $0$ and $d(p,x)$; in particular $b(p)=0$.

The curvature input makes $b_\sigma$ concave along every affine
geodesic $\gamma$ of constant speed $v$.  To see precisely what
passes to the limit, squared-distance comparison says that
\[
       s\longmapsto d(z,\gamma(s))^2-v^2s^2
\]
is concave.  This includes cut points: shorten a minimizing segment
from $z$ to $\gamma(s_0)$ by a fraction $\delta$ at its initial end.
The remaining segment has a smooth inverse exponential branch at its
terminal point.  Its radius plus the discarded length bounds the
original distance from above and touches it at $s_0$.  The index-form
comparison with the linearly growing parallel field, using
$\sec\geq0$, bounds the second derivative of the squared support
by $2v^2/(1-\delta)$.  Letting $\delta\downarrow0$ gives the
concavity assertion by the one-dimensional upper-support criterion.
At a point where $z=\gamma(s_0)$ the support
$v^2(s-s_0)^2$ gives the same bound directly.  No minimizing
assumption on the whole interval of $\gamma$ is needed.
For $z=\sigma(T)$, divide this concave function minus $T^2$ by
$2T$.  If $a_T=d(\sigma(T),\gamma(s))-T$, the result is
\[
       a_T+\frac{a_T^2-v^2s^2}{2T}\longrightarrow
       b_\sigma(\gamma(s)).
\]
Passing to the limit in the concavity inequality proves the claim.
Consequently $b$ is convex along all geodesics.

For $r\geq0$, its closed sublevel $C_r$ is the intersection of
$\{b_\sigma\geq-r\}$ over these rays.  It contains $p$, and every
minimizing segment from $p$ to a point of $C_r$ stays in $C_r$ by
convexity.  If $C_r$ were noncompact, properness would give points
$q_j\in C_r$ with $d(p,q_j)\to\infty$.  The corresponding
unit-speed segments stay in compact balls on each fixed time window.
Choose a common ultrafilter refining $j\to\infty$ and take
their limits at every nonnegative time.  All limits exist in those
compact balls, and the identity
$d(\gamma_j(s),\gamma_j(t))=|s-t|$ passes to the limit.  The
result is a minimizing ray $\sigma$ from $p$ lying in the closed
set $C_r$.  But at time $r+1$ its own Busemann function equals
$-r-1$, contradicting membership in $C_r$.  This proves properness.
If the component is compact there are no minimizing rays and the
same definition gives $b=0$.

Choose $c>0$ below half the uniform Jacobi comparison radius supplied
by the curvature bound.  The quantitative input here can be obtained
directly from the Jacobi equation.  If $|\operatorname{Rm}|\leq K$, parallel
translation turns a radial variation into
$J''+\mathcal R J=0$, $J(0)=0$, $J'(0)=w$, with
$\|\mathcal R\|\leq K|u|^2$ along the radial geodesic with
initial velocity $u$.  Gronwall and two integrations give
\[
 |J(t)-tw|\leq
 \frac{K|u|^2e^{\max\{1,K|u|^2\}}}{6}|w|t^3,
 \qquad 0\leq t\leq1.
\]
For example, the uniform radius
$r_K=(2+Ke^{K+1})^{-1}$ ensures that the coefficient before
$|w|t^3/6$ is at most $3$.  Hence
$|d\exp_x|_u(w)|\geq|w|/2$ when $|u|\leq r_K$.
Take $2c\leq r_K$.  This proves nonsingularity on the tangent
balls needed below, without a prior injectivity assumption.  A
sectional-curvature bound supplies such a tensor bound by polarization.

Define
\[
 i_c(x)=\sup\{r\in[0,c]:\exp_x\text{ is injective on }B(0,r)
                       \subset T_xM\}.
\]
Below the nonsingularity scale this is the usual injectivity radius
truncated at $c$.  It is positive by the inverse function theorem at
zero, and the open ball of radius $i_c(x)$ itself is an injectivity
domain: any proposed pair of colliding vectors would already lie in
one smaller injectivity domain.
It is also lower semicontinuous.  Indeed, choose
$0<r<s<i_c(x)$ and identify nearby tangent spaces in a chart.
The map $(q,u)\mapsto(q,\exp_q u)$ is locally invertible along
the closed tangent ball of radius $s$ at $x$, since its differential
is triangular with invertible vertical block.  Injectivity on this
compact ball persists as $q$ varies.  Otherwise a sequence of
colliding pairs has a limiting pair in that ball.  Distinct limits
contradict injectivity at $x$; equal limits contradict local
invertibility of the total map.  Continuity of the tangent norms
then puts each nearby $r$-ball inside this fixed $s$-ball.  Thus
$i_c(q)\geq r$ near $x$, proving lower semicontinuity.
On the compact nonempty set $\{b=0\}$, $i_c$ therefore has a
positive lower bound $m$.  We claim
$i_c\geq\min(m,c)$ everywhere.  If not, choose $q$ violating this
inequality and $\eta>0$ small enough that
$i_c(q)+\eta b(q)<\min(m,c)$.  Minimize $i_c+\eta b$ on the compact
sublevel $\{b\leq b(q)\}$, at $x$.  This minimum is below $\min(m,c)$,
so $b(x)>0$ and $i_c(x)<c$.

We need the first cut loop and its moving-endpoint bound.  Write
$\iota=i_c(x)<c$ and choose $\iota<r<c$.  On the compact
tangent ball $\overline B(0,r)$ the exponential is locally
injective but is not injective.  Its distinct colliding pairs form
a compact set: a pair approaching the diagonal would contradict
local injectivity there.  Minimize $\max\{|u|,|w|\}$ over
this set.  The two minimizing radii are equal.  If, for instance,
$|w|<|u|$, move $u$ slightly radially inward and lift its image
through the inverse branch near $w$; both radii then become strictly
less than $|u|$, contradicting minimality.  If the minimum is
$\rho$, minimality makes the open tangent ball of radius $\rho$
injective, so $\rho\leq\iota$.  The collision gives the reverse
inequality, hence $\rho=\iota$.

Let $y=\exp_xu=\exp_xw$.  On the two local inverse branches
near $y$, the functions one half the squared radius have gradients
$U=d\exp_x|_u(u)$ and $W=d\exp_x|_w(w)$ by Gauss' lemma.
Both gradients have norm $\iota$.  Their maximum has a local
minimum at $y$, so $U=-W$: if $U+W\ne0$, the direction
$-(U+W)$ strictly decreases both functions, since
\[
 \langle U,-(U+W)\rangle
 =\langle W,-(U+W)\rangle=-\tfrac12|U+W|^2<0.
\]
Geodesic uniqueness now joins the two radial segments smoothly at
$y$, producing a geodesic from $x$ back to $x$ of length
$2\iota$, with midpoint $y$.  Convexity along this entire
geodesic gives $b(y)\leq b(x)$.

More generally, two distinct geodesics from $z$ to the same endpoint,
with lengths $a$ and $d$, give
$i_c(z)\leq(a+d)/2$.  For equal lengths this is the collision
bound.  If $a>d$ and the claimed bound failed, choose
\[
       (a+d)/2<s<\min\{a,i_c(z)\}.
\]
The point at length $s$ on the longer geodesic has distance $s$
from $z$.  This follows because a shorter minimizing geodesic,
provided by completeness, would give two exponential vectors
strictly inside the injectivity domain.  But following the shorter
geodesic to the common endpoint and then the longer one backwards
reaches this point with length $d+a-s<s$, a contradiction.
Reverse the two radial segments to apply the same inequality at
$y$; their terminal velocities are distinct by geodesic uniqueness.
It follows that $i_c(y)\leq\iota<m$, and therefore $b(y)>0$.
Move $y$ a short distance toward $\{b=0\}$ along a minimizing geodesic
$\gamma(s)$.  Convexity gives $b(\gamma(s))\leq b(y)-hs$ for some
$h>0$.  The two inverse exponential branches remain defined and
distinct near $y$.  If their vectors at $\gamma(s)$ are $u(s)$
and $w(s)$, put $F(s)=(|u(s)|+|w(s)|)/2$.  Gauss' lemma gives
\[
 F(0)=\iota,\qquad
 F'(0)=\frac{\langle U+W,\gamma'(0)\rangle}{2\iota}=0.
\]
Reversing the corresponding geodesics and applying the unequal-length
bound gives $i_c(\gamma(s))\leq F(s)$.  Thus
\[
 i_c(\gamma(s))+\eta b(\gamma(s))
 \leq i_c(x)+o(s)+\eta b(x)-\eta hs
 <i_c(x)+\eta b(x)
\]
for small positive $s$, contradicting minimality in the same sublevel.
This proves the claimed uniform injectivity bound.  Choose a positive
radius $r_0<\min(m,c,1)$.  The exponential at every center is
injective on this ball, its image lies in the intrinsic unit ball,
and its differential has all singular values at least $1/2$ by the
Jacobi estimate.  In orthonormal frames its inverse therefore has
entries bounded by $2$, so the determinant expansion bounds its
absolute determinant by $n!2^n$.  Change of variables gives the
positive uniform volume lower bound
$\operatorname{vol}_{\mathbb R^n}(B(0,r_0))/(n!2^n)$; its value
may depend on the entire component through $m$.

The source comparison is with
\cite[Lemma 3.8, p.~15; Lemma 3.13, pp.~17--19;
Lemma 4.10 and Corollary 4.12, pp.~29--31;
Lemma 5.1, pp.~35--37]{MaederBaumdickerSeidel2025}.
That paper continues through successive totally convex strata to
compare injectivity radii with a soul.  The argument needed here is
the smooth specialization using the stationary average of two
inverse-branch lengths and a compact convex zero set.  The small
penalty $\eta b$ supplies the global minimum contradiction directly;
the stronger soul comparison is not required for this volume bound.

\subsection{Scalar integrals and regular fibers}
\label{sec:rf-scalar-integral}

To propagate this volume bound, the static scalar-integral input is as follows.
For each dimension $n$ there is $P_n>0$ such that every complete
$n$-manifold $(N,h)$ with $\sec_h\geq-1$ satisfies
\[
             \int_{B_h(p,1)}R_h\,d\mu_h\leq P_n
                         \quad\hbox{for every }p\in N.
\]
It is Petrunin's estimate \cite[Theorem 1.1, manuscript p.~1]{Petrunin2009}.
The geometric content behind this input is not a consequence of the
volume evolution equation.  On a compact regular slab
$f^{-1}([u,v])$, let $\mathbf n=\nabla f/|\nabla f|$, let
$B=\nabla^2 f/|\nabla f|$ restricted to the levels, and set
$H=\operatorname{tr}B$, $G=H^2-|B|^2$.  The integrated hypersurface
Bochner identity reads
\[
 \int_{f^{-1}([u,v])}(G-\operatorname{Ric}(\mathbf n,\mathbf n))\,d\mu
 =\int_{f^{-1}(v)}H\,dA-\int_{f^{-1}(u)}H\,dA.
\]
Together with the Gauss identity
$R_{f^{-1}(s)}=R-2\operatorname{Ric}(\mathbf n,\mathbf n)+G$,
it replaces an ambient scalar integral by lower-dimensional scalar
integrals, $G$, and the two signed boundary terms.  The slab identity
follows first with a smooth cutoff in $f$, by integration by parts,
and then with the interval indicator by coarea and approximation.
Both endpoint normals point toward increasing $f$; replacing their
signs indiscriminately would destroy this identity.

The induction needs more than complete hypersurfaces: level sections
can be incomplete and their curvature lower bounds vary with the
second fundamental form.  The following regular-fiber formulation
retains the error which the Gauss equation produces.

Let $(N,g)$ be a complete $(m+k)$-manifold with $\sec_g\geq-1$.
On an open set $U$ choose smooth functions $f_i,h_i$, $1\leq i\leq k$.
For parameters $\delta>0$ and $H_0\geq0$ require, everywhere on $U$,
\[
\begin{gathered}
 |\nabla f_i|,|\nabla h_i|\leq1,\qquad
 \langle\nabla f_i,\nabla h_i\rangle\leq-1+2\delta,\\
 |\langle\nabla a_i,\nabla b_j\rangle|\leq\delta
 \quad(i\ne j,\ a_i\in\{f_i,h_i\},\ b_j\in\{f_j,h_j\}),\\
 \langle\nabla f_i,\nabla f_j\rangle\leq0\quad(i\ne j),\qquad
 \nabla^2 f_i,\nabla^2 h_i\leq H_0g.
\end{gathered}
\]
Assume $F=(f_1,\ldots,f_k)$ is a submersion on $U$, and let
$L=F^{-1}(c)\cap U$ have its induced metric.  We call this the regular
fiber of the paired defining functions.  The fourth inequality is the
tightness condition.  It preserves control of induced curvature when
one adds another defining function.  Let $E:L\to[0,\infty)$ be
continuous with $\sec_L\geq-E$ pointwise.

The normalized induction assertion is this: for every $m\geq2$ and
$k\geq0$ there is $\delta(m,k)>0$ such that, for every $H_0\geq0$ and
$\eta>0$, there is $C=C(m,k,H_0,\eta)>0$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-corner-normalized}
       \int_L R_L^+\,d\mu_L\leq C\left(1+\int_LE\,d\mu_L\right),
       \qquad R_L^+=\max(R_L,0),
\end{equation}
whenever $L$ is compact, connected, complete, has intrinsic diameter
at most one, and its closed ambient $\eta$-neighborhood is contained
in $U$.  The tolerance is chosen before the Hessian and buffer bounds.
For $m\geq3$ the corresponding local assertion bounds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-corner-local}
 \frac{\int_{L\cap B_g(p,1)}R_L^+\,d\mu_L}
      {1+\int_{L\cap B_g(p,2)}E\,d\mu_L}\leq B(m,k,H_0),
 \qquad p\in L,
\end{equation}
provided $\overline{B_g(p,2)}\subset U$.  The intersections in this
formula use ambient balls; completeness of the whole open fiber is
not assumed.  The constants are uniform over the ambient manifold,
defining functions, fiber value, basepoint and error function.

For surfaces, Gauss--Bonnet gives
$\int_LR_L\leq8\pi$ and $R_L^-=\max(-R_L,0)\leq2E$.
Hence \eqref{eq:rf-corner-normalized} holds with $C=8\pi+2$.
Suppose now that the normalized assertion is known in fiber dimension
$m-1$ for every codimension.  We describe the two steps proving the
local assertion in dimension $m$ and then recovering its normalized
form.

\subsubsection{A regular radial function}

First consider the ambient annulus $r/2<d(p,\cdot)<3r$, on which distance $d(p,\cdot)$
has local ascent rate greater than some fixed $v<1$: at each point
$y$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, there is $z$ with
$d(y,z)<\varepsilon$ and
$d(p,z)-d(p,y)>v\,d(y,z)$.
We describe why the level function is regular on the old fiber and
why its levels are compact.  Choose a tolerance for the next induction
step and then auxiliary constants
\[
\begin{gathered}
 0<\Delta\leq\min\{[16(k+1)]^{-1},[256(k+2)^2]^{-1}\},\quad
 \varepsilon=\frac{\Delta}{8(k+1)},\quad
 \sigma=\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2048},\quad
 \tau=(1+\sigma^2/8)^{-1},\\
 \delta_0=(\varepsilon/256)^2,\qquad
 \mu=\varepsilon/64,\qquad
 r\leq\min\{1/2,\varepsilon/[32(H_0+1)]\}.
\end{gathered}
Use $v=1-\sigma^2/8$ in the ascent assumption and put $q=\mu r$.
These choices ensure, for $0\leq\delta\leq\delta_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-tilt-budget}
 4\sqrt\delta+3q/r+2H_0r+\sigma^2/8\leq\varepsilon/2.
\end{equation}
In particular the old tolerance is fixed before the Hessian bound
determines the scale.

Directional smoothing of distance on the annulus produces one smooth
function $u$, close to $\tau d(p,\cdot)$, with
\[
 |u-\tau d(p,\cdot)|\leq\tau\sigma^4r/1048576,\quad
 1-\sigma^2\leq|\nabla u|\leq1,\quad
 \nabla^2u\leq(5/r)g
 \quad(r<d(p,\cdot)<2r).
\]
It is proper over the value interval
$(9\tau r/8,15\tau r/8)$, whose inverse image lies in this annulus.
The smoothing retains the near-orthogonality information on the
value tube $|f_i-f_i(p)|<q$: its mixed gradient products with both
$f_i$ and $h_i$ have absolute value at most $\varepsilon/2$.
The terms on the left of \eqref{eq:rf-tilt-budget} account for the
opposition error, value-tube width, Hessian error and smoothing error,
respectively.

We give the construction, including the directional estimates.
Write $d=d(p,x)>0$ and let $\gamma:[0,d]\to M$ be a unit-speed
minimizing geodesic from $p$ to $x$ in the defining domain.
For an old pair $(f_i,h_i)$ put
$A=f_i\circ\gamma$, $B=h_i\circ\gamma$.
The opposite-gradient bound gives
$|A'+B'|\leq2\sqrt\delta$, while $A'',B''\leq H_0$.
Integrating the second-derivative inequality backward from $d$ gives
\[
 A'(d)\leq\frac{A(d)-A(0)}d+\frac{H_0d}{2},
 \qquad
 B'(d)\leq\frac{B(d)-B(0)}d+\frac{H_0d}{2}.
\]
Also $|(A+B)(d)-(A+B)(0)|\leq2\sqrt\delta\,d$.
Combining these upper bounds with $|A'(d)+B'(d)|\leq2\sqrt\delta$
therefore yields
\[
 |A'(d)|,|B'(d)|\leq
 4\sqrt\delta+\frac{|f_i(x)-f_i(p)|}d+\frac{H_0d}{2}.
\]
A shortened-segment upper support for $d(p,\cdot)$ has gradient
$\gamma'(d)$ at $x$, so it has exactly these mixed derivative
bounds, including at a cut point of the original distance.
The same estimates, with arbitrarily small additional loss, hold
on a neighborhood of a point in the closed value tube, by continuity
of the endpoint gaps and finiteness of the old family.

Choose small exponential coordinate balls.  The pulled-back fields
corresponding to $\nabla f_i,\nabla h_i$ have small oscillation
there.  Freezing one field at the evaluation point changes the
directional derivative of any unit-gradient upper support by at
most its coordinate derivative norm times this oscillation.
The elementary one-variable upper-support comparison, applied along
each coordinate segment and then in its reverse direction, turns
these two-sided support derivative bounds into two-sided increment
bounds for distance.  Convolution by a nonnegative kernel of integral
one preserves those increment inequalities.  Dividing by the segment
length and passing to the limit gives the same directional bounds
for the convolution.  The same convolution retains semiconcavity
and the Lipschitz bound; the small metric and connection errors
convert these to intrinsic gradient and Hessian estimates.

On the compact annulus
$r/2-\ell\leq d(p,\cdot)\leq3r+\ell$, where
$\ell=\sigma^2r/1024$, use these constrained approximations near
the closed value tube and ordinary distance approximations on its
open complement.  The biased-cutoff regularized-minimum construction
developed below glues them into a single smooth function $w$.
Its gradient is a convex combination of the active branch gradients;
thus it preserves the two-sided linear constraints as well as the
norm and upper Hessian bounds.  Set
\[
 e=\sigma^4r/1048576,\qquad \zeta=\sigma^2/8,\qquad
 H_* = \frac{4}{3(r/2-\ell)}+r+\zeta\leq5/r.
\]
The resulting estimates are $|w-d(p,\cdot)|\leq e$,
$|\nabla w|\leq1+\zeta$ and $\nabla^2w\leq H_*g$.
On the tube the mixed derivative bound is
$4\sqrt\delta+q/(r/2-\ell)+2H_0r+\zeta$;
since $\ell\leq r/64$, this is bounded by the left-hand side
of \eqref{eq:rf-tilt-budget}.

To obtain the lower gradient bound, maximize
$d(p,y)-v\,d(x,y)$ on $\overline B(x,\ell)$.
The local ascent assumption excludes an interior maximum, so a
maximizer $y$ satisfies
\[
 d(x,y)=\ell,\qquad d(p,y)-d(p,x)\geq v\ell.
\]
The ball lies in the buffered annulus where ascent is assumed.
Taylor's upper estimate for $w$ along a minimizing segment from
$x$ to $y$, together with its value error at both endpoints, gives
\[
 |\nabla w(x)|\geq v-2e/\ell-H_*\ell/2=:L_*.
\]
The selected constants ensure $\tau L_*\geq1-\sigma^2$.

Finally localize the band without changing $w$ on $r<d(p,x)<2r$.
Take a smooth cutoff $\psi$ equal to one near the closed $r/2$
ball and supported in the $r$ ball, and a smooth cutoff $\theta$
equal to one near the closed $2r$ ball and supported in the $3r$
ball.  Replace $w$ by
\[
 \widetilde w=\theta(1-\psi)w+(1-\theta)Q,
 \qquad Q>15r/8.
\]
Below radius $r$ its value is less than $9r/8$, and above radius
$2r$ its value is greater than $15r/8$, using $e<r/8$ and the
value estimates on the transition annuli.  Thus the inverse image
of $(9r/8,15r/8)$ lies entirely in the unchanged core and in the
compact support of $\theta$.  Every compact subinterval has a
closed, hence compact, inverse image.  Setting $u=\tau\widetilde w$
gives the properness and all the derivative bounds asserted above.

\subsubsection{An opposite function on each strip}

Put $s=\tau r/1024$ and choose a strip centered at a value
$z=2\tau t_0$, $7r/12\leq t_0\leq9r/10$.
Its closed inverse image $u^{-1}([z-s/4,z+s/4])$ is compact.
On its interior construct a second smooth function $v_0$ by smoothing
the distance to the upper level $u=z+3s/2$ and normalizing its gradient.
The upper-level gap is between $s$ and $2s$ on the strip; the
near-unit gradient and Hessian bounds for $u$ hold on its surrounding
$4s$ balls.  Here is the distance and smoothing argument.

Write $T=u^{-1}([z-s/4,z+s/4])$, $a_*=z+3s/2$,
$S=u^{-1}(a_*)$, and $d_S(y)=d(y,S)$.  On the closed
$s/4$-neighborhood of $T$, the value gap belongs to
$[3s/4,9s/4]$.  Put $c=1-2\sigma^2<|\nabla u|$.
To reach the level from $y$, maximize
$u(x)-c\,d(y,x)$ on
\[
 \overline B\bigl(y,(a_*-u(y))/c\bigr)\cap\{u\leq a_*\}.
\]
This is a nonempty compact set.  A maximizing point below the
level cannot lie on the sphere, since comparison with $y$ would
give $u(x)\geq a_*$.  At an interior point below the level,
moving a short distance in the gradient direction increases $u$
faster than $c$ times distance, contradicting maximality.
Thus $S$ is nonempty and $d_S(y)\leq(a_*-u(y))/c$.
This ball stays in a $4s$ buffer.  Integrating $|\nabla u|\leq1$
along a minimizing segment to a nearest point of the closed set
$S$ gives the reverse bound $a_*-u(y)\leq d_S(y)$.
In particular,
\[
 3s/4\leq d_S\leq3s,\qquad
 0\leq d_S-(a_*-u)\leq5\sigma^2s.
\]

For completeness, the smoothing preserves upper Hessian bounds
by the following construction.  At $y\notin S$, choose a nearest
point of $S$ and discard the first quarter of a minimizing segment
from that point to $y$.  Distance from the new initial point, plus
the discarded length, is a smooth upper support at $y$ on an
inverse exponential branch.  The index-form estimate under
$\sec\geq-1$ gives its Hessian upper bound
\[
 \left(\frac{4}{3d_S(y)}+\frac{d_S(y)}4\right)g.
\]
In sufficiently small normalized exponential coordinates, metric
and connection errors are arbitrarily small.  Subtracting a suitable
quadratic function makes the distance concave in these coordinates.
Convolution preserves that concavity and the Lipschitz bound, giving
local smooth approximations with arbitrarily small value error and
arbitrarily small loss in the intrinsic gradient and Hessian bounds.

One must also retain these bounds when gluing.  Choose finitely many
nested cutoffs $\psi_i,\theta_i$, with $\theta_i=1$ near the
support of $\psi_i$, and with some $\psi_i=1$ near each point of
the compact set.  Extend each local approximation $q_i$ by
\[
 b_i=\theta_iq_i+(1-\theta_i)Q+b(1-\psi_i),
\]
where $Q$ bounds the distance above on that set.  Choose $b>0$
small relative to the derivatives of the cutoffs, then the local
value error and regularization scale still smaller than $b$.
Whenever $\psi_i=0$, the branch $b_i$ exceeds an available
branch by a fixed positive gap.  It consequently has zero weight
in a finite regularized minimum.  Every active branch is in the
region $\theta_i=1$; its only extra derivative terms come from
$b(1-\psi_i)$ and are small.
Indeed the binary regularized minimum
$(a+b-\chi_\lambda(a-b))/2$, with $\chi_\lambda$ a smooth
convex approximation of absolute value equal to it outside a small
interval, has gradient a convex combination of the two gradients.
Its Hessian is the same combination of Hessians minus
$\chi_\lambda''(a-b)(da-db)^2/2$.  Iteration therefore preserves
the required upper bounds on active branches.

Apply this on the compact $s/4$-neighborhood of $T$, with value
error $\sigma^2s$ and derivative loss $\sigma^2$.  It produces
a smooth $\rho$ with
\[
 |\rho-d_S|\leq\sigma^2s,\quad |\nabla\rho|\leq1+\sigma^2,
 \quad\nabla^2\rho\leq(3/s)g.
\]
The Hessian coefficient follows from
$16/(9s)+3s/4+\sigma^2\leq3/s$.
Thus $u+\rho$ lies between $a_*-\sigma^2s$ and
$a_*+6\sigma^2s$, and its Hessian is at most $4g/s$.
In general, if a smooth $w$ has lower oscillation at most $e$
and Hessian at most $H g$ on a radius-$R$ ball, following the
negative gradient direction for distance $R$ gives
\[
 w(x)-e\leq w(\gamma(R))
 \leq w(x)-R|\nabla w(x)|+HR^2/2,
 \qquad |\nabla w(x)|\leq e/R+HR/2.
\]
Use $R=\sigma s/8$, $e=7\sigma^2s$, $H=4/s$.
It follows that $|\nabla u+\nabla\rho|\leq64\sigma$ on $T$.
Normalize $v_0=\rho/(1+\sigma^2)$; the near-unit lower bound
for $|\nabla u|$ then gives
$\langle\nabla u,\nabla v_0\rangle\leq-1+128\sigma$.
Our choice of $\sigma$ is small enough to give
\[
 |\nabla v_0|\leq1,\qquad
 \langle\nabla u,\nabla v_0\rangle\leq-1+\varepsilon^2/8,
 \qquad \nabla^2v_0\leq(3/s)g.
\]
The squared opposition error is useful: it implies
$|\nabla u+\nabla v_0|\leq\varepsilon/2$, so every mixed product
of $\nabla v_0$ with an old unit gradient has absolute value at
most $\varepsilon$.  It is enough to control the mixed products of
the single radial function $u$ during its construction.

\subsubsection{Tilting and compactness of the levels}

Set $a=\Delta/4$, $b=a/(k+1)$, and define the tilted pair
\[
 F=(1-a)u+b\sum_i h_i,\qquad
 G=(1-a)v_0+b\sum_i h_i.
\]
Their gradients still have norm at most one, since all coefficients
are nonnegative and their sum is at most one.  They are nearly
opposite and satisfy the cross bounds with tolerance $\Delta$.
Moreover, for each old defining function,
\[
 \langle\nabla F,\nabla f_j\rangle
 \leq(1-a)\varepsilon/2
          +b\{-1+2\delta+(k-1)\varepsilon\}<0.
\]
Thus the augmented defining family $(F,f_1,\ldots,f_k)$ retains
tightness.  Its Gram matrix has diagonal entries at least
$(1-2\Delta)^2$ and off-diagonal absolute values at most $\Delta$.
For coefficients $c_i$, use $2|c_ic_j|\leq c_i^2+c_j^2$ to bound
its quadratic form below by
$((1-2\Delta)^2-k\Delta)\sum c_i^2>0$.
The augmented map is a submersion, so $F$ restricts to a regular
function on the old fiber.  All augmented Hessians are bounded above
by $\max\{H_0,3/s\}g$.

Regularity alone does not give compact levels.  On the old fiber
through $p$, the opposite-gradient estimate
$|\nabla(f_i+h_i)|\leq2\sqrt\delta$, integrated along a minimizing
geodesic from $p$, gives
$|h_i(x)-h_i(p)|\leq2\sqrt\delta\,d(p,x)$.
Write $B=(1-a)z+b\sum_i h_i(p)$.  Throughout the radial strip,
\[
 |F(x)-(1-a)u(x)-b\sum_i h_i(p)|
       \leq4a\sqrt\delta\,r\leq s/256.
\]
Consequently $|F(x)-B|<s/16$ forces $|u(x)-z|<s/6$.
The latter closed strip is strictly inside the original compact strip.
Intersect it with the closed equations $f_i=f_i(p)$; the result is
a compact subset of the open value tube.  Every closed subinterval
of $(B-s/16,B+s/16)$ therefore has compact inverse image on the old
fiber.  The tilted function is proper over that interval.  The
gradient bounds also leave a uniform ambient buffer of size
\[
 \eta r,\qquad
 \eta=\min\{\tau/98304,\mu/2\}>0,
\]
around the smaller levels used below.  The same $u$ and $F$ work
for every strip center; only the opposite partner may change.

\subsubsection{Uniform geometry of the fibers}

We justify the uniform component, diameter and area estimates needed
for these compact levels.  For this paragraph let there be $k$
defining equations in ambient dimension $n$, with Hessian bound $C$.
On a prefix fiber, consisting of some of these equations, the
restriction of the next function $\varphi$ has gradient equal to the
tangential projection of $\nabla\varphi$.  The Gram matrix
$\mathsf G_{ij}=\langle\nabla f_i,\nabla f_j\rangle$ has positive
diagonal, nonpositive off-diagonal entries and diagonal dominance
margin
\[
 d_0=(1-2\delta)^2-k\delta>0.
\]
The small cross products bound the normal projection; in particular
the restricted gradient has norm between $1/2$ and one.
Write the normal component as $\sum_i a_i\nabla f_i$.
Then $\mathsf Ga=b$, where
$b_i=\langle\nabla\varphi,\nabla f_i\rangle$, and the actual
induced Hessian is
\[
 \nabla_L^2\varphi(v,v)
 =\nabla^2\varphi(v,v)-\sum_i a_i\nabla^2f_i(v,v).
\]
If $-\varepsilon_1\leq b_i\leq0$, diagonal dominance implies
$a_i\leq0$ and $\sum_i|a_i|\leq k\varepsilon_1/d_0$.
For the first assertion, a positive largest coefficient would give
a positive corresponding entry of $\mathsf Ga$; applying the same
maximum-coordinate estimate to $-a$ gives the second.
The upper Hessian bound therefore passes to the fiber with constant
$C+Ck\varepsilon_1/d_0$.  For the chosen tolerances this is at most
$2C$.  This is the role of tightness: its signs allow upper bounds
on the ambient Hessians to control the restriction.

For distance estimates one also needs an opposite function on each
prefix fiber.  If the next pair is $(f_k,h_k)$, replace only its
opposite member by
\[
 \widehat h_k=(1-a_k)h_k+
       \frac{a_k}{k+1}\sum_{i<k}h_i,
       \qquad a_k=2(k+1)\delta.
\]
The defining equations remain the original $f_i=c_i$.
The perturbation has gradient norm at most $2a_k$, and its pairing
with every earlier defining gradient is nonpositive.  Applying the
Gram estimate with enlarged tolerance $\delta+2a_k$ gives, on the
prefix fiber, a pair $\varphi,\psi$ satisfying
\[
 \tfrac12\leq|\nabla\varphi|,|\nabla\psi|\leq1,
 \qquad\langle\nabla\varphi,\nabla\psi\rangle\leq-1/8,
 \qquad\nabla^2\varphi,\nabla^2\psi\leq2C g_L.
\]
The bound $\delta\leq[256(k+1)^2]^{-1}$ leaves the needed
diagonal-dominance margin at every prefix.

Here is the resulting local path estimate.  On a manifold with a
compact intrinsic $40\rho$ ball, suppose a pair $\varphi,\psi$
has the displayed gradient bounds and tangential Hessians bounded
by $H$.  Two points of $\varphi^{-1}(c)$ in the concentric
$\rho$ ball admit a short minimizing geodesic inside the
$3\rho$ ball.  Along it, $|\varphi-c|\leq2\rho$.
Below the level, flow forward by
$X=\nabla\varphi/|\nabla\varphi|^2$ for time $c-\varphi$.
Above it, flow forward by
$Y=\nabla\psi/|\nabla\psi|^2$ until reaching $\varphi=c$.
The latter hitting time is at most $8(\varphi-c)$, since
$d\varphi(Y)\leq-1/8$.  Both vector fields have norm at most two,
so all these trajectories stay inside the compact buffer.
Local ODE continuation thus supplies the full hitting maps.

For a tangent variation $J$ of a normalized gradient trajectory,
the level equation retains $d\varphi(J)=0$ and
\[
 \frac{d}{dt}|J|^2
   =\frac{2\nabla^2\varphi(J,J)}{|\nabla\varphi|^2}
   \leq8H|J|^2.
\]
The same holds with $\psi$ in place of $\varphi$.
The derivative of the first hitting map equals the fixed-time
flow derivative applied to the orthogonal tangential projection of
the initial vector.  For the second hitting map, first project
orthogonally to the $\psi$ level, transport, and then project
obliquely onto the $\varphi$ level along $\nabla\psi$.
Its last projection has norm at most nine by the pairing bound.
Consequently both hitting maps have differential norm at most
$9e^{64H\rho}$ on the required bands.  They agree with the
identity on $\varphi=c$.  Retract each part of the geodesic by the
appropriate map.  The retracted path has length at most
$9e^{64H\rho}$ times its original length.  This also holds when
the path crosses the level infinitely often: both local maps have
the common Lipschitz bound and agree on the crossing set, so their
piecewise composition has the same bound on each parameter interval.

Applying this with $H=2C$ and successively imposing the $k$
unchanged equations gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-fiber-distance}
 d_L(x,y)\leq A^k d_g(x,y),\qquad A=9e^{128C\rho},
 \quad A^k d_g(x,y)<\rho.
\end{equation}
Here the closed ambient $40\rho$ ball about $x$ lies in the
defining domain.  The points also lie in the same path component.  Compact intrinsic
balls in a prefix fiber are available here: its closed intrinsic
ball is a closed subset of its intersection with the compact
ambient $40\rho$ ball, which lies entirely in the defining domain.
Global completeness of the open prefix fiber is unnecessary.

For the small annular levels, rescale lengths upward so that the
compact full fiber lies in an ambient unit ball; the ambient sectional
lower bound remains at least $-1$.  Take $0<\rho\leq1$ so that
every fiber point has the required $40\rho$ buffer, and choose
$q_*=\rho/(4A^k)$.  Bishop--Gromov comparison
bounds the size of a cover by $q_*$ balls by
\[
 N=\left\lceil\frac{V_{-1,n}(3)}{V_{-1,n}(q_*/2)}\right\rceil,
\]
where $V_{-1,n}$ is hyperbolic model-ball volume.
Any two fiber points in one covering ball are joined by
\eqref{eq:rf-fiber-distance} and have intrinsic distance at most
$\rho/2$.  Thus a covering ball meets at most one fiber component,
giving at most $N$ components.  Within a component the intersection
graph of the covering sets is connected; otherwise their unions
would separate the component.  A simple chain has at most $N$
vertices, so the intrinsic diameter is at most $N\rho/2$.
The same local estimate separates distinct components by a fixed
ambient distance.  A sufficiently small open ambient neighborhood
of one component therefore intersects the original fiber in exactly
that component and still contains a fixed-radius closed neighborhood
of it.  Restricting the same defining equations to this neighborhood
produces the compact connected fiber required by the normalized
induction hypothesis, with its induced metric unchanged.

For area, a one-coordinate estimate comes from the normalized
gradient flow and coarea.  On a prefix fiber suppose
$l\leq|\nabla\varphi|\leq1$ and
$\nabla^2\varphi\leq H g$ on level-tangent vectors.
A portion of the level $\varphi=t$ over an ambient radius-$r$
ball is covered by the time-$(t-s)$ flow image of the level
$\varphi=s$ over the radius-$(r+T/l)$ ball, for every
$t-T\leq s\leq t$.  To see surjectivity, continue the trajectory
backward from the target point; its speed at most $1/l$ keeps it
in the compact buffered section.  The forward map on a
$d$-dimensional level has Jacobian at most $e^{dHT/l^2}$.
The area formula and coarea, using $|\nabla\varphi|\leq1$,
therefore give
\[
 \operatorname{area}(\{\varphi=t\}\cap B_g(p,r))
 \leq \frac{e^{dHT/l^2}}{T}
       \operatorname{vol}(\text{prefix fiber}\cap
                              B_g(p,r+T/l)).
\]
All balls here are tested by the ambient distance.  The compact
buffer is retained throughout, so the formula applies to incomplete
prefix fibers as well.
Iterating with $l=1/2$, $H=2C$ and
$T=r/[4(k+1)]$ bounds the actual $k$-codimensional fiber volume by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-fiber-volume}
 \operatorname{vol}(L\cap\overline B_g(p,r))
 \leq\left(\frac{4(k+1)}r\right)^k e^{2nCr}
                    \operatorname{vol}_g(\overline B_g(p,3r/2)).
\end{equation}
The successive radius increments sum to at most $r/2$.
Applying this on a uniformly bounded ambient ball cover and using
the ambient volume comparison gives a uniform total level-area
bound, even if the ambient manifold collapses.  It uses an upper
volume bound, with no injectivity-radius assumption.
This gives a quantitative flow-and-coarea argument for the level-area
bound used in \cite[\S4.5, pp.~9--10]{Petrunin2009}, where the
suggested justification instead uses convergence of corner surfaces.

\subsubsection{Integrating the level estimates}

Shift the tilted value by writing $t=F-B+s/16$ and use
$3s/64\leq t\leq15s/128$.  On these levels the induced-gradient,
area and component-diameter estimates, with common constants, take
the form
\[
\begin{gathered}
\alpha^{-1}\leq|\nabla f|\leq1,\qquad
A(t):=\mu_{L_t}(L_t)\leq\alpha t^{m-1},\\
\operatorname{diam}C\leq\alpha t\quad(C\text{ a component of }L_t),
 \qquad \kappa_i\leq\beta(t)=\alpha/t.
\end{gathered}
\]
Here $f$ is the shifted restriction of $F$ to the old fiber.
There are uniformly finitely many components.  Each is a compact
regular fiber in a buffered ambient neighborhood, so the induction
hypothesis applies to its actual induced metric.  Summing over these
components only enlarges the common constant.
The $\kappa_i$ are the principal curvatures for the increasing-$f$
normal.  Properness and the positive gradient bound make normalized
gradient flow a local trivialization of this compact family of levels.
Differentiating its induced volume density gives
$A'(t)=\int_{L_t}H/|\nabla f|$; in particular $A$ is smooth on
the value interval.

The key estimates on a level, with mean curvature $H$ and
$G=H^2-|B|^2$, are
\[
\begin{split}
 R_L^+&\leq R_{L_t}^++m^2E+2\operatorname{Ric}_L(\mathbf n,\mathbf n)-G,\\
 G&\leq R_{L_t}^++m^2E,\qquad
 K_{L_t}^-\leq E+(H^-+(m-1)\beta)\beta,\\
 \int_{L_t}H^+&\leq(m-1)\beta A(t),\qquad
 \int_{L_t}H^-\leq(m-1)\alpha\beta A(t)-A'(t).
\end{split}
\]
Here $H^+=\max(H,0)$, $H^-=\max(-H,0)$, and $K_{L_t}^-$ is
the negative part of the least sectional curvature of the level.
For the first two inequalities, its tangential scalar trace is
$R_L-2\operatorname{Ric}_L(\mathbf n,\mathbf n)\geq
-(m-1)(m-2)E$.  Combine this with the Gauss identity and
$R_L\geq-m(m-1)E$.  For the third, diagonalize $B$.
Each principal curvature is at least
$H-(m-2)\beta\geq-H^--(m-2)\beta$ and at most $\beta$.
A negative product of two principal curvatures is consequently
at least $-\beta(H^-+(m-1)\beta)$; the Gauss equation gives
the assertion for every tangent two-plane.  Finally
$H^+\leq(m-1)\beta$ and
\[
 A'(t)=\int_{L_t}\frac{H^+}{|\nabla f|}
                   -\frac{H^-}{|\nabla f|}
       \leq\alpha\int_{L_t}H^+-\int_{L_t}H^-.
\]
This proves the last two inequalities without a lower bound on the
individual principal curvatures independent of $H^-$.

Use the continuous nonnegative sectional error
\[
 \widetilde E_t=E+\beta H^-+(m-1)\beta^2
\]
on the actual level.  Scaling each component of diameter at most
$\alpha t$ to bounded diameter gives, after summing components,
\[
 S(t):=\int_{L_t}R_{L_t}^+\leq
 C\left(t^{m-3}+\int_{L_t}\widetilde E_t\right).
\]
The exponent is the level dimension minus two.  The ambient metric,
defining functions, Hessian bounds and buffer are rescaled together;
the previously obtained component bounds keep the normalized induction
parameters uniform.  Equivalently the constant term may be written
as a fixed multiple of $r^{m-3}$, since all these values $t$ are
comparable to the annulus scale $r$.

Write $J(t)=\int_{L_t}E$.  The preceding mean-curvature estimate yields
\[
 \int_{L_t}\widetilde E_t
 \leq J(t)+(m-1)(1+\alpha)\beta(t)^2A(t)-\beta(t)A'(t).
\]
For $0<a<b'$ in the slab interval, coarea and
$\alpha^{-1}\leq|\nabla f|\leq1$ give
\[
 \int_{f^{-1}([a,b'])}R_{L_{f(x)}}^+
 \leq\alpha\int_a^{b'} S(t)\,dt,\qquad
 \int_a^{b'} J(t)\,dt\leq\int_{f^{-1}([a,b'])}E.
\]
The upper speed bound in the second inequality is as important as
the lower speed bound in the first.  Since $\beta=\alpha/t$,
integration by parts gives
\[
 -\int_a^{b'}\beta A'
 =\beta(a)A(a)-\beta(b')A(b')+\int_a^{b'}\beta' A
 \leq\alpha^2 a^{m-2}.
\]
Also $\beta^2A\leq\alpha^3t^{m-3}$ and
$\int_a^{b'} t^{m-3}\,dt\leq (b')^{m-2}/(m-2)$.
Thus there is a uniform $C_1$ such that
\[
 \int_{f^{-1}([a,b'])}R_{L_{f(x)}}^+
 \leq C_1\left((b')^{m-2}+\int_{f^{-1}([a,b'])}E\right).
\]
This is the place where $m\geq3$ is used in the slab integration.

The scalar and Bochner identities now eliminate the normal Ricci term:
\[
\begin{split}
 \int_{f^{-1}([a,b'])}R_L^+
 &\leq2\int_{f^{-1}([a,b'])}R_{L_{f(x)}}^+
       +2m^2\int_{f^{-1}([a,b'])}E\\
 &\quad+2\int_{L_a}H-2\int_{L_{b'}}H.
\end{split}
\]
At the lower endpoint, use $\int H\leq(m-1)\beta A$.
At the upper endpoint, use
$-\int H\leq\int H^-\leq(m-1)\alpha\beta A-A'$.
The area bound therefore gives a uniform $C_2$ with
\[
 \int_{f^{-1}([a,b'])}R_L^+
 \leq C_2\left((b')^{m-2}+\int_{f^{-1}([a,b'])}E\right)-2A'(b').
\]
Retain the enlarged slab $[a,3b/2]$.  Since $A(3b/2)\geq0$
and $A(b)\leq\alpha b^{m-1}$, the mean value theorem supplies
$b'\in(b,3b/2)$ with $-A'(b')\leq2\alpha b^{m-2}$.
The positive scalar integral on $[a,b]$ is at most that on $[a,b']$.
Replacing its error integral by the one on $[a,3b/2]$ proves the
required inner-slab bound.  For the chosen strips,
$a=3s/64$, $b=5s/64$ and $3b/2=15s/128$; thus every remainder
has the required order $r^{m-2}$.

It remains to cover the desired radial annulus by these inner slabs.
Fix a finite $1/262144$-net in the numerical interval $[7/12,3/5]$.
For a point with $113r/96\leq d(p,x)\leq19r/16$, the distance
approximation puts $u(x)/(2\tau r)$ in this interval.  Some net
point therefore supplies a strip center $z$ with $|u(x)-z|\leq s/128$.
On the old fiber $f_i(x)=f_i(p)$, integration of
$|\nabla(f_i+h_i)|\leq2\sqrt\delta$ gives the centered tilt
error at most $4(\Delta/4)\sqrt\delta\,r\leq s/256$.
Thus $|F(x)-B|\leq3s/256$, which places the shifted value
$F(x)-B+s/16$ in $[3s/64,5s/64]$.  The same point lies in the
strip's open domain.  This is a finite cover independent of the
manifold and $r$.  Every enlarged slab lies in the buffered radial
annulus; summing its error integrals costs at most the fixed number
of strips.  We obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-corner-annular}
 \int_{L\cap\{113r/96\leq d(p,\cdot)\leq19r/16\}}R_L^+
 \leq B\left(r^{m-2}+
       \int_{L\cap\{r/2\leq d(p,\cdot)\leq3r\}}E\right).
\end{equation}
Here $0<r\leq r_0(m,k,H_0)$, $\overline{B_g(p,4r)}\subset U$,
and the local ascent condition holds throughout $r/2<d(p,\cdot)<3r$.
The number $v$ and defining-function tolerance depend on the dimensions
before $r_0,B$ are chosen, as in the construction above.

\subsubsection{Concentration at selected centers}

For the second step, suppose the ratios in
\eqref{eq:rf-corner-local} are unbounded.  We first construct a
pointed metric limit, retaining the actual fiber sections.  Write
$d=m+k$ for the ambient dimension.  The lower bound $\sec\geq-1$
implies $\operatorname{Ric}\geq-(d-1)g$.  Relative volume comparison
bounds the number of $\eta$-separated points in each fixed-radius
ball, uniformly along the sequence, for every $\eta>0$.
Maximal separated families are finite nets.  Completeness makes
closed bounded balls compact, and their uniform covering bounds
give compact Gromov--Hausdorff precompactness.  Here convergence
means that the spaces can be embedded isometrically into a common
metric space with Hausdorff error tending to zero, retaining their
basepoints.  A diagonal subsequence gives compact limits $Y_i$
of the closed integer-radius balls, for every positive integer $i$.

The inclusions of source balls also have subsequential limits:
based isometries $e_i:Y_i\longrightarrow Y_{i+1}$.  They satisfy
the important radial property
\[
 B_{Y_{i+1}}(p,i)\subset e_i(Y_i).
\]
Indeed, an approximating sequence for a point strictly inside this
ball eventually belongs to the smaller source ball, and compactness
there supplies its preimage.  Glue these compact stages by their
isometries and complete the union to obtain $X$.  If $n>R+1$,
successive radial properties put every later-stage point of radius
at most $R+1$ in $Y_n$.  The dense union consequently approximates
every point of $\overline B_X(p,R)$ through $Y_n$.  The compact
image of $Y_n$ is closed, so it contains this entire ball.
Thus $X$ is proper.

The source length metrics admit approximate splits: if
$0<r<d(a,b)$ and $\varepsilon>0$, there is $z$ with
\[
 d(a,z)=r,\qquad d(z,b)<d(a,b)-r+\varepsilon.
\]
Put approximating endpoints and split points in a sufficiently large
stage.  Compactness and vanishing realization errors give exact
splits in $X$; repeated midpoint subdivision in the complete space
then gives minimizing segments.  The compact stages themselves
need not be geodesic.  Approximate splits also remove boundary
errors when restricting a realization to smaller open balls:
a point of radius $<R+\eta$ can be moved into the radius-$R$
ball by distance $<3\eta$.  This proves pointed convergence to
the same proper geodesic space $X$ on every bounded region.

A further diagonal gives, for each index $j$, a metric space $Q_j$
containing isometric copies of source and limit balls of radii
tending to infinity, with Hausdorff errors $\varepsilon_j\to0$.
The spaces $Q_j$ need not be compatible with one another.
Let
\[
 E_j=L_j\cap\overline B(p_j,3/2)
\]
with ambient distances.  Take near-images in $X$ of all points of
$E_j$ through $Q_j$, allowing error $\varepsilon_j+1/(j+1)$.
Their closures lie eventually in the fixed compact ball
$\overline B_X(p,5/2)$.  Compactness of its nonempty compact
subsets in Hausdorff distance supplies a further limit $K$.
Combining the two Hausdorff errors gives approximation in both
directions between $E_j$ and $K$.  The radial bound and the
membership $p_j\in E_j$ give
\[
 p\in K\subset\overline B_X(p,3/2).
\]
This construction uses the actual fiber sections; it does not
require convergence of their defining functions.

Every fixed finite configuration in $X$ can now be approximated
in the source manifolds.  The hyperbolic comparison angles depend
continuously on the three distances when the adjacent lengths
are positive.  Thus the lower-curvature four-point comparison
inequality, and each strict-angle packing bound, pass to $X$.
Neither a noncollapse assumption nor an upper sectional bound
has entered this construction.

Choose rates
$v<c_-<c<c_+<1$ and a small angle $0<\theta<\pi/2$ with
$c<\cos(2\theta)$.
At each point count the largest number of arbitrarily short directions
whose pairwise comparison angles exceed $\theta$, and take the minimum
over $K$.  Ambient dimension bounds this integer.  Choose a divergent
sequence for which this minimum is as large as possible among all
divergent sequences of the same dimensions and parameter bounds.

We first explain the punctured ascent neighborhoods and exceptional
centers.  A maximal finite family of directions separated by comparison
angle greater than $\alpha>0$ is an angular net: every further point
makes comparison angle at most $\alpha$ with one member.  Otherwise
it could be added to the family.  Its size is bounded in terms of
ambient dimension and $\alpha$: in the smooth manifolds, Toponogov
comparison bounds comparison angles by angles of unit tangent vectors,
and spherical packing bounds their number.  Every finite strict-angle
configuration transfers to the approximating manifolds, so the same
bound holds in the limit.

Fixing this finite net at $x$, the hyperbolic cosine law shows that,
for $y$ sufficiently close to $x$, one of its endpoints $q$ makes
comparison angle greater than $\pi-2\alpha$ at $y$ between $x$
and $q$.  Choose $\alpha$ so that $c_-<\cos(2\alpha)$.
Hyperbolic chord comparison along a minimizing segment from $y$ to
$q$ then gives local ascent of $d(x,\cdot)$ with rate $c_-$.
Indeed the derivative at the starting point of the model chord
distance is minus the cosine of that comparison angle; it is
strictly greater than $c_-$.  This differentiates the explicit model
distance, without assuming differentiability of distance on $X$.
Thus each $x$ has a positive punctured ascent radius.

Choose $B(x)$ to be the greatest capped radius such that ascent with
rate $c_-$ holds whenever $0<d(x,y)<2B(x)$.
The supremum is admissible because the last inequality is strict:
each such $y$ belongs to the doubled ball of a smaller admissible
radius.  Define the exceptional set
\[
 S=\{y\in K: B(x)/4\leq d(x,y)
                    \text{ for every }x\in K\setminus\{y\}\}.
\]
It is finite.  To see this, take a finite subcover of $K$ by the
balls $B(x,B(x)/4)$.  An exceptional point in one of these balls
must be its center.  Removing the centers from the covering balls
leaves only finitely many other centers to consider; for each
nonexceptional one, add a punctured ball witnessing its failure to
belong to $S$.  These finitely many punctured balls cover $K\setminus S$.
No continuity of $B$ is needed.

At every $y\in S$, choose a common smaller radius $b>0$ on which
the stronger rate $c_+$ holds, and put $\rho=b/16$.
For a source point $x$, define its outermost bad radius by
\[
 a_j(x)=\sup\bigl(\{0\}\cup
   \{d_j(x,z):0<d_j(x,z)\leq b,
          \ d_j(x,\cdot)\text{ fails rate-}c\text{ ascent at }z\}\bigr).
\]
Thus ascent holds at every distance strictly between $a_j(x)$ and
$b$.  Reference lifts $y_j$ of an exceptional point satisfy
$a_j(y_j)\to0$.  Here the strict gap $c<c_+$ is essential.
A local ascent increment in a limit annulus can be chosen so short
that its excess over rate $c$ exceeds the Hessian-comparison Taylor
error.  This is a strict inequality in three distances and persists
in the source.  It implies local ascent there by the same
one-dimensional comparison.  Compactness upgrades the pointwise
statement to a uniform statement on each fixed closed annulus.
Consequently no bad point can remain at a fixed positive distance.

In the compact source fiber section intersected with
$\overline B(y_j,\rho)$, select $q_j$ satisfying
\[
 a_j(q_j)\leq a_j(y_j),\qquad
 a_j(q_j)\leq2a_j(x)\quad
       (x\text{ in that intersection}).
\]
Such a selection does not require continuity of $a_j$.
If it takes the value zero, use a zero point.  Otherwise local
injectivity radii have a positive common lower bound on this compact
set, and every bad point lies beyond that bound.  The infimum of
the bad radii is therefore positive; choose within twice that
infimum, or use $y_j$ if its value is smaller.

In fact $q_j$ converges to the same exceptional point $y$.
Any subsequential limit $x\in K$ has $d(x,y)\leq\rho$.
Since $a_j(q_j)\to0$, passing finite ascent increments to the limit
with the strict loss $c_-<c$ gives rate-$c_-$ ascent on
$0<d(x,z)<b$.  Maximality implies $B(x)\geq b/2$.
If $x\ne y$, the defining exceptional-point inequality gives
$b/8\leq B(x)/4\leq d(x,y)\leq b/16$, a contradiction.
Forward approximation of the marked sections ensures $x\in K$;
this is why the actual fiber sections are retained in the limit.

Remove fixed small neighborhoods of the finitely many exceptional
points.  The remaining compact subset is covered by finitely many
annuli controlled by \eqref{eq:rf-corner-annular}, with small strict
margins at both endpoints.  Their cover transfers to the source
sections.  Inside each exceptional neighborhood, use concentric
annuli about $q_j$ at scales $r_i=r_0\lambda^i$, where
$\lambda=227/228$.  The intervals
$(113r_i/96,19r_i/16)$ overlap and cover all smaller positive radii.
As long as $r_i\geq2a_j(q_j)$, their buffered annuli
$(r_i/2,3r_i)$ have the required ascent.
Choose an integer $N_*$ with $3\lambda^{N_*}<1/2$.
Buffered annuli whose indices differ by at least $N_*$ are disjoint,
so their error integrals have multiplicity at most $N_*$.
Also
\[
 \sum_{i\geq0}r_i^{m-2}
       =\frac{r_0^{m-2}}{1-\lambda^{m-2}}<\infty
       \qquad(m\geq3).
\]
Summing the annular estimates therefore bounds the scalar mass
outside $B(q_j,4a_j(q_j))$ by a constant times the original
denominator $1+\int_{B(p_j,2)\cap L_j}E_j$.
When $a_j(q_j)=0$, the same summation covers the punctured
neighborhood, and the omitted center has zero induced volume.

There are only finitely many selected centers.  Since the original
weighted ratio diverges, one fixed center, along a subsequence, must
satisfy
\[
 \frac{\int_{L_j\cap B(q_j,4a_j(q_j))}R_{L_j}^+}
 {1+\int_{L_j\cap B(q_j,8a_j(q_j))}E_j}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
The local denominator is at most the original one.  If all these
finitely many local ratios were bounded, adding their numerators
and the controlled outside mass would bound the original ratio.
After discarding finitely many terms, the selected radius is positive;
a radius-zero open ball has zero numerator.  Put $r_j=a_j(q_j)>0$.
We have obtained both $r_j\to0$ and near-minimality throughout
the retained neighborhood.

\subsubsection{Rescaling and angular rank growth}

If the chosen radius is $r_j\to0$, rescale lengths by $(4r_j)^{-1}$,
metrics by $(4r_j)^{-2}$, defining functions by $(4r_j)^{-1}$, and the
curvature error by $(4r_j)^2$.  The paired-gradient inequalities and
tightness persist; the upper Hessian bound improves, and ambient
sectional curvature remains at least $-1$ for large $j$.
An $m$-dimensional scalar integral and its error integral both multiply
by $(4r_j)^{2-m}$.  Since $m\geq2$ and $4r_j\leq1$, the rescaled
weighted ratio is no smaller than the original shrinking-ball ratio.
It therefore still diverges.  Centers are chosen within the old
radius-$4/3$ section and $r_j\leq1/64$, so the new radius-$3/2$
section lies inside the old marked section.  Both forward and backward
approximation of these sections survive the pointed limit.

Here is why the rank increases at every point of the new marked
section.  Fix such a point.  Backward approximation supplies source
points $p_j$ in the old fiber sections whose distances from $q_j$
are $O(r_j)$.  In the old scale both sequences converge to the same
exceptional point $y$.  In particular $p_j$ eventually lies in the
neighborhood where near-minimality was imposed, so
$a_j(p_j)\geq r_j/2$.  The stronger ascent at $y$, and the strict
gap $c<c_+$, also give $a_j(p_j)\to0$ by the moving-center version
of the annular stability argument above.  The supremum defining
$a_j(p_j)$ therefore supplies a bad point $z_j$ with
\[
 r_j/4<d(p_j,z_j)\leq a_j(p_j)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Its distance in the new scale is greater than $1/16$.  It can
therefore contribute a direction visible in the new limit.

Lift an old small-angle configuration at $y$ to points $q_{j,i}$
at distances from $p_j$ tending to $b_i>0$.  Put
$a_j=d(p_j,z_j)$, $b_{j,i}=d(p_j,q_{j,i})$ and
$e_{j,i}=d(z_j,q_{j,i})$.  The hyperbolic change-of-vertex cosine
identity and failure of ascent give
\[
 \cos\widetilde\angle_{-1}(z_jp_jq_{j,i})
 \leq\frac{\sinh a_j\cosh b_{j,i}+c\sinh e_{j,i}}
              {\cosh a_j\sinh b_{j,i}}\longrightarrow c.
\]
Thus the direction to $z_j$ is separated from all the old ones by
an angle strictly greater than $\theta$.  Choose an intermediate
angle $\beta>\theta$ within every available strict margin and truncate
all the segments at the same small rescaled radius $t$.
For two truncated endpoints, their original separation $D_j$ obeys
\[
 \sinh(D_j/2)\geq\sin(\beta/2)\sinh(t\,4r_j).
\]
In the limit their separation is at least $2t\sin(\beta/2)$.
Hyperbolic comparison angles are not unchanged by rescaling, so choose
$t$ additionally small enough that
\[
 \sinh(t\sin(\beta/2))>\sin(\theta/2)\sinh t.
\]
The limiting comparison angles are then strictly greater than $\theta$.
Take also $t<1/16$, so the bad-point segment can be truncated at
that radius.  This construction works for arbitrarily small $t$.
It adds one direction to every old configuration at $y$, and the
rank at $y$ is at least the minimum rank of the old marked section.
Since the new marked point was arbitrary, the new minimum rank is
strictly larger.  This contradicts its maximal choice and proves
the local weighted bound.

Finite localization and scaling of a compact diameter-one fiber,
retaining its ambient buffer, recover
\eqref{eq:rf-corner-normalized} in dimension $m$.
Indeed, set $\rho_0=\min(1,\eta/2)$.  About each fiber point,
rescale the ambient metric by $\rho_0^{-2}$ and the defining
functions by $\rho_0^{-1}$.  The radius-two ball remains in the
defining domain, the sectional lower bound remains at least $-1$,
and the upper Hessian bound becomes $\rho_0H_0\leq H_0$.
The local assertion gives
\[
 \int_{L\cap B_g(p,\rho_0)}R_L^+
 \leq B\left(\rho_0^{m-2}
             +\int_{L\cap B_g(p,2\rho_0)}E\right)
 \leq B\left(1+\int_{L\cap B_g(p,2\rho_0)}E\right).
\]
Intrinsic diameter at most one puts the fiber in an ambient ball
of radius two.  Ambient Bishop packing supplies a cover by at most
$\lceil V_{-1,m+k}(6)/V_{-1,m+k}(\rho_0/4)\rceil$ balls of
radius $\rho_0$ centered on the fiber.  Their error integrals have
overlap at most this same number.  Summing gives the uniform
normalized constant.
Induction from $m=2$, simultaneously over all codimensions, is now
closed: each annular step used fiber dimension $m-1$ and codimension
$k+1$.  In codimension zero the local ratio is exactly
\[
 \frac{\int_{B(p,1)}\max(R,0)\,d\mu}{1+\mu(B(p,2))}.
\]
Thus the weighted bound, followed by Bishop volume comparison on the
double ball, gives the required scalar-integral bound.
In dimension three the same annular-concentration argument can be
started directly from compact surface levels, without extra defining
functions.  This is the three-dimensional specialization used for
the following reduction.  For a complete surface take its product with
a flat line.  Scalar curvature pulls back from the surface and
product volume is the product measure.  The width-one cylinder
$B(p,1/2)\times(-1/2,1/2)$ lies inside the product unit ball.
Since $R\geq-2$, $R^+\leq R+2$, so the three-dimensional estimate and
Bishop volume comparison bound the integral of $R^+$ on the cylinder,
hence on the surface half-ball.  A dimension-dependent finite covering
by half-balls bounds the integral on a surface unit ball.  Curvature
vanishes in dimensions zero and one.
The regular-slab and concentration mechanisms are
\cite[Sections 3.1--3.7, pp.~5--7; Sections 4.3--4.7,
pp.~8--14]{Petrunin2009}.

\subsection{From volume to curvature}

Along a flow with nonnegative curvature operator, distances decrease
and unit balls expand as sets, whereas $\partial_t d\mu=-R\,d\mu$.
Apply the latter identity first to the fixed set $B_{g(a)}(x,1)$.
Its closure is compact by completeness, so finitely many coordinate
charts supply uniform domination for differentiation of the metric
densities.  A disjoint measurable subdivision subordinate to these
charts gives
\[
 \frac{d}{dt}\mu_{g(t)}(B_{g(a)}(x,1))
       =-\int_{B_{g(a)}(x,1)}R(\cdot,t)\,d\mu_{g(t)}
       \geq-P_n.
\]
Here $R\geq0$, the fixed set lies in $B_{g(t)}(x,1)$, and the
static scalar-integral estimate applies on that larger ball.
Integration and inclusion in the terminal ball give
\[
 \mu_{g(t)}(B_{g(t)}(x,1))
       \geq\mu_{g(a)}(B_{g(a)}(x,1))-P_n(t-a).
\]
Thus an initial uniform bound $v>0$ leaves a bound $\nu=v/2$
for $a\leq t\leq a+\delta$, where
$0<\delta\leq v/(2P_n)$ and the interval lies inside the flow domain.
The static estimate is a substantive geometric input: its proof uses
regular level hypersurfaces, integrated Gauss and Bochner identities,
and a concentration contradiction with dimension induction.
The moving-volume application is
\cite[Theorem 4.2 and equations (4.2)--(4.3), pp.~3160--3161]{CabezasRivasWilking2015}.

If $(t-a)R(x,t)$ were unbounded on this right interval,
point picking followed by parabolic rescaling would produce a complete,
nonflat ancient limit with nonnegative curvature operator, a uniform
spacetime full-curvature bound, and
\[
             \mu(B(x_*,r))\geq\nu_*r^n\quad\hbox{for every }r>0,
             \qquad \nu_*=\nu/4^n>0
\]
on its zero slice.  The controlled cylinders needed here have a specific
form.  For $A_k=k+1$ select $(p_k,\tau_k)$ and $Q_k=R(p_k,\tau_k)>0$
such that
\[
\begin{split}
 Q_k&>[64(n+8)A_k]^2,\qquad Q_k(\tau_k-a)\geq2A_k,\\
 R(x,t)&\leq4Q_k\quad\text{if }\tau_k-A_k/Q_k\leq t\leq\tau_k,
 \quad d_{g(\tau_k)}(p_k,x)<A_k/\sqrt{Q_k}.
\end{split}
\]
Here is the point selection and the subsequent conversion to a fixed
spatial cylinder.  Temporarily fix $A>0$, put
$\alpha=2A$ and $L=8(n+8)A$, and choose $(x_1,t_1)$ where
$(t_1-a)R(x_1,t_1)$ exceeds both $2A$ and
$[64(n+8)A]^2(b-a)$.  Use $p=x_1$ as reference point and restrict
selection to
\[
 \mathcal S=\{(z,s):a\leq s\leq b,\ d_{g(s)}(p,z)<1,\
                         R(z,s)(s-a)\geq\alpha\}.
\]
Every such point lies in
$[a,b]\times\overline B_{g(b)}(p,1)$, since distances decrease.
This is one compact set on which the smooth scalar curvature is
bounded.  Set $q(z,s)=\sqrt{R(z,s)}$ and
$d(z,s)=d_{g(s)}(p,z)$.  If a selected point $(y,\tau)$ fails
the desired doubling property, replace it by $(z,s)\in\mathcal S$
with $s\leq\tau$, $d(z,s)\leq d(y,\tau)+L/q(y,\tau)$ and
$q(z,s)>2q(y,\tau)$.  The quantity $d+2L/q$ does not increase:
\[
 d(z,s)+\frac{2L}{q(z,s)}
 \leq d(y,\tau)+\frac{2L}{q(y,\tau)}.
\]
Repeated doubling cannot continue on the compact set.  Thus it
terminates at $(y,\tau)$, with $Q=R(y,\tau)\geq R(x_1,t_1)>0$,
$Q(\tau-a)\geq\alpha$, and
\[
 d+\frac{2L}{\sqrt Q}\leq
       \frac{2L}{\sqrt{R(x_1,t_1)}}<\frac14,\qquad
 d=d_{g(\tau)}(p,y).
\]
At all earlier points of $\mathcal S$ of radial distance at most
$d+L/\sqrt Q$, scalar curvature is at most $4Q$.
If a point at a time
$s\in[\tau-\alpha/(2Q),\tau]$ is outside $\mathcal S$ but still
inside the reference ball, its failure of the threshold gives
$R(z,s)<\alpha/(s-a)\leq2Q$.  Therefore the $4Q$ bound holds
throughout this shorter interval in the moving ball
$B_{g(s)}(p,d+L/\sqrt Q)$.

The distinction between this moving ball and a terminal ball matters.
The required distance estimate has an additive, rather than a
distance-proportional, error.  On a unit-speed minimizing segment
of length $\ell$, with $\operatorname{Ric}\leq\Lambda g$, use
parallel fields multiplied by
\[
 \phi(u)=(1-e^{-\lambda u})(1-e^{-\lambda(\ell-u)}),
 \qquad\lambda>0.
\]
The nonnegative index forms, summed in an orthonormal frame, give
$\int\phi^2\operatorname{Ric}(\gamma',\gamma')\leq
n\int(\phi')^2$.  Direct integration of the exponentials gives
$\int(\phi')^2\leq2\lambda$ and
$\int(1-\phi^2)\leq4/\lambda$.  Consequently
\[
       \int\operatorname{Ric}(\gamma',\gamma')
                    \leq2n\lambda+4\Lambda/\lambda.
\]
For a distance at a cut point, split a minimizing segment at an
interior point and use a smooth inverse exponential branch on the
terminal part.  Keep these paths fixed while varying the metric.
The sum of their lengths is a touching upper support for distance,
and its time derivative is the negative sum of the two Ricci
integrals.  It is therefore at least
$-C_\lambda$, where $C_\lambda=4n\lambda+8\Lambda/\lambda$.
The Ricci bound is needed only along the two segments, which lie in
the prescribed moving ball.

This support bound implies backward confinement: if the terminal
distance is at most $r_0$ and
$r_0+C_\lambda(\tau-s_0)<r_1$, then
\[
 d_{g(s)}(p,z)\leq r_0+C_\lambda(\tau-s)<r_1
                   \quad(s_0\leq s\leq\tau),
\]
provided the Ricci bound is available in $B_{g(s)}(p,r_1)$.
For completeness, take $c>C_\lambda$ with the same strict margin.
If the inequality with $c$ failed, the continuous function
$d_{g(s)}(p,z)+cs$ would have a minimum after the offending time
and at distance less than $r_1$.  Its touching upper support
would also have a minimum from the left there, but its derivative
would be at least $c-C_\lambda>0$, a contradiction.  Let
$c\downarrow C_\lambda$.  Distance is continuous here by local
metric comparison on compact neighborhoods of the relevant paths.
This argument also shows that the use of the moving-ball bound
does not assume the confinement it is proving.

Apply it with $\lambda=\sqrt Q$, $\Lambda=4Q$,
$r_0=d+L/(4\sqrt Q)$, $r_1=d+L/\sqrt Q$, and
$s_0=\tau-\alpha/(2Q)$.  Since $(4n+32)\alpha=L$,
\[
 r_0+C_\lambda(\tau-s_0)
       =d+\frac{3L}{4\sqrt Q}<r_1.
\]
The terminal ball about $y$ of radius $L/(4\sqrt Q)$ is
contained in the terminal ball about $p$ of radius $r_0$.
It is therefore controlled throughout this backward interval.
Now $L/4\geq A$ and $\alpha/(2Q)=A/Q$ give the stated cylinder.
The argument follows the threshold-region and confinement steps of
\cite[Corollary 45.1(a), equations (45.5)--(45.8),
pp.~2684--2685]{KleinerLott2013}, with explicit conservative constants.

Under $\widetilde g_k(s)=Q_kg(\tau_k+s/Q_k)$ the cylinders have radius
$A_k$, backward depth $A_k$, scalar bound four, and base scalar one.
Nonnegative curvature operator gives a full-curvature bound $4n^2$.
For each fixed $r$, $r/\sqrt{Q_k}\leq1$ eventually.  Bishop--Gromov
comparison from the source unit-ball bound then gives a lower bound
for the rescaled $r$-balls, with coefficient $\nu/2^n$ sufficient for
the comparison construction.  Derivative estimates on interior
cylinders give a common bound on $|\partial_sR|$ near the basepoint
for $-1\leq s\leq0$.  Choose $0<\delta<1$ so small that the base
scalar at $s=-\delta$ is still at least $1/2$.  Replace $s$ by
$t-\delta$ before taking the limit.  Thus the nonflat base slice is at
$t=0$ inside the interval $(-\infty,\delta)$.
Section~\ref{sec:rf-compactness} gives compatible limits on every fixed
radius and backward time range.  Their diagonal limit is complete and
ancient.  Here are the two volume comparisons needed for this limit.
Write $v_0=\nu/2^n$ and $D=4n+32$.  For each fixed $r>0$ choose
$H=\max\{r,D+1\}$.  Eventually the controlled cylinder contains
all the balls used below.  The additive distance estimate, with
scalar bound four and $\lambda=1$, implies
\[
 B_{\widetilde g_k(0)}(p_k,H)
       \subset B_{\widetilde g_k(-\delta)}(p_k,2H),
\]
since $D\delta<H$.  The volume of the fixed terminal ball is no
smaller at $-\delta$ than at zero.  Bishop--Gromov comparison on
the earlier slice therefore gives
\[
 \mu_{\widetilde g_k(-\delta)}(B(p_k,r))
 \geq \left(\frac r{2H}\right)^n
       \mu_{\widetilde g_k(-\delta)}(B(p_k,2H))
 \geq \frac{v_0}{2^n}r^n=\nu_*r^n.
\]
The index after which this holds may depend on $r$; no uniformity
over unbounded radii is required.

To transfer this lower bound to the complete limit, one must also
cover source balls by the convergence maps.  Fix $A>0$.  The closed
limit ball of radius $4A$ is compact and eventually belongs to one
fixed convergence domain.  Metric convergence gives inverse
differentials of norm at most two there.  Every source point at
distance less than $A$ can be joined to the base by a path of length
less than $A$.  Such a path cannot first leave the image of the
limit $4A$-ball: up to its first exit its inverse has length less
than $2A$, whereas an exit point is at limit distance $4A$.
Compactness of the closed ball and openness of the embedding image
justify the first-exit argument, including continuation of the
inverse at that endpoint.  Thus the source $A$-ball is covered.

For every fixed $1<C<2$, apply coverage to the source $3A$-ball.
Metric convergence on the closure of its fixed inverse domain gives
inverse differentials of norm at most $C$.  Almost minimizing paths
between points of the source $A$-ball can be chosen within the
$3A$-ball, since their lengths are less than $2A$.  Hence inverse
distances are at most $C$ times source distances, and the inverse
points lie in the limit $2A$-ball.  Conversely, compare forward
differentials on the compact closure of the limit $6A$-ball.
Almost minimizing paths between these inverse points stay in that
ball and give the reverse distance inequality.  Thus both distance
distortions are at most $C$, for all sufficiently large $k$.
Normalized
$n$-dimensional Hausdorff measure, which is the Riemannian volume,
then satisfies
\[
 \mu_k(B(p_k,A))\leq C^n\mu_\infty(B(p_\infty,CA)).
\]
Indeed the inverse image lies in the limit $CA$-ball, and the
reverse Lipschitz inequality bounds source Hausdorff measure by
$C^n$ times its image measure.  Set $A=r/C$ and use the source
lower bound to obtain
$\nu_*(r/C)^n\leq C^n\mu_\infty(B(p_\infty,r))$.
Letting $C\downarrow1$ proves the asserted lower bound with its
unchanged coefficient $\nu_*$.  No extension past the original
terminal time is used in this construction.

For clarity, the ancient-model input required at this point is:
a complete connected noncompact ancient flow in dimension
$n\geq2$, with nonnegative curvature operator, one uniform full-curvature
bound on its ancient time range, and a positive parabolic noncollapsing
constant, has zero asymptotic volume ratio provided $R(x,0)>0$ somewhere.
Here parabolic noncollapsing means that a closed backward ball cylinder
$B_{g(t)}(p,r)\times[t-r^2,t]$ with
$|\operatorname{Rm}|\leq r^{-2}$ has terminal ball volume at least
$\kappa r^n$.  This is Proposition~\ref{prop:bounded-ancient-zero-volume} in the
ancient-solutions chapter.  Its proof uses
Proposition~\ref{prop:rf-bounded-harnack}, before the present
slice-bounded theorem is available.

The limit above contradicts that input.  Bishop--Gromov comparison gives
a positive asymptotic volume ratio from the displayed lower bound.
The volume ratio is nonincreasing in ancient time.  To prove this,
first take an interior slab $[s,t]$ on which $R\leq S$.
The additive distance estimate above, with $\lambda=1$, gives
\[
 B_{g(t)}(p,r)\subset
 B_{g(s)}(p,r+(4n+8S)(t-s)+1).
\]
The volume of the fixed terminal ball decreases with time because
$R\geq0$.  Divide the resulting volume inequality by $r^n$ and
let $r\to\infty$.  The additive change in radius has asymptotic
factor one, proving $\operatorname{AVR}(g(t))\leq
\operatorname{AVR}(g(s))$.
At the included time zero, a full-curvature bound $K$ gives
two-sided tangent-norm comparison with factor
$C=e^{n^3K|t-s|}$.  Distances change by at most $C$, and
volumes by at most $C^n$.  Thus the volume ratios at the two
times differ by a multiplicative factor at most $C^{2n}$.
Letting the interior time tend to zero extends monotonicity to
that endpoint.  Neither this step nor the interior comparison
uses Harnack.

It follows that every earlier slice has volume ratio at least the
positive terminal value $\theta$.  Bishop--Gromov and basepoint
comparison give, at every center and radius on every earlier slice,
$\mu(B(x,r))\geq(\theta/2^n)r^n$.  This implies the required
parabolic noncollapsing statement, including the terminal slice,
even without using its curvature antecedent.  The zero-volume-ratio
theorem is therefore applicable.  The fixed-set volume identity
used here has sign $-\int R\,d\mu$; the corresponding displayed
identity in \cite[proof of Corollary 44.1, p.~2683]{KleinerLott2013}
has a sign misprint.
This contradiction bounds $(t-a)R$.  We spell out the local estimate
which removes its apparent singularity at $a$.  In dimension $n\geq2$
there is $C_n>0$ with the following property.  Suppose $[a,b]$ lies
inside a complete flow's time interval, $\operatorname{Ric}\geq0$,
$r>0$, $K\geq1$, and
\[
 R(x,a)\leq r^{-2}\quad(x\in B_{g(a)}(p,r)),\qquad
 R(x,t)\leq K/(t-a)\quad
 (a<t\leq b,\ x\in B_{g(t)}(p,r)).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-chen-local}
 R(x,t)\leq\frac{e^{C_nK}}{(r-d_{g(t)}(p,x))^2}
 \quad(a\leq t\leq b,\ x\in B_{g(t)}(p,r)).
\end{equation}
To see the quantitative mechanism, fix a smooth nonincreasing profile
$0\leq\chi\leq1$, equal to one on $(-\infty,1]$ and zero on
$[2,\infty)$.  Choose positive constants $a_0,b_0,c_0$ such that
$(\chi')^2\leq a_0\chi$, $\chi''\geq-b_0$ and
$\chi'\geq-c_0$.  Such a profile can be obtained by squaring a
smooth cutoff; its compact transition region bounds the derivatives.
Set $N=c_0(4n+32)$, $S=b_0+2a_0+2(n-1)c_0$,
$D=4(N+2S)+1$ and $L=De^{8K}$.
If the claimed intermediate estimate
$R(x,t)\leq4L^2/(r-d_{g(t)}(p,x))^2$ fails, scalar point selection gives
$(y,\tau)$ with $Q=R(y,\tau)\geq R(x,t)>0$ and $d=d_{g(\tau)}(p,y)$,
such that $d+2L/\sqrt Q<r$ and $R\leq4Q$ whenever
$a\leq s\leq\tau$ and $d_{g(s)}(p,z)\leq d+L/\sqrt Q$.
This time the doubling selection uses the whole moving $r$-ball
region, with no reciprocal-time threshold.  It terminates by the
same compact terminal-ball argument, preserving $d+2L/\sqrt Q$.
The selected time is greater than $a$, by the initial bound, and
$Q(\tau-a)\leq K$.

Set $\sigma=L/(2\sqrt Q)$ and
$u(z,s)=\chi((d_{g(s)}(p,z)-d)/\sigma)R(z,s)$.
We justify comparison for this possibly nonsmooth cutoff.
At a noncentral positive maximum, the Calabi upper distance support
$\rho$ has $|\nabla\rho|=1$,
$\Delta\rho\leq2(n-1)/\rho$, and
$\partial_s\rho\geq-(4n+32)\sqrt Q$ at contact.
The last inequality uses $\operatorname{Ric}\leq4Qg$ on the
selected moving ball and the preceding Ricci-integral estimate.
The smooth function $\phi=\chi((\rho-d)/\sigma)$ is a touching
lower support for the actual cutoff.  The chain rules give
\[
 \partial_s\phi-\Delta\phi+
                  2\frac{|\nabla\phi|^2}{\phi}\leq H,
 \qquad
 H=\frac{c_0(4n+32)\sqrt Q}{\sigma}
                +\frac{b_0+2a_0+2(n-1)c_0}{\sigma^2}.
\]
In the transition region $\rho\geq\sigma$, so the Laplacian
distance denominator is absorbed by $\sigma^{-2}$.  On the flat
region $\chi'=0$, including its boundary by smoothness.  At the
center the cutoff is locally one and the scalar equation applies
directly.

Since $R\geq0$, the product $\phi R$ touches $u$ from below and
inherits its spatial maximum.  There
$\phi\nabla R=-R\nabla\phi$ and $\Delta(\phi R)\leq0$.
Using $\partial_sR=\Delta R+2|\operatorname{Ric}|^2$ and
$|\operatorname{Ric}|^2\leq R^2$ gives
\[
 \partial_s(\phi R)
 \leq2R(\phi R)+R\left(\partial_s\phi-\Delta\phi+
                  2\frac{|\nabla\phi|^2}{\phi}\right)
 \leq8Q u+4QH.
\]
All cutoff supports lie in the compact terminal closed ball of
radius $d+2\sigma$, and distance and scalar curvature are jointly
continuous there.  To prove the associated ODE comparison, take a
rate $\beta>8Q$ and maximize
$e^{-\beta(s-a)}(u+H/2)$ on this compact spacetime set.
A value larger than its initial upper bound occurs at $s>a$ with
$u>0$.  The touching lower time support has a local maximum
relative to earlier times, so its derivative is nonnegative.
The displayed differential inequality makes that derivative at most
$e^{-\beta(s-a)}(8Q-\beta)(u+H/2)<0$, a contradiction.
Let $\beta\downarrow8Q$.  Initially $u\leq r^{-2}$, so at the
selected center where $\chi=1$ this proves
\[
 Q\leq e^{8Q(\tau-a)}(r^{-2}+H/2)-H/2.
\]
Here $H=N\sqrt Q/\sigma+S/\sigma^2$ as above.
Since $L\geq4e^{8K}(N+2S)$ and $L\geq1$,
\[
 e^{8K}H=e^{8K}(2N/L+4S/L^2)Q\leq Q/2.
\]
Thus $Q\leq2e^{8K}/r^2$, contrary to $Q>4L^2/r^2$.
Finally $4L^2\leq\exp((16+\log(4D^2))K)$ proves
\eqref{eq:rf-chen-local} with that value of $C_n$.
Choosing $r$ uniformly from the initial scalar bound and applying
\eqref{eq:rf-chen-local} with each point as center gives a uniform
right-slab scalar bound.  Nonnegative curvature operator converts it
to a full-curvature bound.  This is the scalar specialization of
\cite[Theorem 3.1, manuscript pp.~10--11]{Chen2009}: that theorem
estimates the full curvature norm without a nonnegative Ricci
assumption and localizes its square.  Here nonnegative Ricci
curvature permits the simpler scalar reaction bound and compact
terminal-ball comparison just proved.
In dimensions zero and one the curvature vanishes, so this reduction
is unnecessary.

For completeness, the passage from these local right-hand bounds to a
whole compact slab $[a,b]$ uses the bounded-slab Harnack inequality.
Let $b_*$ be the supremum of the $c\in[a,b]$ for which curvature is
bounded on $[a,c]$ on the component.  An initial right bound supplies
$c_0>a$ and $K_0$.  For each $c<b_*$, the bounded-slab inequality
with $v=0$ implies that $(t-a)R(x,t)$ is nondecreasing up to $c$.
Pointwise time continuity therefore gives
\[
       (t-a)R(x,t)\leq(b_*-a)R(x,b_*)\quad(a<t\leq b_*).
\]
The original slice bound at $b_*$ bounds $R(x,b_*)$ by one constant
$K_*$ for all $x$.  On $[c_0,b_*]$ it follows that
$R(x,t)\leq(b_*-a)K_* /(c_0-a)$; combine this with the initial
bound on $[a,c_0]$.  Nonnegative curvature operator converts this to
a full-curvature bound.  Thus $b_*$ itself belongs to the bounded
interval.  If $b_*<b$, the local right bound at $b_*$ extends it,
contradicting the definition of the supremum.  Hence $b_*=b$.
Apply the bounded-slab Hamilton block estimate on each such component,
then send its left endpoint to $T_0$.  This proves the stated finite
inequality once the geometric volume inputs have been supplied.

This reduction is an additional dependency of the stated slice-bounded
theorem.  The bounded-ancient volume theorem belongs to the
ancient-model analysis.  Its use must respect the bounded-flow
dependency order specified above; slice bounds are not automatically
uniform by time continuity.

\subsection{Path integration and ancient times}

Assuming the differential estimate, apply it with $v=\gamma'/2$.
Nonnegative Ricci curvature implies
$\operatorname{Ric}(\gamma',\gamma')\leq R|\gamma'|^2$, so
for $f(s)=R(\gamma(s),s)$,
\[
 f'(s)\geq-\left(\frac1{s-T_0}
                       +\tfrac12|\gamma'(s)|^2_{g(s)}\right)f(s).
\]
Multiplication by
$(s-T_0)\exp(\tfrac12\int_{t_1}^s|\gamma'|^2)$ and integration proves
\eqref{eq:rf-harnack-path}.  This proof never divides by $R$, so it
also covers its zero set.  The finite-time factors must be retained.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:rf-ancient-harnack}
Let a Ricci flow on a connected $n$-manifold be defined for $t\leq0$.
Assume complete slices, nonnegative curvature operator, slice-wise
bounded curvature operator, and nonflatness on every slice
($\forall t\leq0\ \exists x:\ |\operatorname{Rm}|(x,t)\ne0$).
Then
\[
 \partial_tR+2dR(v)+2\operatorname{Ric}(v,v)\geq0
 \quad(t\leq0),
\]
with the derivative at zero taken from the left.  For every
$t_1<t_2\leq0$ and $C^1$ path,
\[
 R(\gamma(t_2),t_2)\geq R(\gamma(t_1),t_1)e^{-E(\gamma)/2}.
\]
In particular scalar curvature at a fixed spatial point is
nondecreasing in time.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For $t<0$ restrict to $(T_0,0)$ and let $T_0\to-\infty$ in
\eqref{eq:rf-harnack}; $R/(t-T_0)$ tends to zero and the derivative is
the same actual scalar derivative for all these restrictions.
Smoothness up to zero passes the inequality to the left derivative
there.  The previous integrating factor without the initial-time
factor proves the path estimate, including its endpoint by continuity.
Take $v=0$ for monotonicity.  These deductions also apply directly to
uniformly bounded ancient flows using only the bounded-slab theorem,
as required in the preceding reduction.
\end{proof}
Compare \cite[Theorem 4.40, p.~82]{MT2007}.

\section{Constructing harmonic coordinates}
\label{sec:rf-harmonic-coordinates}

Curvature bounds do not directly bound derivatives of the metric in
an arbitrary coordinate system.  Harmonic coordinates provide a
controlled choice.  We need the construction both for local geometric
analysis and, in dimension two, for the annular conformal coordinates
in Section~\ref{sec:annular-uniformization}.  The construction begins
on a radial coordinate ball; no global completeness assumption enters.
In this section $B_s$ denotes the Euclidean ball of radius $s$
centered at zero; other centers are displayed explicitly.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:rf-harmonic-radius}
Fix $n\geq2$, $R>0$, and $K\geq0$.  There are $r>0$ and $H>0$,
depending only on these data, with the following property.  Suppose
$g$ is a smooth positive metric on $\mathbb R^n$ satisfying
\[
 \tfrac14|v|^2\leq g_x(v,v)\leq\tfrac94|v|^2,
 \qquad |\operatorname{Rm}_g|(x)\leq K\quad(x\in B_R),
 \qquad g_x(x,w)=\langle x,w\rangle\quad(x,w\in\mathbb R^n).
\]
There is a smooth local diffeomorphism $e:B_{2r}\to B_R$ with
$e(0)=0$ such that $h=e^*g$ extends to a smooth positive metric on
$\mathbb R^n$.  On $B_{2r}$ the standard coordinate functions
satisfy $\Delta_h y_i=0$, and
\[
 \tfrac19 I\leq h\leq9I,\qquad
 |Dh(x)|\leq H,\qquad
 |Dh(x)-Dh(y)|\leq H|x-y|^{1/2}.
\]
Moreover $d_g(0,e(x))\leq3|x|$.  The derivatives and norms of
the coefficient field $h$ in this statement are Euclidean ones.
\end{theorem}

The comparison with the usual harmonic-coordinate theorem is local:
\cite[Proposition 26.49, Step 3, equations (26.146)--(26.150),
pp.~383--384]{ChowEtAl2010} obtains such coordinates on an exponential
lift by invoking Jost--Karcher and DeTurck--Kazdan.  We develop the
radial-metric construction used here.  Its constants precede the
choice of $g$; qualitative smoothness constants used along the way
need not be uniform.

\subsection{Radial transport and weak harmonic replacements}
\label{sec:rf-weak-harmonic-replacements}

Write $T(x)v$ for parallel transport of $v$ along $t\mapsto tx$,
$0\leq t\leq1$.  The Gauss identity implies $g_0=I$ and makes
these radial lines affinely parametrized geodesics.  Thus $T(x)$
is an isometry from the Euclidean inner product to $g_x$.  Varying
the radial line in direction $w$ and commuting the two covariant
derivatives gives
\[
 T(x)^{-1}\nabla_w(T(\cdot)v)|_x
 =\int_0^1T(tx)^{-1}
       \operatorname{Rm}_{tx}(x,tw)T(tx)v\,dt.
\]
Here the curvature is regarded as its $(1,3)$ tensor with the
same convention as the connection commutator.  The metric upper
bound gives, with $A=3/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-radial-frame}
 |\nabla_w(T(\cdot)v)|_g\leq KA^2|x||w||v|,
 \qquad |T(x)^{-1}w-w|\leq KA^2|x|^2|w|.
\end{equation}
For the second inequality, differentiate the radial coframe and
use torsion freeness to interchange the radial and transverse
directions.  Its difference from the identity is the integral of
the frame connection evaluated on $(w,tx)$.  The first bound,
integrated along the same segment, gives the second.  Consequently
$g(v,v)=|T^{-1}v|^2$ differs from $|v|^2$ by at most
$\theta(2+\theta)|v|^2$ wherever $KA^2|x|^2\leq\theta$.

Put $a(x)=\sqrt{\det g(x)}\,g(x)^{-1}$.  On a sufficiently
small ball $B_{4s}$ the preceding estimate makes $a-I$ smaller
than any prescribed $\varepsilon>0$.  This choice depends only
on $n,R,K,\varepsilon$.  On $B_{2s}$ seek
$U_i=x_i+w_i$, with $w_i\in H^1_0(B_{2s})$, satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-harmonic-replacement}
 \int a\nabla w_i\cdot\nabla\phi
   =-\int(a-I)e_i\cdot\nabla\phi
       \quad(\phi\in H^1_0(B_{2s})).
\end{equation}
All integrals here are with respect to Euclidean measure.  The
subtracted identity contributes zero because $\phi$ has zero
boundary trace.  Ellipticity and the ball Poincare inequality
make the left side a coercive bounded form on the zero-boundary
energy completion.  The Riesz representation theorem therefore
constructs a unique $w_i$.  Testing with $w_i$ and then applying
Poincare gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-correction-energy}
 \int_{B_{2s}}|\nabla w_i|^2\leq C\varepsilon^2s^n,
 \qquad \int_{B_{2s}}|w_i|^2\leq C\varepsilon^2s^{n+2}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $U_i$ is weakly harmonic for $g$.  In constructing
this completion with the Riemannian volume, the linear boundary
value is first multiplied by a compact cutoff equal to one on
the ball.  Locality removes this cutoff from the equation; metric
and volume comparability identify the displayed energy norms.

Each $U_i$ has a smooth interior representative before any
quantitative coordinate estimate is applied.  Here is the local
regularity mechanism.  The derivatives in the energy completion
are weak coordinate derivatives, as follows by integration by
parts on a sequence of smooth approximants.  In a relatively
compact subball, test the divergence equation by the backward
difference quotient of $\eta^2$ times a forward difference
quotient of $U_i$.  Coercivity bounds the first derivatives of
these difference quotients; derivatives of $a$ and $\eta$ occur
only in bounded lower-order terms.  Weak compactness yields
second weak derivatives.  Differentiating the distributional
equation repeats this argument on smaller balls, giving every
local Sobolev order.  Sobolev embedding then gives a smooth
representative.  Two such representatives agree everywhere on
an overlap because they agree almost everywhere and are
continuous; positive volume of open sets proves this last step.
The original weak equation consequently becomes $\Delta_gU_i=0$
pointwise.  This argument uses bounded derivatives of this
particular smooth metric, and asserts no uniform estimates yet.

\subsection{From small energy to an invertible coordinate map}

The energy bound alone does not make $DU$ close to the identity.
To obtain that conclusion, compare $\nabla U_i$ with the radial
unit field $X_i=T e_i$, and write $V=\nabla U_i-X_i$.
For any compactly supported vector field $Y$, commuting derivatives
of the harmonic scalar gives the weak connection identity
\[
 \int\langle\nabla V,\nabla Y\rangle_g
 =-\int\operatorname{Ric}(\nabla U_i,Y)
   -\int\langle\nabla X_i,\nabla Y\rangle_g.
\]
The sign of the Ricci term follows from
$\Delta^\nabla\nabla U_i=\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}\nabla U_i$.
Choose $\sigma=C(n)(K+1)s^2$ so that
$s^2 nK\,|X_i|_g\leq\sigma$ and
$|\nabla X_i|_g\leq\sigma/s$, using
\eqref{eq:rf-radial-frame}.  Test the identity with
$\eta^2(|V|_g^2+\sigma^2)^{p-1}V$, $p\geq1$.
The derivative of the power has the favorable sign; Cauchy's
inequality absorbs half the derivative energy.  With
$v=(|V|_g^2+\sigma^2)^{1/2}$ this gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-power-energy}
 \int|\nabla(\eta v^p)|_g^2
 \leq22p^2\left(
    \frac{nK+2}{s^2}\int\eta^2v^{2p}
       +\int v^{2p}|\nabla\eta|_g^2\right).
\end{equation}
These integrals use $d\mu_g$ and $s\leq1$.

For clarity, the iteration retains the radius dependence.
Ellipticity transfers the Euclidean Sobolev inequality with
exponent $2\chi=2n/(n-1)$, which also applies when $n=2$.
Apply it to the cutoffs in \eqref{eq:rf-power-energy}, with
$p=\chi^j$ and balls about $z$ of radii $s/2^{j+1}$.
If $b_j$ is the $L^{2\chi^j}$ norm on the $j$th ball, then
\[
 b_{j+1}\leq((B/s)L^j)^{1/(2\chi^j)}b_j.
\]
The sums of $\chi^{-j}$ and $j\chi^{-j}$ converge, and
$\sum_j(2\chi^j)^{-1}=n/2$.  The product is therefore bounded
by $Cs^{-n/2}$.  Continuity at $z$, together with the shrinking
balls and growing exponents, gives
\[
 |V(z)|_g^2+\sigma^2
 \leq Cs^{-n}\left(
       \int_{B(z,s/2)}|V|_g^2\,dx
           +|B(z,s/2)|\sigma^2\right)
       \quad(z\in B_{s/2}).
\]
The measure in this last formula can be Euclidean by the fixed
volume bounds.  By \eqref{eq:rf-correction-energy} and the coframe
estimate, its right side is at most
$C\varepsilon^2+C(K+1)^2s^4$.
The same coframe estimate compares $X_i$ with $\nabla x_i$;
ellipticity converts the gradient error to an ordinary differential
error.  Thus, given any $\tau>0$, choosing $\varepsilon$ and then
$s$ sufficiently small yields
\[
 |DU_i-e_i^*|\leq\tau\quad\hbox{on }B_{s/2}.
\]
All these choices are made before the metric.

Center the map $F=(U_1,\ldots,U_n)-U(0)$.  Choose the component
tolerance so that $|DF-I|\leq\beta\leq1/2$ on a ball $B_\rho$.
Integrating along segments shows that $F-I$ is
$\beta$-Lipschitz.  Hence $F$ is injective and its differential
is invertible.  For $|y|<\rho/4$, the contraction
$x\mapsto y-(F(x)-x)$ preserves $\overline B_{\rho/2}$ and
has a unique fixed point.  This proves
$B_{\rho/4}\subset F(B_\rho)$, with a smooth inverse on the
image.  The inverse differential has bounds $2/3$ and $2$.
Its pullback metric therefore has ellipticity $1/9,9$.
More precisely, if $|g-I|\leq\alpha$, expansion of
$(DF^{-1})^Tg\,DF^{-1}$ gives
\[
 |(F^{-1})^*g-I|\leq4\alpha+6\beta.
\]
Both errors can be chosen arbitrarily small.  This is the
quantitative freedom needed for the next estimate.

\subsection{Uniform first derivatives without curvature derivatives}

Now let $h$ be a harmonic-coordinate metric on $B_S$, with
$aI\leq h\leq bI$ and $|\operatorname{Rm}_h|\leq K$.
For a harmonic function $f$ with $|\nabla f|_h\leq G$, put
$Q=\nabla^2f$.  The differentiated equation is used in the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rf-hessian-divergence}
 \Delta^\nabla Q=\operatorname{div}\mathcal B+\mathcal R(Q),
 \qquad
 \mathcal B(a,b,c)=h(a,b)\operatorname{Ric}(\nabla f,c)
                         -df(\operatorname{Rm}(a,b)c).
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal R(Q)$ is the algebraic curvature action from
commuting a traced derivative with a free tensor slot:
\[
 \mathcal R(Q)(a,b)=-\sum_j\left[
 Q(\operatorname{Rm}(E_j,a)E_j,b)
       +Q(E_j,\operatorname{Rm}(E_j,a)b)\right]
\]
for an orthonormal frame $E_j$.  The identity follows by writing
the Codazzi defect of $Q$ as $-df(\operatorname{Rm}(a,b)c)$
and the divergence of $Q$ as $\operatorname{Ric}(\nabla f,\cdot)$.
In particular,
$|\mathcal B|\leq C(n)KG$ and
$|\mathcal R(Q)|\leq C(n)K|Q|$.
Integration by parts leaves the derivative on the tensor test:
\[
 \int\langle\nabla Q,\nabla Z\rangle
 =\int\langle\mathcal B,\nabla Z\rangle
       -\int\langle\mathcal R(Q),Z\rangle.
\]
This is why no bound for $\nabla\operatorname{Rm}$ is required.

Set $v=(|Q|^2+\sigma^2)^{1/2}$, with
$\sigma=(1+C(n)KG)s$ on a ball of radius $s\leq1$.
Testing by $\eta^2v^{2p-2}Q$ gives the same power estimate as
\eqref{eq:rf-power-energy}, with $nK+2$ replaced by
$2n^2K+1$.  The preceding iteration bounds $|Q(z)|^2$ by
its local squared mass and $\sigma^2$.  To supply that mass,
integrate the scalar Bochner identity
\[
 \tfrac12\Delta|\nabla f|^2
       =|Q|^2+\operatorname{Ric}(\nabla f,\nabla f)
\]
against a squared cutoff.  Absorbing the mixed cutoff term yields
\[
 \int_{B_{S/4}}|Q|^2
     \leq C(a,b,n)(K+S^{-2})G^2|B_{S/2}|.
\]
The mass and iteration bounds give a uniform Hessian bound on
$B_{S/8}$.  For $f=y_i$, the coordinate gradient bound is
$G\leq a^{-1/2}$ and
$\nabla^2 y_i(\partial_j,\partial_k)=-\Gamma^i_{jk}$.
Thus the connection coefficients are bounded.  Metric
compatibility,
\[
 \partial_k h_{ij}
       =h_{\ell j}\Gamma^\ell_{ki}
                          +h_{i\ell}\Gamma^\ell_{kj},
\]
gives $|Dh|\leq L(n,S,a,b,K)$ on this smaller ball.

\subsection{The half-Holder estimate and the local surface chart}

Harmonicity of the coordinates cancels the contracted Christoffel
terms in the Ricci equation.  For each coefficient $u=h_{ij}$,
\[
 \Delta_h u=-2\operatorname{Ric}_{ij}+\mathcal Q_{ij}(Dh,Dh),
 \qquad
 |\Delta_hu|\leq2nKb+\frac{32n^2b}{a^3}L^2.
\]
The last bound follows by expanding the quadratic connection
terms and using the inverse-metric bound $a^{-1}$.  Put
$A=\sqrt{\det h}\,h^{-1}=I+E$.
The map $h\mapsto\sqrt{\det h}\,h^{-1}$ is smooth near $I$,
so the freely chosen bound $|h-I|<\delta$ makes $|E|$ as small
as desired, while the established bound on $Dh$ bounds $DE$.
Choose a spatial cutoff $\eta$ supported in $B_{S/32}$ and
equal to one on $B_{S/128}$.  For $v=\eta u$ the equation is
\[
 \Delta v=F-\operatorname{div}(E\nabla v),
\]
where $F=\operatorname{div}(A\nabla v)$ has a uniform supremum
bound.  Indeed its expansion contains only the bounded
$\sqrt{\det h}\Delta_hu$, $u$, $Du$, $A$, $DA$, and the first
two cutoff derivatives.  A second cutoff extends $E$ globally,
preserving its small supremum and a bounded half-Holder seminorm.

Here is the estimate that absorbs the last term.  Multiply $v$
by a fixed smooth time cutoff $\zeta$, zero at time zero and
one at time one.  The resulting function has heat residual
\[
 (\partial_t-\Delta)(\zeta v)
    =\zeta'v-\zeta F+
                        \operatorname{div}(\zeta E\nabla v).
\]
The zero-initial-data heat representation bounds its gradient
half-Holder seminorm at time one by
\[
 C\bigl(\|v\|_\infty+\|F\|_\infty
                    +n[E\nabla v]_{1/2}\bigr).
\]
For the divergence term this follows from cancellation of the
second derivative of the Gaussian kernel.  Subtract the source
value at the center in the convolution.  If its half-Holder
seminorm is $Q_0$ and $d=|x-y|$, the difference of the two
Hessian convolutions is bounded both by
$C Q_0t^{-3/4}$ and by $C Q_0d\,t^{-5/4}$; the latter uses
the third derivative of the kernel.  Splitting the time
integral at $d^2$ gives $C Q_0d^{1/2}$.  The ordinary bounded
source is estimated using the first kernel derivative.
Consequently, with $J=[Dv]_{1/2}$,
\[
 J\leq C(\|v\|_\infty+\|F\|_\infty
                       +n[E]_{1/2}\|Dv\|_\infty)
                           +Cn\|E\|_\infty J.
\]
Compact smoothness makes $J$ finite initially.  Choosing the
coefficient tolerance so that $Cn\|E\|_\infty\leq1/2$
absorbs it, giving a uniform bound independent of this initial
seminorm.  Summing the finitely many coefficient slots proves
the asserted bound on $[Dh]_{1/2}$.

To finish Theorem~\ref{thm:rf-harmonic-radius}, choose this small
tolerance first, then the weak-coordinate radii and inverse map.
Take $S=\rho/8$ in its image and $r=S/256$.
Extend $(F^{-1})^*g$ by a convex cutoff combination with the
Euclidean metric, equal to the pullback on $B_S$ and with
ellipticity $1/9,9$ globally.  On $B_S$, change of coordinates
preserves curvature and identifies $\Delta_h y_i$ with
$\Delta_gF_i=0$.  The preceding estimates apply on $B_{S/128}
=B_{2r}$.  Finally the image under $e=F^{-1}$ of the segment
$tx$ has length at most $3|x|$, proving the distance bound.

For an arbitrary smooth surface metric near $p$, choose a
normalized exponential chart.  Shrink it until its coefficients
are within $1/2$ of $I$, and extend them by a convex cutoff
with $I$.  The Gauss identity survives this extension.  On a
smaller compact ball the smooth curvature has a finite bound,
so the theorem supplies a harmonic chart centered at $p$.
Its first coordinate $u$ has nonzero differential.  On a small
oriented disk the one-form $\iota_{\nabla u}d\mu$ is closed,
because $\Delta u=0$, and hence equals $dv$ for a smooth
function $v$.  The covectors $du,dv$ are orthogonal, have equal
nonzero norm, and give an invertible differential of $(u,v)$.
The inverse function theorem therefore gives coordinates in
which the metric is a positive smooth multiple of $du^2+dv^2$.
This proves the local isothermal input used for the annulus,
including the absence of any completeness premise on that
surface metric.

\section{Pointed compactness and complete interior slices}
\label{sec:rf-compactness}

\begin{definition}\label{def:rf-pointed-convergence}
A sequence of based flows $(M_k,g_k(t),p_k)$ on $J=(T',T)$ converges
smoothly in the pointed sense after a subsequence if there are a
connected based flow $(M_\infty,g_\infty(t),p_\infty)$, a strictly
increasing subsequence $\sigma$, connected open sets
$U_j\subset U_{j+1}$ with compact closures and union $M_\infty$,
and base-preserving smooth open embeddings
$\phi_j:U_j\to M_{\sigma(j)}$, such that
$\phi_j^*g_{\sigma(j)}\to g_\infty$ with every mixed space and time
derivative uniformly on compact subsets of $J\times M_\infty$.
Each compact subset is required to lie in the domains for all
sufficiently large $j$.  The equivalent spacetime maps
$(t,x)\mapsto(t,\phi_j(x))$ preserve time and its vector field
$\partial_t$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:rf-compactness}
Fix $n$ and $T'<0<T$.  Let $(M_k,g_k(t),p_k)$ be connected based
$n$-dimensional Ricci flows on $J=(T',T)$, with calibrated Riemannian
volume on the zero slice.  Suppose:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item For every $A>0$, $\overline{B_{g_k(0)}(p_k,A)}$ is compact
 for all sufficiently large $k$.
 \item For every $A>0$ there is $K_A\geq0$ such that, for all
 sufficiently large $k$, for every $s,t\in J$ and every
 $x\in B_{g_k(s)}(p_k,A)$,
 $|\operatorname{Rm}_{g_k(t)}|(x)\leq K_A$.
 \item There are $r_0,\kappa>0$ such that, eventually,
 $\mu_{g_k(0)}(B_{g_k(0)}(p_k,r_0))\geq\kappa r_0^n$.
\end{enumerate}
There is a smoothly pointed convergent subsequence, and its limit is
complete at every $t\in J$.
\end{theorem}

The spacetime formulation additionally records, on each zero-time ball
and each compact time interval containing zero, a time-preserving
embedding carrying $\partial_t$ to $\partial_t$, equal to the identity
at time zero, with a curvature bound on its image.  For these ordinary
product flows, the identity map supplies this datum from condition~2.
No completeness of an individual approximating manifold is assumed.
The eventual index may depend on $A$; $K_A$ is independent of both
times and of the eventual index.

\subsection{Charts, overlaps, and the limit metric}

We describe the geometric construction rather than presuppose the
limit manifold.  The case $n=0$ is the one-point connected manifold.
For $n>0$, fix a radius and a compact time interval in $J$.
Local curvature bounds and the volume lower bound give a lower
injectivity radius on a slightly smaller zero-time ball.
Volume comparison propagates the base-ball lower bound to centers
at bounded distance.  Normal coordinates then have positive lower
and upper metric bounds.  Derivative estimates on a buffered time
interval, metric comparison, and differentiated coordinate equations
bound every mixed jet of their metric coefficients.  The radius of
each chart and every constant may depend on the chosen ball and
buffered interval.

We spell out the uniform size of the chart cover.  For a fixed source
metric write $B(p,A)$ for the ball to be covered, and fix
$0<\delta\leq A$.  Suppose $\overline{B(p,5A)}$ is compact and
$\operatorname{Ric}\geq-(n-1)Kg$ there, with $K\geq0$.  Let
\[
 V_{n,K}(r)=|S^{n-1}|\int_0^r s_K(u)^{n-1}\,du,\qquad
 s_K(u)=
 \begin{cases}
 u,&K=0,\\
 \sinh(\sqrt K u)/\sqrt K,&K>0.
 \end{cases}
\]
Thus $V_{n,K}$ is the ball-volume function for the simply connected
constant-curvature $-K$ model, with $V_{1,K}(r)=2r$.
For each $x\in B(p,A)$, the ball $B(x,4A)$ has compact closure
inside $\overline{B(p,5A)}$, and $B(p,2A)\subset B(x,3A)$.
Local relative volume comparison gives
\[
 \mu(B(x,\delta/2))
 \geq\frac{V_{n,K}(\delta/2)}{V_{n,K}(3A)}
                          \mu(B(x,3A))
 \geq\frac{V_{n,K}(\delta/2)}{V_{n,K}(3A)}
                          \mu(B(p,2A)).
\]
The local form of Bishop--Gromov uses compact closure of the larger
ball, so its radial geodesics and polar integration stay in the
controlled region; completeness of the whole manifold is unnecessary
(\cite[Theorem 1.34, p.~19; proof of Theorem 5.6, p.~86]{MT2007}).

If a finite set of centers in $B(p,A)$ has pairwise distance at least
$\delta$, its open $\delta/2$-balls are disjoint and lie in $B(p,2A)$.
The latter has positive finite volume.  Adding the displayed lower
bounds and cancelling that volume shows that the number of centers
is at most
\[
                         \frac{V_{n,K}(3A)}{V_{n,K}(\delta/2)}.
\]
Start with any center and add a point outside all open $\delta$-balls
whenever such a point exists.  Every addition preserves the separation,
so this process must terminate before exceeding the displayed bound.
The terminal $\delta$-balls cover $B(p,A)$.  For $\delta>A$ the
single basepoint ball already covers it.
Condition~1 and the zero-time case of condition~2 make the bound
uniform along the eventual source sequence.  This packing step does
not use the noncollapse hypothesis; noncollapse is needed for the
uniform injective coordinate radius.

Here is the noncollapse-to-injectivity step used for that radius.
Relative volume comparison first transports
$\mu(B(p,r_0))\geq\kappa r_0^n$ to every center in $B(p,A)$:
choose a larger controlled ball, compare the center's small ball to a
fixed base ball, and retain a positive lower bound depending only on
$A,r_0,\kappa$ and the curvature bound.  On the same controlled ball
the curvature tensor has a uniform bound $K$.  The Jacobi equation
then gives a common radius $s$ below both the curvature comparison
radius and the controlled source radius.  The exponential map at each
center is nonsingular on $B(0,s)$.

Injectivity is a separate global issue.  The local metric argument
in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:ancient-positive-ratio-obstruction},
starting at \eqref{eq:ancient-local-injectivity}, applies here with
the transported volume lower bound; that argument uses only local
curvature, volume and compact confinement.  In detail, relative volume
comparison gives a lower bound $w_0>0$ for the center's $s/4$-ball,
whereas the Jacobi bound gives an upper bound
$P=n!(3/2)^n\omega_ns^n$ for the pullback volume of $B(0,s)$.
Choose $N$ with $(N+1)w_0>P$, then choose the possible collision
radius $\rho$ small enough for the short-return construction with
this $N$.  A first collision gives a radial return of length at most
$2\rho$.  Its successive lifts have $N+1$ distinct endpoints:
a repeated endpoint would give a finite orbit of a local isometry,
whose sum of squared radial norms has two minima along a strictly
convex geodesic.  The explicit radius margins and this convexity
argument are given there.  Lifting radial paths from these endpoints
gives at least $N+1$ preimages of every point of the $s/4$-ball.
Change of variables would then imply $(N+1)w_0\leq P$, a
contradiction.  Thus the closed $\rho$-ball is injective.
Restricting the canonical exponential to the open
ball makes it a diffeomorphism onto the intrinsic ball of radius
$\rho$; radial length equality and the normal-coordinate derivative
bound give the two-sided distance estimates used above.

Choose a normal-coordinate radius $R$ uniformly for all centers in
$B(p,A)$, and a smaller $\rho>0$ with $2\rho<R$ on which the metric
coefficient estimates hold.  Apply the preceding cover with
$\delta=\rho/4$.  Radial distance in an injective normal chart is
exactly the Euclidean norm, so its $\rho/4$-ball is covered by the
image of the compact coordinate ball $\overline{B(0,\rho/4)}$.
Insert the basepoint among the centers, increasing the bound by at
most one, and repeat centers as necessary to label every source cover
by the same finite set with index zero at the basepoint.  The maps
and centers vary with the source index, but the coordinate domains,
compact covering pieces and number of labels do not.

The ambient distance bounds needed below also deserve attention.
Suppose $a|\xi|^2\leq\Phi^*g(\xi,\xi)\leq b|\xi|^2$ on
$B(0,2\rho)$, with $a>0$.  For $x,y\in B(0,\rho/2)$, the straight
coordinate segment gives
$d_g(\Phi(x),\Phi(y))\leq\sqrt b\,|x-y|$.
For the opposite inequality choose paths whose lengths approach this
ambient distance.  That distance is less than $\rho$, by joining the
two radial segments through the chart center, so the paths can be
chosen with length less than $\rho$.  Each stays in the intrinsic
$3\rho/2$-ball of the center and thus in the normal chart.
The inverse differential has norm at most $a^{-1/2}$ there.
Integration along the paths and passage to their infimum gives
\[
       \sqrt a\,|x-y|\leq d_g(\Phi(x),\Phi(y))
                         \leq\sqrt b\,|x-y|.
\]
Thus a metric lower bound in a larger coordinate region really does
control ambient distance on the inner region, even for incomplete
source metrics.

The coefficient compactness is joint in space and time.  For later use,
once the limit charts and embeddings below have been constructed,
take the limit chart matching source label $j$ and write its
coordinate parametrization as $q=q_j:V_j\to M_\infty$.
On compact subsets of $J\times V_j$, the pulled-back coefficients of
$g_k(t)$ have bounds for every mixed derivative.  The source readout
is first obtained locally: compact containment puts
$F_k(q_j(x))$ in the image of the matching source chart $e_{kj}$,
and its inverse
coordinate map is smooth there.  The readout converges to the identity
in every spatial jet.  Pulling back a bilinear coefficient field
therefore gives
\[
 \bigl((F_k\circ q)^*g_k(t)\bigr)_x(v,w)
 =G_{k,j}(t,b_k(x))
     \bigl(Db_k(x)v,Db_k(x)w\bigr),
\]
where $b_k\to\mathrm{id}$ smoothly and $G_{k,j}$ is the original
source-chart coefficient.  The chain rule and the product rule give
convergence of every mixed $(t,x)$ derivative.  A finite cover of a
compact set supplies one threshold for all local formulas and all basis
components; a diagonal choice over derivative order gives the
countable all-jets statement.  This is why the construction needs
spatial readout jets rather than only $C^0$ distance approximation.

For completeness, the bounds feeding this compactness have two
separate parts.  The local Shi estimate gives, for every curvature
derivative order $\ell$, a tail bound on a larger source ball and a
short time interval ending at the chosen interior time.  At zero time
we intersect the finitely many tails $\ell\leq m$ and use the
finite-order pullback estimate for a normal exponential chart:
curvature derivatives, the normalized initial metric and the radial
Gauss identity bound the $m$th metric-coefficient jet on the closed
coordinate ball.  The coupled radial-frame calculation leading to
\eqref{eq:ancient-radial-connection} gives this estimate: curvature
components, the Jacobi coframe and the connection are bounded in
that order, with spatial order $m$ of $\nabla^\ell\operatorname{Rm}$
requiring only $\ell+m$ covariant curvature derivatives.
This is a local calculation independent of the ancient-flow
application in which it is developed.  The threshold may depend on $m$, as it must for
$C^\infty$ convergence, but it is uniform over every center and every
normal chart in the fixed finite cover.

To extend the coefficient bounds through the whole time interval,
keep the spatial point and chart fixed.  If $w$ is the coordinate
tangent vector, the Ricci contraction satisfies
\[
 |\operatorname{Ric}_t(w,w)|
 \leq n^3K\,g_t(w,w)
\]
whenever $|\operatorname{Rm}_t|\leq K$.  The metric evolution
$\partial_tg_t=-2\operatorname{Ric}_t$ and Gronwall therefore give
\[
 e^{-2n^3K(T-T')}g_0(w,w)
 \leq g_t(w,w)
 \leq e^{2n^3K(T-T')}g_0(w,w).
\]
The curvature bound is required at the fixed image point for every
time; no uniform choice of a time slice is silently substituted.
Differentiating the metric equation and using the Shi bounds then
supplies the mixed time derivatives used by the spacetime jet
extraction.  This is the time-comparison mechanism in
\cite[Proposition 5.14, pp. 90--91; Theorem 5.15, pp. 91--92]{MT2007}.

Take a diagonal subsequence over increasing balls, compact time
intervals exhausting $J$, and derivative orders.  There are countably
many fixed coordinate domains $V_i$, source charts
$e_{ki}:V_i\to M_k$, smooth positive metric coefficient limits
$G_i(t,x)$, and limits
\[
                 D_{ij}(x,y)=\lim_k d_{g_k(0)}(e_{ki}(x),e_{kj}(y)).
\]
The distance convergence is locally uniform.  Uniform upper and lower
Lipschitz bounds for each chart pass to the limit; triangle symmetry
and the triangle inequality pass pointwise.  Identify $(i,x)$ with
$(j,y)$ exactly when $D_{ij}(x,y)=0$.  The triangle inequality proves
transitivity, and the lower chart bound shows that no two different
points of one chart are identified.

The zero-distance relation determines actual open overlaps.  Write
$c_i|x-x'|\leq D_{ii}(x,x')\leq L_i|x-x'|$ for the inherited
chart bounds.  First observe a source coverage fact.  If
$\overline B(y,r)\subset V_j$, the image of $B(y,r)$ under
$e_{kj}$ contains the source ball $B(e_{kj}(y),c_jr)$.
Indeed its image is open, its closure is compact, and any boundary
point is the image of a coordinate point at distance $r$ from $y$,
so lies at source distance at least $c_jr$.  Intersecting with the
connected source ball proves coverage.  These source balls are
connected because the Riemannian distance is a length distance;
near-minimizing paths to the center stay in the ball.  Completeness
is not needed.

Now suppose $D_{ij}(x,y)=0$.  Choose such a compact target ball and
a small coordinate neighborhood of $x$, of radius at most
$c_jr/[2(L_i+1)]$.  For all sufficiently large $k$, its whole
source image lies in $e_{kj}(B(y,r))$.  Thus the source inverse
transition is defined on this fixed neighborhood with compact target.
For any point $x'$ in the neighborhood, compactness of its inverse
coordinates gives a limiting partner $y'$ with $D_{ij}(x',y')=0$.
The partner is unique by the lower chart bound.  Hence the overlap
is open, and its transition $T_{ij}$ satisfies
\[
 |T_{ij}(x)-T_{ij}(x')|\leq (L_i/c_j)|x-x'|.
\]
Interchanging the charts gives its inverse; the triangle inequality
and uniqueness give every cocycle identity.  Compact subsets of an
overlap have one compact target and one eventual source threshold,
by a finite cover of these neighborhoods.

There is also quantitative smoothness.  A source transition $T_k$
is a local isometry between the coefficient metrics $G_{k,i}(0)$
and $G_{k,j}(0)$.  Uniform ellipticity and the upper metric bound
first bound $DT_k$.  Preservation of the Levi-Civita connection gives
\[
 D^2T_k=DT_k\,\Gamma_{k,i}
       -\Gamma_{k,j}(T_k)(DT_k,DT_k).
\]
Inverse metric bounds and the coefficient jets bound the Christoffel
symbols and their derivatives; differentiating this identity
inductively bounds every derivative of $T_k$ on the confined domain.
Smooth compactness on smaller compact sets now applies.  Every
subsequential limit must be $T_{ij}$ by the zero-distance identity,
so all transition jets converge to those of this one smooth map.

The quotient charts are open embeddings, since their overlap domains
are open.  The continuous functions $D_{ij}$ descend, by the triangle
inequality, to a separating distance on the quotient.  This distance
induces precisely the chart topology: distance balls are open in all
charts; conversely, a sufficiently small distance ball about $q_i(x)$
lies in the image of any chosen smaller compact coordinate ball about
$x$, by the same source coverage and compact-partner argument.
Thus the quotient is Hausdorff.  The countably many Euclidean chart
bases give a countable base, so the smooth transitions define a
smooth manifold.  This auxiliary distance is not yet identified with
the path distance of the metric constructed next.
The $G_i$ obey the tensor transition law, since it holds before taking
limits:
\[
 G_j(t,T_{ij}(x))(DT_{ij}(x)v,DT_{ij}(x)w)=G_i(t,x)(v,w).
\]
The compact target confinement just established permits evaluation
at the varying source transitions before passing to this identity.
The retained lower metric bounds make every $G_i$ positive, so these
coefficients give a smooth metric $g_\infty(t)$.  The coordinate
Ricci expression uses the metric inverse and at most two spatial
derivatives.  Positivity makes inversion continuous on each compact
coordinate neighborhood.  Convergence of two spatial derivatives and
one time derivative therefore passes
\eqref{eq:rf-equation} to the limit.  All mixed jets converge, so the
limiting flow is smooth.

Charts into each source do not initially agree exactly on the limiting
overlaps.  We construct actual embeddings near any given compact
quotient set by a finite induction.  Write $q_i:V_i\to M_\infty$
for the quotient charts.  The induction retains a compact set $P$,
an open neighborhood $O$ with compact closure, and embeddings
$F_k:O\to M_k$ for all sufficiently large $k$.  It also retains the
following local formula: near each point of $O$, in some fixed chart,
\[
             F_k(q_j(y))=e_{kj}(b_k(y)),
             \qquad b_k\longrightarrow\mathrm{id}\quad\hbox{in }C^\infty
\]
on compact sets.  A fixed compact neighborhood of the basepoint is
included in $P$ and its original source-chart formula is kept exactly.
One source chart initializes these properties.

These local formulas imply two useful convergence statements.
First, on every compact $C\subset V_i$ with $q_i(C)\subset O$,
\[
             d_{g_k(0)}(F_k(q_i(x)),e_{ki}(x))\longrightarrow0
             \quad\hbox{uniformly for }x\in C.
\]
Indeed, in a local formula choose $y$ with $q_j(y)=q_i(x)$.
The distance from $e_{kj}(b_k(y))$ to $e_{kj}(y)$ tends uniformly
to zero by the upper Lipschitz bound.  The distance from
$e_{kj}(y)$ to $e_{ki}(x)$ tends to $D_{ji}(y,x)=0$.
A finite cover gives the assertion on $C$.
Second, the readout $e_{ki}^{-1}F_kq_i$ converges smoothly to
the identity wherever $q_i$ takes values in $O$.  To verify that
the inverse is actually defined, take a compact coordinate ball
inside $V_i$.  The lower chart Lipschitz bound places its boundary
a fixed positive source distance from its center.  The image is
open, and its compact closure is closed; connectedness of small
source metric balls therefore puts a uniformly smaller source
ball inside that image.  The preceding distance approximation
places $F_kq_i$ in it.  This argument is local and does not require
complete source metrics.

For smooth convergence, let $T_{ij}$ be the limiting transition
from chart $i$ to chart $j$, and $T_{k,ji}=e_{ki}^{-1}e_{kj}$
the source transition in the opposite direction.  In the neighborhood
of a retained local formula the readout equals
\[
                 T_{k,ji}\circ b_k\circ T_{ij}.
\]
On a slightly smaller compact neighborhood all compositions are
defined.  The source transitions converge smoothly to $T_{ji}$,
so the chain rule gives convergence to
$T_{ji}T_{ij}=\mathrm{id}$ in every derivative.  A finite cover
again makes this uniform on any specified compact subset.

Now adjoin a compact piece in a new chart $q:V_i\to M_\infty$.
Choose compact sets $A,A'$ in the old region and $C\subset V_i$
so that
\[
 P\subset\operatorname{int}A,\qquad
 A\subset\operatorname{int}A',\qquad A'\subset O,
\]
and the new piece lies in $\operatorname{int}C$.
The set $K=C\cap q^{-1}(A')$ is compact and is contained in the
open overlap $q^{-1}(O)$.  This set includes every identification
between the larger old region and the entire closed new piece.
Choose a smooth cutoff $\chi$ equal to one near $K$ with compact
support in $q^{-1}(O)$, and use the actual readout
$r_k=e_{ki}^{-1}F_kq$ there.  Extend
\[
                    a_k(x)=x+\chi(x)(r_k(x)-x)
\]
by the identity off that support.  Smooth convergence of $r_k$ to
the identity gives $\|D(a_k-\mathrm{id})\|_\infty<1/2$ eventually.
The mean value inequality on Euclidean space then gives
$|a_k(x)-a_k(y)|\geq|x-y|/2$.  Thus $a_k$ is injective and locally
invertible; it is proper because it is the identity outside a
compact set, so its open and closed image is all Euclidean space.
It is a smooth diffeomorphism.  For large $k$ its displacement is
smaller than the distance of the support to the chart boundary, so it
preserves $V_i$.  The corrected chart $g_k=e_{ki}\circ a_k$ satisfies
$g_k(y)=F_k(q(y))$ for every $y\in C$ with $q(y)\in A'$.

The margin between $A$ and $A'$ now excludes unwanted identifications.
On the compact product $A\times C$ the distances
$d_{g_k(0)}(F_k(x),g_k(y))$ converge uniformly to
$d_\infty(x,q(y))$.  This follows from the local formulas, the
limits $D_{ij}$, and the small displacement of $a_k$.
All zeros of the limiting distance lie in the open subset
$A\times q^{-1}(\operatorname{int}A')$ of this product:
if $x=q(y)$ and $x\in A$, then $q(y)\in\operatorname{int}A'$.
On its compact complement the limiting distance has a positive
minimum, unless that complement is empty.  Thus any source collision,
for all sufficiently large $k$, has $q(y)\in A'$.  There the
established agreement gives
\[
 F_k(x)=g_k(y)=F_k(q(y)).
\]
Both $x$ and $q(y)$ lie in $O$, where $F_k$ is injective, so
$x=q(y)$.  Conversely that equality implies agreement.  We have
therefore proved the exact equivalence
\[
       F_k(x)=g_k(y)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x=q(y),
       \qquad x\in A,\ y\in C.
\]

Consequently $F_k$ on $\operatorname{int}A$ and $g_kq^{-1}$ on
$q(\operatorname{int}C)$ glue to one injective local diffeomorphism
on their union.  Its local inverses show that it is an open embedding.
This union has compact closure contained in $A\cup q(C)$ and contains
both $P$ and the new compact piece.  On the old part the map is
unchanged; on the new part it has the local formula with correction
$a_k$.  Thus all induction properties, including the exact basepoint
formula, persist.  The old open domain may shrink, but the retained
compact set, enlarged by the new piece, remains inside the next domain.
This distinction is what permits finitely many corrections.

Every compact quotient set has a finite cover by interiors of compact
chart pieces.  The induction therefore gives the required embeddings
on a neighborhood of that set.  Exhaust the connected limit by
relatively compact connected open sets and, for each stage, choose a
source index larger than the previous one for which its embedding is
defined and the first finitely many prescribed jet errors are small.
This diagonal choice gives
Definition~\ref{def:rf-pointed-convergence}.  Embeddings chosen for
different source indices need not agree with one another.  Their
coordinate readouts converge to the identity, so their pullback
metrics have the same limits $G_i$.  This develops the finite patching
step summarized using partitions of unity in
\cite[Theorem 5.6, pp.~86--87]{MT2007}.

\subsection{Boundary escape and completeness at zero}

The chart extraction retains the following coverage property: for each
$R>0$ there are finitely many chart indices and compact coordinate
pieces $C_i\subset V_i$ such that, for all sufficiently large $k$,
\[
                    B_{g_k(0)}(p_k,R)\subset\bigcup_i e_{ki}(C_i).
\]
The pieces and their number are fixed after the subsequence is selected;
pointwise existence of a finite cover on each individual manifold would
not give this assertion.  We now show precisely how this property
enters completeness.

Let $p_\infty=q_{i_0}(p)$ and define
\[
                    \rho(q_i(x))=D_{i_0i}(p,x).
\]
Triangle inequalities show that this is independent of the chosen
representative, continuous and nonnegative, with
$\rho(p_\infty)=0$.  We use it as an auxiliary radius; no identification
with Riemannian distance is needed here.
If $\rho(q_i(x))<R$, then $e_{ki}(x)$ eventually lies in the
covered source $R$-ball.  Some one covering chart occurs along an
infinite subsequence.  In that chart write
$e_{ki}(x)=e_{kj}(y_k)$ with $y_k\in C_j$, and pass to a further
subsequence with $y_k\to y\in C_j$.  Its upper Lipschitz bound gives
\[
 d_{g_k(0)}(e_{ki}(x),e_{kj}(y))
       \leq L_j|y_k-y|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus $D_{ij}(x,y)=0$ and $q_i(x)=q_j(y)$.  Every point with
$\rho<R$ belongs to the compact set $\bigcup_jq_j(C_j)$.
For $r<R$ the closed set $\{\rho\leq r\}$ is therefore compact.

The quotient is connected as well.  Otherwise choose a nontrivial
subset that is both open and closed, containing $p_\infty$ and
excluding a point $q$.  Choose $R>\rho(q)$ and split each of the
finitely many covering pieces according to these two quotient subsets.
Both parts are compact.  Any pair from opposite parts has positive
limiting cross-distance, so compactness and uniform convergence make
their source images disjoint for large $k$.  The source basepoint
belongs to the first union and a source representative of $q$ to the
second: a contrary infinite sequence of representations in a wrong
part would give a zero-distance partner there by the preceding
compactness argument.  These two disjoint closed source unions cover
the source $R$-ball and both meet it, contradicting its connectedness.
A Riemannian ball is connected even without completeness: a point
in the ball can be joined to its center by a path of length less than
the radius, and every initial subpath stays in the ball.

Put $E_j$ equal to the component containing $p_\infty$ of
$\{\rho<j+1\}$.  Local connectedness of the manifold makes $E_j$
open.  Its closure is compact and lies in $E_{j+1}$, since it is
connected and lies in $\{\rho<j+2\}$.  The sets $E_j$ exhaust the
quotient: a path from the basepoint to any fixed point has compact
image and hence bounded $\rho$.  Also
\[
                    \rho=j+1\quad\hbox{on }\partial E_j.
\]
Indeed, the boundary of a component of an open set in a locally
connected space is contained in the boundary of that open set;
continuity of $\rho$ then gives the displayed equality.
Use this particular exhaustion in the diagonal embedding construction.

On the compact boundary $\partial E_j$ the source distances
$d_{g_{\sigma(k)}(0)}(p_{\sigma(k)},\phi_k(x))$ converge uniformly
to $\rho(x)$.  To see uniformity, cover that boundary by finitely
many compact chart representatives and combine the original
cross-distance convergence with the local chart approximation of
$\phi_k$.  Given $A>0$, choose $j>A$.  Error less than one now
puts the whole embedded boundary outside the source $A$-ball,
with one eventual index valid for every boundary point.

For large $k$, $\phi_k$ is defined beyond $\overline{E_j}$.
The set $\phi_k(E_j)$ is open and its closure lies in the compact,
hence closed, set $\phi_k(\overline{E_j})$.  A closure point outside
$\phi_k(E_j)$ must be the image of $\partial E_j$, so cannot lie
in the source $A$-ball.  Therefore $\phi_k(E_j)$ is both relatively
open and relatively closed in that connected ball and contains its
center.  It covers the whole ball.

Apply this coverage with radius $2A$.  For any fixed
$x\in B_{g_\infty(0)}(p_\infty,A)$, choose a path of length less
than $A$ from $p_\infty$ to $x$.  Its compact image lies in the
embedding domain eventually, and convergence of lengths puts
$\phi_k(x)$ in the source $2A$-ball.  Coverage supplies
$y\in E_j$ with $\phi_k(y)=\phi_k(x)$.  Both points lie in the
same embedding domain, so injectivity gives $x=y\in E_j$.
Consequently every limit $A$-ball has compact closure.  A Cauchy
sequence is bounded, has a convergent subsequence in one such compact
set, and hence converges.  This proves completeness of $g_\infty(0)$
without assuming completeness of any approximating metric.
Compare \cite[Theorems 5.9--5.11, pp.~88--90; Proposition 5.14,
pp.~90--91; Theorem 5.15, pp.~91--92]{MT2007}.

\subsection{The second time variable}

Curvature and norms converge through the metric jets.  To transfer
condition~2 to a limit ball, choose a short path from $p_\infty$ to
its point at time $s$ and put that path in a compact exhaustion
stage.  Length convergence places its image in a slightly larger
source $s$-ball.  Apply the same source bound at any other time $t$
and then pass to the limit.  Thus, for every $A>0$, some $K_A'<\infty$
bounds limit curvature at all $t\in J$ on every ball
$B_{g_\infty(s)}(p_\infty,A)$, uniformly over $s\in J$.

Here is a direct completeness argument using exactly that quantifier
order.  Fix $t\in J$ and a $g_\infty(t)$-Cauchy sequence $(x_i)$.
It lies in a ball of radius $r$ about $p_\infty$, after increasing
$r>0$.  Condition~2 on the limit gives a curvature bound $K$ on the
fixed set $B_{g_\infty(t)}(p_\infty,3r)$ at every intermediate time
between $t$ and zero.  Hence $|\operatorname{Ric}|\leq n^3K$ there
(this coarse contraction constant suffices).  Pointwise integration
of the metric equation compares tangent norms by the factor
$L=\exp(n^3K|t|)$.

For sufficiently close $x_i,x_j$, a nearly shortest $g_\infty(t)$-path
between them has length less than $r$ and remains in the ball of
radius $3r$.  Comparing its lengths at zero and at $t$ and taking
the infimum gives
$d_{g_\infty(0)}(x_i,x_j)\leq Ld_{g_\infty(t)}(x_i,x_j)$.
The sequence is therefore Cauchy for the complete zero-time metric.
It converges there, hence in the manifold topology, and thus also
for $g_\infty(t)$, which induces the same topology.  This proves
completeness at every interior time.

A curvature bound only on zero-time balls does not supply the fixed
$t$-ball bound in this proof.  The two-time condition is the point of
\cite[Theorem 1.7, p.~179]{Topping2014}; the failure under weaker
hypotheses is discussed in \cite[Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 1.4,
pp.~176--177]{Topping2014}.

\subsection{Ball volumes and static noncollapse in the retained limit}

\begin{lemma}[Volume and noncollapse under complete pointed convergence]
\label{lem:rf-limit-static-noncollapse}
Suppose the pointed convergence of
Definition~\ref{def:rf-pointed-convergence} is given in dimension
$n\geq1$.  Fix an interior time $t$ at which the limit metric
$g_\infty=g_\infty(t)$ is complete.  For $x\in M_\infty$,
write $x_i=\phi_i(x)$, $h_i=g_{\sigma(i)}(t)$ and
$\mu_i=\mu_{h_i}$.  Then, for every $r>0$,
\[
       \mu_i(B_{h_i}(x_i,r))
             \longrightarrow\mu_{g_\infty}(B_{g_\infty}(x,r)).
\]
Suppose further that the source metrics are metrically
$\kappa$-noncollapsed, with $\kappa>0$: for every source center
$y$ and radius $r>0$,
\[
 |\operatorname{Rm}_{h_i}|\leq r^{-2}\ \hbox{on }B_{h_i}(y,r)
       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
       \mu_i(B_{h_i}(y,r))\geq\kappa r^n .
\]
For the conclusion at a given $x$, it suffices that this implication
hold for all sufficiently large $i$ at each fixed radius and
the centers $x_i$.
Then the complete limit metric is metrically
$\kappa$-noncollapsed with the same constant.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Coverage at an arbitrary center.}
Fix $C>1$ and $r>0$, and let $R=Cr+1$.
The closed limit $R$-ball about $x$ is compact and lies in one
exhaustion stage.  Uniform convergence of the positive metric
matrices gives, eventually on this ball,
\[
 C^{-1}|v|_{g_\infty}
       \leq |d\phi_i(v)|_{h_i}
       \leq C|v|_{g_\infty}.
\]
The corresponding inverse differential bound is at most $C$.
Take a source path from $x_i$ of length less than $r$.
If it first exited the image of the open limit $R$-ball, the
image of the closed ball, being compact, would contain the
exit point.  The inverse path up to that point has length
less than $Cr<R$, a contradiction.  The inverse endpoint
is in fact at limit distance less than $Cr$.  Hence
\[
 B_{h_i}(x_i,r)\subset
       \phi_i(B_{g_\infty}(x,Cr)).
\]
Conversely, a minimizing limit segment of length less than
$r/C$ lies in the controlled ball.  Its image has length less
than $r$, giving
\[
 \phi_i(B_{g_\infty}(x,r/C))\subset B_{h_i}(x_i,r).
\]
These are statements about the actual embeddings; completeness
of a source metric is unnecessary.

\emph{The volume squeeze at every radius.}
The singular values of $d\phi_i$ with respect to these metrics
lie between $C^{-1}$ and $C$, so its volume Jacobian lies between
$C^{-n}$ and $C^n$.  Change of variables on the embedding image
and the two inclusions give
\[
 C^{-n}\mu_{g_\infty}(B(x,r/C))
 \leq\mu_i(B(x_i,r))
 \leq C^n\mu_{g_\infty}(B(x,Cr)).
\]
To let $C\downarrow1$, we need continuity in radius, including
radii meeting the cut locus.  A positive-radius intrinsic sphere
in a complete manifold is covered by the exponential image of
the Euclidean sphere of the same radius: choose a minimizing
segment to each of its points.  That Euclidean sphere has
$n$-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero.  The exponential map is
smooth; on local coordinate neighborhoods it is Lipschitz on
compact subsets and therefore preserves such null sets.
Riemannian volume has a smooth positive coordinate density.
A finite cover of the compact tangent sphere thus shows that
the intrinsic sphere has zero volume.

For radii tending to $r$, the indicators of the corresponding
balls converge off this null sphere.  They are eventually
dominated by the indicator of the $(r+1)$-ball, whose volume is
finite by completeness and local finiteness.  Dominated
convergence proves continuity of ball volume at $r$.
The displayed squeeze now proves the asserted volume limit.

\emph{Strict curvature margins.}
Assume $|\operatorname{Rm}_{g_\infty}|\leq r^{-2}$ on $B(x,r)$.
Choose $0<\rho<a<r$ and put $C=a/\rho>1$.
The compact closure of $B(x,a)$ lies inside $B(x,r)$.
Metric convergence through order two, uniform positive
definiteness, and the coordinate formulas
$\Gamma=g^{-1}*\partial g$ and
$\operatorname{Rm}=g*(\partial\Gamma+\Gamma*\Gamma)$
give uniform curvature-norm convergence on that closure.
Here the star denotes a finite sum of tensor contractions;
only inverses of uniformly positive matrices and their first
two derivatives occur.  A finite coordinate cover makes
the convergence uniform even when the point varies.
Because $r^{-2}<\rho^{-2}$, all sufficiently late embedded
points of that compact set have curvature norm less than
$\rho^{-2}$.

Coverage at radius $\rho$ places the entire source $\rho$-ball
inside the image of $B(x,a)$.  Thus the source noncollapse
implication applies and gives
$\mu_i(B(x_i,\rho))\geq\kappa\rho^n$.
Volume convergence at $\rho$ yields
$\mu_{g_\infty}(B(x,\rho))\geq\kappa\rho^n$.
This ball is contained in $B(x,r)$; let $\rho\uparrow r$ to
obtain the required $\kappa r^n$ lower bound.
The eventual index may depend on $x,\rho,a$; no common index
over all radii or centers was used.
\end{proof}

Applying this lemma separately at each complete interior slice
preserves static noncollapse throughout a pointed flow limit.
A curvature bound on a backward ball cylinder includes its
terminal spatial bound, so static noncollapse at every slice
also gives parabolic noncollapse with the same constant.
This is the complete-ball passage needed in the compactness
arguments of \cite[Corollary 44.1, pp. 2682--2683, and
Appendix E, pp. 2848--2849]{KleinerLott2013}.

\section{Inputs to the later arguments}
\label{sec:rf-interfaces}

The later surgery construction starts from
Proposition~\ref{prop:rf-normalize} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:rf-local}.  On each smooth interval it uses the
scalar clock $-6/(1+4t)$ and the pinching condition with the same
absolute time; a restart at time $a$ does not replace $t$ by $t-a$
inside the logarithmic barrier.  Scalar upper bounds then give full
curvature bounds by \eqref{eq:rf-full-bound}.

Reduced geometry and evolving-curve estimates use
Proposition~\ref{prop:rf-evolution},
Theorem~\ref{thm:rf-shi}, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:rf-metric-comparison} on the same component,
metric, and time slab as their application.  Ancient-model limits
use Theorem~\ref{thm:rf-compactness}; they must verify its volume
normalization, noncollapse and two-time bounds.  They may use the
bounded ancient Harnack consequence before the asymptotic
volume-ratio argument.  Use of the full slice-bounded
Theorem~\ref{thm:rf-harnack} requires the additional volume-to-curvature
argument in Section~\ref{sec:rf-harnack}.
