\chapter{A global controlled surgery flow}
\label{ch:global-surgery-v4}

The construction first extends a supplied finite controlled flow on its own
carrier history, then produces a global flow from a normalized metric.
The time domain is $[0,\infty)$; the carrier may change at surgery.
Write $F=(M_t,g(t),\mathcal J_F,\mathcal E_F)$, with surgery times $J_F$.
Ordinary slabs solve $\partial_tg=-2\operatorname{Ric}$; events retain their
incoming slice, retained region, cap charts, outgoing slice and comparison
maps. Admissibility and pinching are as in Section~\ref{sec:surgery-carriers}.
Canonical control applies where $R\geq r(t)^{-2}$; noncollapsing tests
surviving backward balls of radius at most $\epsilon$, with the specified
positive-component exception.

The terminal policy comes from Theorem~\ref{thm:branch-continuation}.
Put $\rho=\delta(T)r(T)$ and let $\Omega_{\rm core}$ consist of regular
terminal components meeting $\{R\leq\rho^{-2}\}$. At a nonempty event,
the retained limit region is exactly $\Omega_{\rm core}$ minus the selected
escaping neck ends. Each end contains the positive half-neck, misses the
negative half-neck and escapes every compact set. At a vanishing event,
each point of the last ordinary slab satisfies $R(x,t)>L_x>\rho^{-2}$
for $s_x\leq t<T$, for some $s_x<T$. The policy concerns only observed events.

\section{The order of choices}
\label{sec:global-choice-order}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform surgery setup]
\label{thm:global-setup}
\lean{PoincareMT.repairedControlledSchedules}
\uses{thm:cap-persistence,cor:singular-selector,thm:metric-surgery,thm:rf-local,lem:rf-continuation}
For every positive upper bound on twice the neck accuracy there is a
standard surgery setup with that bound, a common monotone height selector,
and positive initial controls. These choices precede the initial manifold
and every subsequent control function. The height at time $t$ is
$h(\delta(t)r(t),\delta(t))$.
\source{MT2007}\dcref{Definition 15.7 and Proposition 15.2, pp. 353--360}
\end{theorem}

A metric-surgery package $K$ supplies $K.\delta_0,K.C_0,K.R_0$.
Its setup selects a standard cap flow, $\epsilon,C$ and $h(\rho,\delta)$,
with

\[
  0<\epsilon<\tfrac12,\qquad
  \epsilon\leq\min\{1/200,K.\delta_0/2\},\qquad
  0<C_0\leq C,\qquad 1\leq C,
\]

and, for positive $\rho,\delta$,
$0<h(\rho,\delta)\leq\min\{K.\delta_0\rho,\rho\delta\}$.  The selector is monotone in both
arguments.  The surgery height at time $t$ is always

\begin{equation}
  h_t=h(\delta(t)r(t),\delta(t)).
  \label{eq:global-height}
\end{equation}

Fix the standard flow of Section~\ref{sec:standard-cap}. Choose neck,
topology and common singular-limit accuracies, then the gluing parameter
$\beta$, then $C=\max\{C_\kappa,C_{\rm standard}+1\}$. Here the ancient
alternatives use $\epsilon,2\epsilon$ and the standard flow uses
$\beta\epsilon/3$. Four spatial jets bound the normalized scalar gradient
and evolution on regular slabs. After that analytic coefficient and the
initial $\kappa_0$, choose the selector of Corollary~\ref{cor:singular-selector}.

The number $0<\beta<1/2$ controls gluing of two evolving necks. The
recent neck is $\beta\epsilon$-close to the shrinking cylinder; the
older strong neck has accuracy $\beta\epsilon/2$, normalized by its own
joining-time scalar. An exact joining isometry identifies their metrics
and centers. Their common recent spatial chart, restricted to length
$\epsilon^{-1}$, then gives an $\epsilon$-neck on normalized time
$(-1,0]$, smooth through the joining slice
\cite[Proposition 15.2, pp.~353--354]{MT2007}.

Normalize the final central scalar to one and let $s=-d$ be the joining
time. If $d\geq1$, the recent neck covers the required interval. Otherwise
four-jet control and the scalar evolution give $1/4<R(s)<1$. The older
neck has squared scale $\lambda=R(s)^{-1}\in(1,4)$ and reaches back to
$s-\lambda<-1$. Its pullback by the exact joining isometry agrees with
the recent metric at $s$.

Uniform accuracy follows by contradiction as $\beta\to0$: the joining
identity bounds transition-map jets, and Ricci naturality transports the
shrinking-cylinder evolution to the older chart. Both pieces then converge
to the same cylinder through the fixed required jet order. The Ricci
equation matches all mixed derivatives at the join. Thus one $\beta$,
chosen after $\epsilon$ and before the input necks, gives a smooth strong
$\epsilon$-neck on $(-1,0]$.

The initial cutoff is

\begin{equation}
 \Delta_0=\min\left\{\frac{\beta\epsilon}{3},\delta_{13},
       K_0^{-1},D_0^{-1}\right\},
 \label{eq:global-delta0}
\end{equation}

where $\delta_{13}>0$ is an applicability cutoff for metric surgery,
$K_0$ bounds the volume of the standard metric's closed core, and
$D_0^{-1}\leq R(g_0^{\rm cap})\leq D_0$. All four entries are positive.
The normalized initial-flow estimate supplies a Ricci flow on
$[0,1/16]$ with curvature bounded by $2$ and volume lower bound
$\operatorname{Vol}(B(x,r))\geq\kappa_0r^3$ for $r\leq\epsilon$; ordinary
uniqueness and maximality identify it with the eventual flow and exclude an
early surgery \cite[Claim 15.1, p.~353; Definition 15.7, p.~360]{MT2007}.

\section{Prefixes and the global numerical schedule}
\label{sec:global-prefixes}

For a positive integer $i$, a parameter prefix $p$ consists of arrays

\[
 (r_0,\ldots,r_i),\qquad (\kappa_0,\ldots,\kappa_i),\qquad
 (\Delta_0,\ldots,\Delta_i),
\]

all positive and antitone, with $r_0=\epsilon$, $r_j\leq\epsilon$, and
$\Delta_j\leq K.\delta_0$.  The $j$th entry controls the half-open interval

\begin{equation}
 E_j=\begin{cases}
 [0,2^0/32),&j=0,\\
 [2^{j-1}/32,2^j/32),&j>0.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:global-entry}
\end{equation}

The endpoint $T_j=2^j/32$ is the start of the next physical epoch.  A
prefix is seed-compatible when it uses the fixed setup,
$\kappa_0$ is its initial noncollapsing constant, and $\Delta_0$ is the value in
\eqref{eq:global-delta0}.

The noncollapsing induction has the following quantified form:

\begin{theorem}[Noncollapsing induction on an observed epoch]
\label{thm:global-noncollapse}
\lean{PoincareMT.m46NoncollapseInductionFromMilestones}
\uses{thm:global-setup,thm:m15-noncollapse,thm:branch-continuation,thm:cap-persistence}
for every seed-compatible $p$ there is a positive $\kappa_{\rm new}$ and a
function $\operatorname{cutoff}(r)$ such that
\begin{align*}
0<\kappa_{\rm new}&\leq\kappa_i,\\
0<\operatorname{cutoff}(r)&\leq\Delta_i
\end{align*}
for $0<r\leq r_i$, and every observed next-epoch flow satisfying the old
prefix controls, the terminal policy, the canonical bound at $r$, and
$\delta\leq\operatorname{cutoff}(r)$ is $\kappa_{\rm new}$-noncollapsed.

Here ``noncollapsed'' is the actual cylinder statement: if a point is not in
a positive component, every surviving backward cylinder of radius
$0<s\leq\epsilon$ on $[t-s^2,t]$, based on the whole terminal ball
$B_{g(t)}(x,s)$ and equal to the identity there at $t$, with
$|\mathrm{Rm}|\leq s^{-2}$ has

\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Vol}_{g(t)}B(x,s)\geq \kappa_{\rm new}s^3.
 \label{eq:global-noncollapse}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix the old prefix. For a proposed next radius, choose the action budget,
the auxiliary radius $\rho$, and then a standard-cap radius, time and
comparison tolerance. Escape through a cap's side costs too much kinetic
action; reaching its late-time face costs too much scalar action. Only
then choose a positive cutoff below the seed, scalar and cap thresholds.
All these choices precede the tested flow.

Comparison paths to the closure of an old seed neighborhood $V_0$ lie
below half the barrier. Hence an infimizing sequence stays in a compact
regular spacetime region. In finitely many gauges, square-root-time energy
controls velocities and positions; lower semicontinuity attains the action
infimum. Endpoint variation gives the minimizing region.

All minimizing initial vectors over $\overline V_0$ form a compact set
in the same surviving exponential family $E$. Outside its critical image,
finitely many inverse branches capture nearby minimizers. Their minimum
action is locally Lipschitz. Sard's and Rademacher's theorems imply that
almost every endpoint has one minimizing branch: equal action derivatives
give equal terminal momenta, and backward ODE uniqueness identifies the
branches. Compact capture makes this branch stable.

For the stable source $H$, set $W=H\cap E_\tau^{-1}(V_0)$. Its image
has the full volume of $V_0$, with bounds
\[
 l|_{E_\tau(W)}\leq \frac{L}{4\epsilon},\qquad
 \operatorname{Vol}(E_\tau(W))\geq
 \frac{\kappa_{\rm old}s_*^3}{8},\qquad
 \max\{\epsilon^2,(s/2)^2\}\leq\tau\leq\overline\tau.
\]
The constants depend on the old prefix before the tested point and flow.
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:m15-noncollapse} to the actual half-radius cylinder;
passing back to radius $s$ loses a factor $1/8$. Larger controlled cylinders
and Bishop--Gromov comparison treat low scalar values and small radii.
Positive components remain excluded here.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Canonical-neighborhood induction]
\label{thm:global-canonical}
\lean{PoincareMT.m47CanonicalInductionFromMilestones}
\uses{thm:global-noncollapse,thm:singular-m30,thm:neck-cap-canonical,thm:cap-persistence}
Canonical induction uses this particular noncollapsing extension at $p$.
It supplies $r_{\rm next}$ and
$\Delta_{\rm next}$ with

\[
  0<r_{\rm next}\leq r_i,\qquad
  0<\Delta_{\rm next}\leq\operatorname{cutoff}(r_{\rm next}),
\]

and proves the canonical-neighborhood predicate on the next observed epoch.
The canonical conclusion holds for the same cutoff and noncollapsing
constant just selected.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix the selected noncollapsing extension. Failure would give radii
$r_n\downarrow0$ and cutoffs $\delta_n\downarrow0$ chosen before their
counterexample flows. Impose the first $n$ cap-persistence bounds at stage
$n$, so every later subsequence retains each fixed bound. The first
canonical failure is attained at $(x_n,t_n)$, with actual scalar
$Q_n\geq r_n^{-2}$; normalize by $Q_n$.

Volume control must also cover the positive components omitted above.
Young high-curvature components have accessible seed cylinders; older ones
have confined whole-component cylinders. Generalized noncollapsing gives
a positive minimum density before choosing $r_n$ and the next cutoff.
Together with the old prefix, this controls the new epoch and its closed
preceding buffer of length $1/128$.

Pinching, strict-past canonical estimates and this volume bound give
compactness of the normalized backward cylinders. Cap contact, including
contact at zero backward duration, would make the selected bad point
canonical by the preselected persistence bounds. Thus the cylinder search
has a maximal common regular backward horizon $H$ on a subsequence.

If $H=\infty$, the limit is complete, nonflat, ancient and nonnegatively
curved. Source volume comparison on slightly smaller balls, followed by
letting their radii increase, gives noncollapse at every scale. The ancient
canonical alternatives transfer on their entire required space-time
domains to the original bad points, a contradiction.

If $H<\infty$, the endpoint construction of
Section~\ref{sec:singular-blowup} supplies compatible local extensions and
a complete metric at $-H$. The positive-curvature neck-scale estimate,
or null-plane splitting (with the flat case separate), bounds its scalar
curvature globally by $B_*$. Set $L=2\max\{1,3B_*\}$ and choose
\[
 d=\min\{H/4,1/(256A_aL)\}>0
\]
before the spatial radius and pinching error. On the original sequence,
the preselected cap bounds exclude contact throughout the proposed longer
window. Keeping the old comparison maps, regular cylinders of every radius
therefore extend to $-H-d$, contradicting maximality. This proves the
canonical conclusion for the already chosen noncollapsing extension.
\end{proof}

Starting with the seed prefix $p_0$, construct $p_{n+1}$ by adjoining
the radius and constants supplied by the noncollapsing and then canonical
induction at $p_n$. The prefix $p_n$ has last index $n+1$.
The old $r$ and $\kappa$ entries are copied exactly. The old last
$\Delta$ entry is replaced by the selected next cutoff, and this same
cutoff is appended as the new last entry. Thus, for every old index $j$,

\begin{equation}
 r_j^{(n+1)}=r_j^{(n)},\qquad
 \kappa_j^{(n+1)}=\kappa_j^{(n)},\qquad
\Delta_j^{(n+1)}\leq\Delta_j^{(n)}.
 \label{eq:global-stability}
\end{equation}

For $j\leq n$ the last inequality is an equality: only the old last
entry can change. Thus each coordinate stabilizes after its one tightening.
The schedule reads coordinate $j$ from stage $j$ after that tightening:
$r(j)=r_j^{(j)}$, $\kappa(j)=\kappa_j^{(j)}$, and
$\Delta(j)=\Delta_j^{(j)}$.  The stability equations imply that these are
positive antitone sequences and that every finite restriction agrees with the
corresponding prefix: for $j\leq n+1$ there is equality for $r,\kappa$
and $\Delta(j)\leq\Delta_j^{(n)}$, with equality also for $\Delta$ when
$j\leq n$. Positivity is inherited from that finite stage; no infinite
infimum of positive cutoffs is taken. The overlap height inequality follows from
monotonicity of $h$ and the three antitone sequences.  This is the concrete
meaning of ``later refinements preserve earlier controls''.

For a positive antitone control function $\delta:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)$,
let $j(t)$ be the unique index with $t\in E_{j(t)}$ and define

\begin{align}
 r(t)&=r(j(t)),\\
 \kappa(t)&=\kappa(j(t)),\\
 h(t)&=h(\delta(t)r(t),\delta(t)).
 \end{align}

On $E_j$ this gives $r(t)=r(j)$ and $\kappa(t)=\kappa(j)$.
If $\delta(t)\leq\Delta(j)$ there, then the full parameter record agrees
with the schedule used in continuation and global assembly:

\begin{equation}
 (r_F(t),\kappa_F(t),\delta_F(t),h_F(t))
 =\bigl(r(j),\kappa(j),\delta(t),h(\delta(t)r(j),\delta(t))\bigr).
 \label{eq:global-profile}
\end{equation}

The half-open convention is essential at an epoch boundary: the old value is
used strictly before the boundary, while the next value is used at and
after it.

\section{One singular epoch and the next frontier}
\label{sec:global-epoch}

\begin{theorem}[Extension across one singular epoch]
\label{thm:global-epoch}
\lean{PoincareMT.repairedEpochExtension}
\uses{thm:global-setup,thm:global-canonical,thm:branch-continuation,prop:unified-branch,thm:rf-pinching,thm:singular-m31,cor:singular-selector}

Fix a compatible prefix $p$ with index $i>0$.  Suppose $F$ has time domain
$[0,H)$ with

\[
 T_i\leq H<T_{i+1},
\]

and a preterminal ordinary slab whose curvature is unbounded as $t\nearrow H$.
The observation $O$ has horizon $H$, old-prefix controls, the terminal event
policy, the next-epoch profile identities, and the overlap inequality
$\delta\leq\Delta_{\rm next}$. The one-epoch continuation gives an extension
$E$ and a new observation $O'$ with

\begin{equation}
 H<H'\leq T_{i+1},\qquad H\in J_E,\qquad
 J_E\cap(H,H')=\varnothing.
 \label{eq:global-progress}
\end{equation}

If $H'<T_{i+1}$, then $\mathcal J_E=[0,H')$ and a new preterminal slab
starts at $H$.  If $H'=T_{i+1}$, the returned flow is not truncated merely
because it reaches the epoch boundary; should its domain end there, the slab
starting at $H$ is still recorded.  The extension retains the old prefix
controls, is admissible and pinched, carries the selected canonical and
noncollapsing predicates on $[0,H')$, and keeps the standard cap model tied to
the actual observation.  The terminal policy for the new event is supplied on
the singleton $\{H\}$ and is combined with the old policy; no policy for
unobserved future events is inferred.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Identify the retained last ordinary slab with its generalized spacetime.
Theorem~\ref{thm:singular-m31} supplies the regular limit and
Corollary~\ref{cor:singular-selector} its deep-horn scale. The explicit
reference equality identifies the limit metric and comparison maps on
the old carrier. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:metric-surgery} with the actual
constants and standard cap, then Theorem~\ref{thm:branch-continuation}
to construct the event and its maximal ordinary restart.
The empty-core alternative is Corollary~\ref{prop:unified-branch}; on a
nonempty restarted carrier, Theorem~\ref{thm:rf-pinching} preserves
Hamilton--Ivey pinching from the terminal metric.

Observe that restart up to its endpoint or the epoch boundary, retaining
the preterminal slab. Parameter equality transports the old profile;
canonical induction and then noncollapsing apply to this same branch.
The auxiliary scalar derivative estimates hold on regular history and
make no assertion through a surgery jump.
\end{proof}

\section{Volume loss at the corrected scale}
\label{sec:global-volume-loss}

\begin{theorem}[Surgery volume loss]
\label{thm:global-volume-loss}
\lean{PoincareMT.repairedVolumeLoss}
\uses{thm:global-epoch,thm:metric-surgery}
For a controlled flow with the terminal event policy, volume has finite
left limits at surgery times and cannot increase at an event. For sufficiently
small cutoff, each cap removes volume at least $c h^3/\delta$, with one
positive uniform $c$. A zero-cap event instead discards a nonempty component.
The loss and the retained-volume identities concern the actual incoming
and outgoing metrics of the same event.
\source{MT2007}\dcref{Section 15.4 and Section 17.2}
\end{theorem}

Write $v_F(t)=\operatorname{Vol}_{g(t)}(M_t)$. At a nonempty event $T$,
let $A_T^\pm$ be its retained regions, $g_T^*$ its regular-limit metric,
$q_T$ its incoming identification, and $R_{T,i}$ its positive half-necks.
The certificate provides a loss $\ell_T\geq0$ and finite $L_T$ with

\begin{align}
 v_F(t)&\longrightarrow L_T
   &&(t\nearrow T),\\
 \operatorname{Vol}_{g_T^*}(M_T^*)&\leq L_T,\\
 \operatorname{Vol}_{g(T)}(A_T^+)&=
   \operatorname{Vol}_{g_T^*}(q_T(A_T^-)),\\
 v_F(T)+\ell_T&\leq \operatorname{Vol}_{g_T^*}(M_T^*).
 \label{eq:global-event-volume}
\end{align}

The event alternative is the geometric source of positivity.  Either some
cap index $i$ satisfies $\ell_T>0$ and

\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Vol}_{g(T)}(C_{T,i})+\ell_T
 \leq \operatorname{Vol}_{g_T^*}(R_{T,i}),
 \label{eq:global-neck-loss}
\end{equation}

or the event has zero caps, $\ell_T=0$, and a connected component outside
$A_T^-$ has strictly positive incoming volume.  Vanishing events have empty
post-volume and are bounded by their supplied left-limit volume.  The
zero-cap alternative is deliberately not forced into a positive loss estimate.

The corrected estimate is cubic:

\begin{equation}
 \ell_T\geq c\,\frac{h(T)^3}{\delta(T)}
 \qquad\text{when the event has at least one cap}.
 \label{eq:global-cubic-loss}
\end{equation}

For a proof, the positive axial neck interval has length $\delta(T)^{-1}$.
Scaling three-dimensional volume gives
\[
 \operatorname{Vol}_{g_T^*}(R_{T,i})
 \geq c_n h(T)^3/\delta(T),\qquad
 \operatorname{Vol}_{g(T)}(C_{T,i})\leq C_{\rm cap}h(T)^3.
\]
For the two applicability thresholds, choose
\[
 d=\min\{d_{\rm cap}(K),d_{\rm neck},c_n/(2C_{\rm cap})\}>0,
 \qquad 0<\overline\delta\leq d,
\]
and require $\delta(T)\leq\overline\delta$. Subtracting the cap bound leaves
$c h(T)^3/\delta(T)$ with $c=c_n/2$ for every cap.
The positive half-necks are disjoint from one another and the retained
region. Summing over all $n_T$ caps and using retained-volume equality gives
\begin{equation}
 v_F(T)+n_T c\,h(T)^3/\delta(T)
 \leq\operatorname{Vol}_{g_T^*}(M_T^*)\leq L_T.
 \label{eq:global-all-cap-loss}
\end{equation}
The determinant evolution $\partial_t d\mu=-R\,d\mu$ and the scalar lower
bound $R\geq-6$ give, on a regular slab,

\begin{equation}
 v_F(b)\leq e^{6(b-a)}v_F(a),\qquad a\leq b,
 \label{eq:global-volume-growth}
\end{equation}

and the left-limit comparison concatenates this inequality across events.
Thus $e^{-6t}v_F(t)$ is nonincreasing and pays the weighted sum of all
per-cap losses.  The scale $h^3/\delta$ follows from actual chart volume;
the quadratic expression printed in \cite[Lemma 17.12, pp.~410--411]{MT2007}
is corrected here.

\section{Finite horizons and no finite accumulation}
\label{sec:global-finiteness}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform finite event bound]
\label{thm:global-finiteness}
\lean{PoincareMT.m50ObservedFiniteness_from_M49,PoincareMT.repairedFinitePrefix}
\uses{thm:global-volume-loss}
Fix a finite time horizon, an initial volume bound, a positive lower surgery
height and an upper cutoff. Under the controlled-flow and terminal-policy
hypotheses, one integer bounds every finite set of observed surgery events.
Consequently surgery times cannot accumulate at a finite time.
\source{MT2007}\dcref{Lemma 17.12 and Section 17.2, pp. 408--411}
\end{theorem}

Fix $B>0$, an initial volume bound $V_0<\infty$, and a lower height
$h_{\min}>0$.  On an observed interval contained in $[0,B]$, suppose the
pinching, terminal-strong-neck, disappearing-boundary, and zero-cap controls
hold, while every event satisfies $\delta(T)\leq\bar\delta$ and
$h_{\min}\leq h(T)$.  Set $\sigma=c h_{\min}^3/\bar\delta>0$.
The multiplicity estimate \eqref{eq:global-all-cap-loss} gives, for every
finite set $S$ of observed event times,
\[
 e^{-6B}\sigma\sum_{T\in S}n_T
 \leq\sum_{T\in S} e^{-6T}\sigma n_T\leq V_0.
\]
Choose an integer $N_c\geq e^{6B}V_0/\sigma$ before choosing the flow.
It bounds total cap multiplicity, not just the number of times with a cap.

Let $c(t)$ count components and $d_T=1$ at a zero-cap or vanishing event,
$d_T=0$ otherwise. Cutting along $n_T$ spheres adds at most $n_T$ components;
capping adds none, and an event with $d_T=1$ removes a component. Thus
\[
 c(T)+d_T\leq c(T^-)+n_T.
\]
Sum over the complete event prefix to an included time, finite by raw-flow
local finiteness. Ordinary identifications cancel, so
$\sum d_T\leq c(0)+\sum n_T$ and the event count is at most
$c(0)+2\sum n_T$. A requested finite subset must first be enlarged to
this complete prefix; the resulting uniform bound also controls approach
to an excluded horizon.

Each normalized initial component has a unit ball of volume at least
$\omega_3/2$, hence $c(0)\leq2V_0/\omega_3$. Choosing
$N_0\geq2V_0/\omega_3$ gives the uniform bound $N=N_0+2N_c$,
including deletion-only events.

Fix an epoch and its uniform event bound $N$. Each unsuccessful restart
adds the old horizon as a fresh observed surgery time, with no events in
the intervening open gap. After $N+1$ unsuccessful restarts the same
extended flow would contain $N+1$ distinct observed events. Thus a restart
reaches the epoch boundary, retaining the terminal slab there. Extension
preserves initial volume and parameters; the height at the right boundary
is a common positive lower height for all these stages.

For any compact time set, the bound on finite subsets forces the event set
itself to be finite. Hence the flow carrying the loss certificate has no
finite accumulation of surgery times, including at an excluded horizon.

\section{Compatible finite prefixes and the global assembly}
\label{sec:global-assembly}

A finite stage carries its flow, observation, parameter prefix and exact
comparison maps. Epoch extension and finite restart give the next stage,
preserving metric pullbacks, event references, retained regions and numerical
controls. Comparisons agree on passing to a common later stage. Since the
completed epoch horizons tend to infinity, their compatible union gives a
flow $G$ on $[0,\infty)$ and retains a finite-prefix witness at every index.

Applied to a supplied controlled $F$ on $[0,T)$ with the chosen
$\epsilon,C,\delta$, this constructs an extension of that same flow.
Its earlier events and carrier identifications persist, together with
the terminal policy and schedule agreement.

For the normalized branch, let $g_0$ be a normalized initial metric on a compact
nonempty three-manifold $M$ with no embedded projective plane of trivial normal
bundle.  Let $\delta$ be antitone on $[0,\infty)$, positive there, and satisfy

\begin{equation}
 \delta(t)\leq\Delta(j)\quad(t\in E_j).
 \label{eq:global-control-cutoff}
\end{equation}

The normalized initial-flow estimate supplies the first stage, including
the time-zero identification. The
completed chain produces a flow $G$ for the already selected schedule $S$, with

\[
 \mathcal J_G=[0,\infty),\qquad
 \delta_G=\delta,\qquad \epsilon_G=\epsilon,
 \quad C_G=C.
\]

There is a diffeomorphism

\[
 \iota:M\longrightarrow M_0^G
\]

such that for all $x,v,w$

\begin{equation}
 g_G(0)_{\iota(x)}(d\iota_xv,d\iota_xw)=g_0(x)(v,w).
 \label{eq:global-initial-identification}
\end{equation}

Apply the volume argument to this same flow. Its event data yield the
left-limit comparisons, cubic loss, component-event bound and local
finiteness. Once a slice becomes empty, every later slice stays empty and
no later surgery occurs.

We have proved the following global existence statement, with the same
schedule and control function throughout:

\begin{theorem}[Global controlled surgery flow]
\label{thm:global-controlled-flow}
\lean{PoincareMT.repairedGlobalSchedule,PoincareMT.repairedGlobalFlow}
\uses{thm:global-setup,thm:global-epoch,thm:global-finiteness,thm:global-volume-loss}
for every $\varepsilon_{\rm bound}>0$ there are $K$ and a global schedule
$S$ with $2\epsilon\leq\varepsilon_{\rm bound}$; every admissible
finite controlled prefix satisfying the schedule extends on its own flow;
and every normalized initial metric with the projective-plane exclusion and every
positive antitone $\delta$ satisfying \eqref{eq:global-control-cutoff}
produces a complete global certificate identified with $S$ and $\delta$.
\source{MT2007}\dcref{Theorem 15.9 and Corollary 15.10, pp. 363--366}
\end{theorem}

The resulting flow has canonical neighborhoods, noncollapsing,
pinching, admissibility, compact volume bounds, event-loss data, local
finiteness, and empty-slice permanence.  The source quantity is exactly
$\rho=\delta r$ in the height selector. Extinction will be proved using
the width comparison.

\section{Choosing the flow for the width comparisons}
\label{sec:global-comparison-calibration}

The global existence theorem permits a choice of accuracy bound and of
positive antitone cutoff.  We now make those choices before constructing
the flow, so that its own local topology and surgery comparison maps
satisfy the hypotheses of the extinction argument.

Let $\varepsilon_{\rm top},\varepsilon_{\rm map},
\varepsilon_{\rm hom}>0$ be the thresholds supplied respectively by
local reconstruction, the comparison map and its homotopy theorem.  Put
$e_* =\min\{\varepsilon_{\rm top},\varepsilon_{\rm map},
\varepsilon_{\rm hom}\}$.  With the fixed accuracy factor
$A=10^{14}$, request the global schedule using the bound $2e_*/A$.
The global theorem returns $2\varepsilon\leq2e_*/A$, hence
$A\varepsilon\leq e_*$.  The constants and schedule are chosen before
the initial manifold.  The factor $A$ is the actual uniform loss retained
by the construction; replacing it by $2$ would weaken the hypotheses of
the subsequent local theorems.

Write $\delta_0,R_0>0$ for the metric-surgery cutoffs, and $\Delta(j)$
for the positive antitone schedule, with $\Delta(j)\leq\delta_0$.
Define, before selecting the flow,
\begin{equation}
 \delta_*(t)=\min\left\{
   \frac{\Delta(\lceil32t\rceil_+)}2,
   1,\frac{R_0^{-1/2}}2\right\},
 \label{eq:global-strict-control}
\end{equation}
where $\lceil s\rceil_+$ is the least nonnegative integer at least $s$.
This function is positive and antitone: the ceiling index is nondecreasing,
$\Delta$ is antitone, and taking a minimum preserves this order.
If $t$ belongs to epoch $E_j$, then $j\leq\lceil32t\rceil_+$.
For $j=0$ this is automatic; for $j=n+1$, the lower epoch bound gives
$2^n\leq32t$, and $n+1\leq2^n$.  Therefore
$\delta_*(t)\leq\Delta(j)/2\leq\Delta(j)$, as required for the
normalized global-flow construction.

Apply that construction with this exact control.  The resulting flow has
$\delta(t)=\delta_*(t)$ on its nonnegative domain, so
\[
 \delta(t)\leq\frac{\Delta(0)}2<\Delta(0),\qquad
 \delta(t)\leq\frac{\delta_0}2,\qquad
 \delta(t)\leq1,\qquad
 \delta(t)\leq\frac{R_0^{-1/2}}2.
\]
Its height bound and $r(t)\leq1$ give
\[
 h(t)\leq\delta(t)^2r(t)\leq\delta(t),
 \qquad h(t)\leq\frac{R_0^{-1/2}}2<R_0^{-1/2}.
\]
In particular $\delta(t)<\delta_0$ and $h(t)<R_0^{-1/2}$ hold at
every surgery time, with explicit margins.

The bounds $A\varepsilon\leq e_*$ and the strict cutoff margins let local
reconstruction, comparison-map and homotopy theorems apply to this same
flow and its actual event maps. These choices are made before the flow,
independently of the later target time.

\section{The flow used in extinction}
\label{sec:global-interfaces}

The extinction argument uses this single flow $G$, its exact event maps,
schedule agreement and permanent-empty property. The width comparison
will supply the terminal empty slice.

Noncollapsing used the stable minimizing region; canonical induction used
the ancient alternatives and backward limits; continuation used the
selected singular region and cap model. Finite restart and compatible
union preserve these choices for arbitrarily large times.
