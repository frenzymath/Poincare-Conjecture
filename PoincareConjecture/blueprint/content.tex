\chapter*{The Poincare Conjecture}

Every closed, simply connected topological three-manifold is homeomorphic
to the three-sphere. This blueprint explains the completed Lean formalization
of that theorem and its smooth counterpart in the
\href{https://github.com/frenzymath/Poincare-Conjecture/tree/main/PoincareConjecture/PoincareLib}{PoincareLib proof library}.
The smooth theorem is reached in Chapter 14; the topological theorem, named
the Poincare conjecture, closes Chapter 18.

\section*{From Lean to mathematics}

The exposition was developed with Archon Horizon from the implemented Lean
definitions, statements, and proof arguments. It selects the mathematical
constructions and intermediate results needed to explain the public theorems,
condensing low-level algebra, transport, and bookkeeping. Its organization
follows the proof library rather than a single textbook's chapter order.
Lean annotations identify the corresponding declarations; prerequisite links
record the mathematical inputs to a statement or its proof. A definition or
foundational result can begin a dependency chain without prerequisites.

Each named node states a definition or result, with its hypotheses visible.
Proofs explain how the preceding constructions are used. Source references

on this site provide background reading; the Lean library is the authoritative
record of the formal argument.

\section*{The proof in eighteen chapters}

Chapters 1--3 establish three-manifold foundations, Ricci flow, reduced
geometry, and noncollapsing. Chapters 4--6 study ancient solutions,
neck-and-cap topology, and singular limits. These identify the local models
needed for the surgery construction and its global continuation in
Chapters 7--8.

Chapters 9--12 develop filling width, curve-shortening ramps, and annular
comparison. Their estimates force finite extinction. Chapters 13--14 then
read a finite surgery history backward, reconstruct the original
connected-sum topology, and use simple connectedness to obtain the smooth
three-sphere conclusion.

Chapters 15--18 supply the passage from topological to smooth manifolds:
protected Dehn surfaces, compact cores, relative rigidity, and the
Hamilton--Cairns compatible smoothing construction. The final step transports
the topological hypotheses through the chosen homeomorphism and applies the
smooth theorem.

\section*{Notation}

Throughout the Ricci-flow chapters, $M$ is a three-manifold, $g(t)$ its
time-dependent metric, $\operatorname{Ric}$ its Ricci tensor, and $R$ its
scalar curvature. The flow equation is
$\partial_t g=-2\operatorname{Ric}$.
$B_{g(t)}(x,r)$ denotes a metric ball at time $t$; a parabolic neighborhood
also specifies a time interval. The positive parameters $\kappa$ and
$\varepsilon$ denote noncollapsing and geometric approximation scales;
their precise hypotheses are stated where they are used.

The sphere $S^3$ is the unit sphere in $\mathbb R^4$.
The notation $M\cong N$ denotes a homeomorphism unless a smooth statement
explicitly requires a diffeomorphism. The symbol $\#$ denotes connected sum,
and $\pi_1(M)$ is the fundamental group. A closed manifold is compact and
without boundary. Topological manifolds are Hausdorff and second-countable;
the terminal theorem includes simple connectedness, hence connectedness and
nonemptiness. Local notation and parameter choices are fixed in each chapter.
