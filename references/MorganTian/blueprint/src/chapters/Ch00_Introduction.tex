\begin{theorem}[Classification of 3-manifolds with free-product fundamental group]
\label{thm:classification-fundamental-group-free-product}
\label{Theorem1}
Let $M$ be a closed, connected $3$-manifold whose fundamental group is a
free product of finite groups and infinite cyclic groups. Then $M$ is
diffeomorphic to a connected sum of spherical space-forms, copies of
$S^2\times S^1$, and copies of the non-orientable $2$-sphere bundle over
$S^1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Poincare Conjecture and spherical space-form conjecture]
\label{cor:poincare-and-spherical-space-form}
\uses{thm:classification-fundamental-group-free-product}

Every closed simply connected $3$-manifold is diffeomorphic to $S^3$, and
every closed $3$-manifold with finite fundamental group is diffeomorphic to a
$3$-dimensional spherical space-form.
\end{corollary}

\section{Ricci flow with surgery and extinction}

A Ricci flow is a family of Riemannian metrics satisfying
\[
  \frac{\partial g(t)}{\partial t}=-2\Ric(g(t)).
\]
A Ricci flow with surgery agrees with an ordinary Ricci flow away from a
discrete set of times. At a surgery time, necks are cut along $2$-spheres,
standard caps are inserted, and specified connected components may be
discarded.

\begin{theorem}[Long-time existence of Ricci flow with surgery]
\label{thm:ricci-flow-surgery-existence}
\label{surgery}
Let $(M,g_0)$ be a closed Riemannian $3$-manifold containing no embedded,
locally separating $\Ar P^2$, equivalently no embedded $\Ar P^2$ with trivial
normal bundle. Then there is a Ricci flow with surgery on $[0,\infty)$ with
initial metric $(M,g_0)$ and a discrete set of surgery times. Across a surgery
time the topology changes by connected-sum decomposition and deletion of
components diffeomorphic to one of
\[
  S^2\times S^1,\qquad \Ar P^3\#\Ar P^3,\qquad
  \text{the non-orientable $S^2$-bundle over $S^1$},
\]
or a manifold admitting a metric of constant positive curvature.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-time extinction of Ricci flow with surgery]
\label{thm:ricci-flow-surgery-finite-extinction}
\uses{thm:ricci-flow-surgery-existence}

\label{finiteext}
Let $M$ be a closed $3$-manifold whose fundamental group is a free product of
finite groups and infinite cyclic groups. Then $M$ contains no locally
separating $\Ar P^2$. For every Riemannian metric $g_0$ on $M$, the flow with
surgery supplied by Theorem~\ref{surgery} becomes extinct: there is $T<\infty$
such that $M_t=\emptyset$ for all $t\geq T$.
\end{theorem}

The two preceding theorems yield a finite sequence
$M=M_0,M_1,\ldots,M_k=\emptyset$ in which each step is a connected-sum
decomposition or deletion of one of the listed standard components. Downward
induction gives Theorem~\ref{Theorem1}.

\begin{corollary}[Equivalent conditions for finite-time extinction]
\label{cor:extinction-equivalence}
\label{corequiv}
Let $(M_0,g_0)$ be a connected Riemannian $3$-manifold with no locally
separating $\Ar P^2$, and let $({\mathcal M},G)$ be a Ricci flow with surgery
for $0\leq t<\infty$ with initial manifold $(M_0,g_0)$. The following are
equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(1)] $M_T=\emptyset$ for every sufficiently large $T$;
\item[(2)] $M_0$ is diffeomorphic to a connected sum of $3$-dimensional
spherical space-forms and $S^2$-bundles over $S^1$;
\item[(3)] $\pi_1(M_0)$ is a free product of finite groups and infinite cyclic
groups;
\item[(4)] every prime factor $P$ of $M_0$ has nontrivial $\pi_2(P)$ or
nontrivial $\pi_3(P)$.
\end{enumerate}
Here a closed $3$-manifold is prime if every separating $2$-sphere bounds a
$3$-ball.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\sketch
\uses{thm:ricci-flow-surgery-existence,thm:ricci-flow-surgery-finite-extinction}
Repeated surgery gives $(1)\Rightarrow(2)$, van Kampen gives
$(2)\Rightarrow(3)$, and Theorem~\ref{finiteext} gives
$(3)\Rightarrow(1)$. The summands in (2) are prime, so
$(2)\Rightarrow(4)$. For the converse, let $P$ be a prime factor. If
$\pi_2(P)=0$, then the universal cover $\widetilde P$ has nontrivial
$\pi_3$; Hurewicz gives $H_3(\widetilde P)\neq0$, so $\widetilde P$ is compact
and $\pi_1(P)$ is finite. If $\pi_2(P)\neq0$, the sphere theorem and the
absence of a locally separating $\Ar P^2$ give a homotopically nontrivial
embedded $2$-sphere. Primality makes it nonseparating; cutting along it gives
$S^2\times I$, hence $P$ is an $S^2$-bundle over $S^1$. The prime
decomposition and van Kampen now give (3).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Consequences of finite-time extinction]
\label{rem:extinction-remarks}
\uses{thm:classification-fundamental-group-free-product, cor:extinction-equivalence}

The extinction argument applies whenever every prime factor has nontrivial
$\pi_2$ or $\pi_3$, without using the sphere theorem. If the initial manifold

\section{Riemannian-geometric conventions}

For a complete Riemannian $n$-manifold, the metric is
$\kappa$-non-collapsed at $x$ if every $r>0$ satisfying
$|\Rm|\leq r^{-2}$ on $B(x,r)$ also satisfies
\[
  \Vol B(x,r)\geq \kappa r^n.
\]
Under the same curvature bound, this volume lower bound gives an injectivity
radius lower bound depending only on $r$ and $\kappa$. If
$\Ric\geq(n-1)K$, Bishop--Gromov comparison says that
\[
  r\longmapsto
  \frac{\Vol B(x,r)}{\Vol B_K(r)}
\]
is nonincreasing and tends to $1$ as $r\to0$.

For a complete manifold of nonnegative sectional curvature, the soul theorem
identifies the manifold with the total space of the normal bundle of a compact
totally geodesic soul. If the sectional curvature is positive and the manifold
is noncompact, the soul is a point and the manifold is diffeomorphic to
$\Ar^n$; positive distance levels from the soul are $(n-1)$-spheres, and the
preimage of a nondegenerate interval in $(0,\infty)$ is
$S^{n-1}\times I$. The splitting theorem states that the existence of a
globally minimizing geodesic line forces an isometric splitting
$N^{n-1}\times\Ar$. In particular, two ends imply such a splitting with $N$
compact.

Fix sufficiently small $\epsilon>0$. An $\epsilon$-neck centered at $x$ is an
image of
\[
  \psi:S^2\times(-\epsilon^{-1},\epsilon^{-1})\longrightarrow M
\]
with $x\in\psi(S^2\times\{0\})$ such that
$\psi^*(R(x)g)$ is $\epsilon$-close in $C^{[1/\epsilon]}$ to the product of
the round $S^2$ of scalar curvature $1$ and the Euclidean interval. An
$\epsilon$-cap is a noncompact submanifold whose end is covered by
$\epsilon$-necks and whose core is a $3$-ball or a punctured $\Ar P^3$. It is
a $(C,\epsilon)$-cap if, after normalization to scalar curvature $1$, its
diameter, curvature ratios, and volume are bounded by $C$. An
$\epsilon$-tube is a copy of $S^2\times(0,1)$ covered by centered
$\epsilon$-necks.

A $C$-component is a compact positively curved component diffeomorphic to
$S^3$ or $\Ar P^3$ such that normalization by $R(x)$ gives diameter at most
$C$, sectional curvatures in $[C^{-1},C]$, and volume in $[C^{-1},C]$. An
$\epsilon$-round component becomes $\epsilon$-close in
$C^{[1/\epsilon]}$ to a round scalar-curvature-one metric after the same
normalization. For sufficiently small $\epsilon$, a connected manifold
covered by $\epsilon$-tubes and $\epsilon$-caps is diffeomorphic to one of
\[
\Ar^3,\quad S^2\times\Ar,\quad S^3,\quad S^2\times S^1,\quad
\Ar P^3\#\Ar P^3,
\]
the total space of a line bundle over $\Ar P^2$, or the non-orientable
$S^2$-bundle over $S^1$. A complete positively curved $3$-manifold has no
$\epsilon$-necks of unbounded curvature. Consequently, if every point with
$R>r_0^{-2}$ has a canonical neighborhood, then the curvature is bounded.

\section{Ricci-flow background}

The scalar curvature of a Ricci flow satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{scalarflow}
  \frac{\partial R}{\partial t}=\triangle R+2|\Ric|^2,
\end{equation}
where $\triangle$ has nonpositive spectrum. Compact initial data admit a
unique short-time solution. Solutions concatenate when their metrics agree at
the joining time, are invariant under time translation, and satisfy the
parabolic rescaling rule
\[
  h(t)=Qg(Q^{-1}t),\qquad Q>0.
\]
If curvature stays uniformly bounded on $[0,T)$ with $T<\infty$, the flow
extends past $T$.

If $\Ric(g)=\lambda g$ for $\lambda>0$, then
$g(t)=(1-2\lambda t)g$ is a shrinking soliton. More generally, if
\[
  \Ric(g)=\lambda g-\Hess^g f,
\]
then pullback by the diffeomorphisms generated by
$(1-2\lambda t)^{-1}\nabla_g f$ converts the associated Ricci flow into a
homothetically shrinking family. This defines a gradient shrinking soliton.

Shi estimates imply that a curvature bound on
$B(x,t_0,r)\times[0,t_0]$ controls the $k$th covariant derivative of
curvature on $B(x,t_0,r/2^k)\times[0,t_0]$ by a constant times
$t^{-k/2}$.

A generalized $3$-dimensional Ricci flow consists of a smooth $4$-manifold
${\mathcal M}$, a time function ${\bf t}$ with connected interval image, a
vector field $\chi$ satisfying $\chi({\bf t})=1$, and a metric $G$ on the
horizontal distribution $\ker(d{\bf t})$. The time-slices are the leaves of
this distribution, the boundary consists of the endpoint time-slices, and
\[
  {\mathcal L}_\chi G=-2\Ric(G).
\]
A strong $\epsilon$-neck is a parabolically shifted and rescaled embedding of
\[
  \bigl(S^2\times(-\epsilon^{-1},\epsilon^{-1})\bigr)\times(-1,0]
\]
whose horizontal metric is $\epsilon$-close in $C^{[1/\epsilon]}$ to the
shrinking round-cylinder flow.

\subsection{Maximum-principle consequences}
\label{sectmax}

Equation~\eqref{scalarflow} implies that $R_{\min}(t)$ is nondecreasing. If
$R_{\min}(0)>0$ on an $n$-manifold, then
\[
  R_{\min}(t)\geq
  \frac{R_{\min}(0)}{1-2tR_{\min}(0)/n},
\]
so a singularity occurs no later than $n/(2R_{\min}(0))$. In dimension $3$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item positive Ricci curvature produces a finite-time singularity whose
normalized metrics become round;
\item at points where scalar curvature tends to $+\infty$, negative curvature
eigenvalues are negligible relative to the largest positive eigenvalue;
\item for a complete bounded nonnegatively curved flow on $[T_0,T_1]$,
\[
  \partial_tR(x,t)+\frac{R(x,t)}{t-T_0}\geq0;
\]
in particular, $\partial_tR\geq0$ for an ancient such flow;
\item if a complete nonflat nonnegatively curved $3$-flow develops a flat
curvature-operator direction at positive time, a cover splits for every later
time as the product of a positively curved surface flow and the static line;
\item no positive-time slice of a nonnegatively curved Ricci flow is isometric
to an open subset of a nonflat $3$-dimensional metric cone.
\end{enumerate}

A sequence of based complete Riemannian manifolds
$(M_n,g_n,x_n)$ converges geometrically to
$(M_\infty,g_\infty,x_\infty)$ if an exhaustion by relatively compact open
sets $U_n\subset M_\infty$ admits basepoint-preserving embeddings
$\varphi_n:U_n\to M_n$ for which $\varphi_n^*g_n\to g_\infty$ smoothly on
compact subsets. The analogous definition applies to Ricci flows.

\begin{theorem}[Compactness theorem for based Ricci flows (Hamilton)]
\label{thm:compactness-based-ricci-flows}
Let $(M_n,g_n(t),(x_n,0))$, $t\in(-T,0]$, be complete based Ricci
flows. Suppose:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(1)] there are $r,\kappa>0$ such that
$B_{g_n(0)}(x_n,r)$ is $\kappa$-non-collapsed for every $n$;
\item[(2)] for every $A<\infty$ there is $C(A)<\infty$ such that
$|\Rm|\leq C(A)$ on $B_{g_n(0)}(x_n,A)\times(-T,0]$ for every $n$.
\end{enumerate}
Then a subsequence converges geometrically to a based Ricci flow
$(M_\infty,g_\infty(t),(x_\infty,0))$ on $(-T,0]$.
\end{theorem}

\section{Reduced geometry and kappa-solutions}

Fix $(x,t)$ in a Ricci flow and let a backward-time path satisfy
$\gamma(0)=x$ and $\gamma(\tau)\in M\times\{t-\tau\}$ for
$0\leq\tau\leq\bar\tau$. Define
\[
  {\mathcal L}(\gamma)=
  \int_0^{\bar\tau}\sqrt\tau
  \bigl(R(\gamma(\tau))+|\gamma'(\tau)|^2\bigr)\,d\tau,
  \qquad
  \ell(\gamma)=\frac{{\mathcal L}(\gamma)}{2\sqrt{\bar\tau}}.
\]
Minimizers are ${\mathcal L}$-geodesics. For $\bar t=t-\bar\tau$, the reduced
volume of $U\subset M_{\bar t}$ is
\[
  \widetilde V_{(x,t)}(U\times\{\bar t\})
  =\int_U \bar\tau^{-3/2}e^{-\ell_{(x,t)}(q,\bar\tau)}
  \,d\vol_{g(\bar t)}(q).
\]
It is nonincreasing in backward time along open sets transported by minimizing
${\mathcal L}$-geodesics. In a generalized flow these quantities are defined
on the points reachable by such minimizers, and the same local monotonicity
holds.

For parabolic noncollapsing, one assumes
$|\Rm|\leq r^{-2}$ on
\[
  P(x,t,r,-r^2)=B_{g(t)}(x,r)\times(t-r^2,t]
\]
and concludes $\Vol_{g(t)}B(x,r)\geq\kappa r^3$. Reduced-volume monotonicity
gives this conclusion for compact Ricci flows and complete nonnegatively
curved flows, with $\kappa$ depending only on the initial geometry.

A $\kappa$-solution is a Ricci flow on $(-\infty,0]$ whose time-slices are
nonflat complete $3$-manifolds of bounded nonnegative curvature and are
$\kappa$-non-collapsed. For a basepoint $(x,0)$ and $t<0$,
\[
  \min_y\ell_{(x,0)}(y,t)\leq\frac32.
\]
If $t_n\to-\infty$ and
$\ell_{(x,0)}(y_n,t_n)\leq3/2$, a subsequence of
$(M,|t_n|^{-1}g(t_n),y_n)$ converges geometrically to a gradient shrinking
soliton. Such an asymptotic soliton is finitely covered by shrinking round
$3$-spheres or by shrinking round cylinders $S^2\times\Ar$.

\begin{theorem}[Compactness of the space of based kappa-solutions]
\label{thm:compactness-kappa-solutions}
For fixed $\kappa>0$, the space of based $\kappa$-solutions
$(M,g(t),(x,0))$ normalized by $R(x,0)=1$ is compact.
\end{theorem}

A $\kappa$-solution without strictly positive curvature is a shrinking round
cylinder or its involution quotient. A noncompact positively curved
$\kappa$-solution is diffeomorphic to $\Ar^3$ and consists of an
$\epsilon$-tube and the core of a $(C_1,\epsilon)$-cap. A compact positively
curved solution is a round shrinking space-form or is diffeomorphic to
$S^3$ or $\Ar P^3$ and, after scalar-curvature normalization, either has
uniformly bounded geometry or contains an $\epsilon$-tube whose complement is
the union of two cap cores.

For every $A<\infty$, bounded curvature at bounded distance supplies
$Q_0(A),Q(A)<\infty$ such that, under the required generalized-flow
hypotheses,
\[
  R(x)\geq Q_0(A),\quad
  d(x,y)<A R(x)^{-1/2}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \frac{R(y)}{R(x)}<Q(A).
\]

\section{The standard solution and surgery construction}

The standard metric is a fixed rotationally symmetric nonnegatively curved
metric on $\Ar^3$ which is cylindrical near infinity and strictly positively
curved elsewhere. Its unique bounded-curvature Ricci flow exists on $[0,1)$,
is asymptotic on compact time intervals to the evolving round cylinder outside
a compact set, and is noncollapsed. Every point of this standard solution lies
in a $(C_2,\epsilon)$-cap core, is the center of a strong $\epsilon$-neck, or
is the center of an evolving $\epsilon$-neck issuing from time zero.

A Ricci-flow-with-surgery spacetime is a Hausdorff $4$-dimensional singular
space with time function ${\bf t}$, compact smooth $3$-dimensional time-slices,
and a vector field $\chi$ satisfying $\chi({\bf t})=1$. Its nonsingular open
part carries a horizontal metric $G$ making
$({\bf t},\chi,G)$ a generalized Ricci flow. Singular points are boundaries of
exposed regions at the discrete surgery times.

Let $C=\max(C_1,C_2)$. A point has a $(C,\epsilon)$-canonical neighborhood if
it lies in a $C$-component, lies in an $\epsilon$-round component, lies in the
core of a $(C,\epsilon)$-cap, or is the center of a strong $\epsilon$-neck.
The inductive surgery construction maintains:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the initial metric is normalized by $|\Rm|\leq1$ and every unit ball has
at least half the Euclidean unit-ball volume;
\item curvature is pinched toward positive;
\item the generalized flow is $\kappa$-non-collapsed on scales at most
$\epsilon$;
\item every point with $R\geq r_0^{-2}$ has a
$(C,\epsilon)$-canonical neighborhood.
\end{enumerate}
The constants $\kappa,r_0$ may decrease during extension but remain positive
on each compact time interval.

At a singular time $T$, let $\Omega$ be the region where curvature remains
bounded and let $\Omega_\rho\subset\Omega$ be the part with
$R\leq\rho^{-2}$. Each relevant end of $\Omega$ is an $\epsilon$-horn. Given
$\delta>0$, there is $h=h(\delta,\rho,r_0)$ such that every point $x$ in such
a horn with $R(x)\geq h^{-2}$ centers a strong $\delta$-neck. Cut each horn at
a central $2$-sphere of curvature $h^{-2}$, discard the high-curvature end,
and glue in an $h^2$-rescaling of a standard cap. Components disjoint from
$\Omega_\rho$ are removed. Bending and interpolation preserve positive
curvature near the gluing region and the pinching condition.

Topologically, surgery performs finitely many $2$-sphere surgeries and deletes
components diffeomorphic to
\[
  S^3,\quad \Ar P^3,\quad \Ar P^3\#\Ar P^3,\quad S^2\times S^1,
\]
the non-orientable $S^2$-bundle over $S^1$, or a spherical space-form. Reduced
volume preserves noncollapsing across sufficiently small surgeries.
Blow-up compactness and the bounded-curvature-at-bounded-distance estimate
show that any first point violating the canonical-neighborhood condition is
modeled either on a $\kappa$-solution or on the standard solution, a
contradiction. Thus the flow extends indefinitely. On a finite time interval,
volume grows by at most a fixed exponential factor while every surgery removes
a uniformly positive volume, so surgery times cannot accumulate.

\section{Finite-time extinction mechanism}

After finitely many homotopically nontrivial $2$-sphere surgeries, surgery no
longer changes $\pi_2$. For a component with nontrivial $\pi_2$, let $W_2(t)$
be the least area of a homotopically nontrivial map $S^2\to M_t$. It is
realized by a harmonic sphere, is continuous during Ricci flow, lower
semicontinuous at surgery, and satisfies
\[
  \frac{dW_2}{dt}\leq-4\pi+\frac{3}{4t+1}W_2.
\]
No nonnegative function with these properties exists for all large time, so
eventually every component has trivial $\pi_2$.

Under the fundamental-group hypothesis of
Theorem~\ref{finiteext}, every remaining component then has finite fundamental
group and nontrivial $\pi_3$. Represent a nonzero class by
$\xi\in\pi_2(\Lambda M,*)$. For a representative
$\Gamma:S^2\to\Lambda M$, let $W(\Gamma)$ be the maximum, over
$c\in S^2$, of the least area of a disk spanning $\Gamma(c)$, and minimize
over representatives of $\xi$. This width is continuous under Ricci flow,
lower semicontinuous at surgery unless its component is deleted, and satisfies
\[
  \frac{dW(\xi)}{dt}\leq-2\pi+\frac{3}{4t+1}W(\xi).
\]
It likewise cannot remain nonnegative for all time, so every component becomes
extinct in finite time.
