\chapter*{About This Blueprint}

This project follows David Gilbarg and Neil S. Trudinger, \emph{Elliptic
Partial Differential Equations of Second Order}, second edition, Classics in
Mathematics (Springer, 2001) \cite{gilbargtrudinger2001}.

Gilbarg--Trudinger develops the elliptic theory used throughout geometric
analysis: maximum and comparison principles, potential estimates, Schauder and
Sobolev methods, weak and strong solutions, fixed-point arguments, boundary
regularity, and gradient and Hessian estimates for nonlinear equations.  In the
Poincare project these results provide analytic infrastructure for regularity
and compactness arguments; this blueprint is a reference layer, not a
standalone proof of the conjecture.

The blueprint is a concise, independently worded mathematical distillation,
not a transcription of the book.  It retains formalization-relevant
definitions, hypotheses, estimates, conclusions, dependencies, and proof
ideas while omitting historical discussion and routine exposition.  It should
be read alongside the cited edition whenever a formal proof requires the full
source argument.

Every formal entry carries a stable semantic label and a provenance marker.
The \emph{dcref} value uses the uniform form
\texttt{chN:source-location}; primed source numbers are written directly, for
example \texttt{ch16:16.65'}, without nested TeX math delimiters.

The seventeen chapters progress from the Laplace equation, maximum principles,
potential theory, functional analysis, Schauder estimates, and Sobolev spaces
through generalized and strong solutions.  They then develop nonlinear maximum
principles and continuation methods, planar elliptic theory, boundary and
interior gradient estimates, equations of mean-curvature type, and finally
fully nonlinear and Monge--Amp\`ere equations.
